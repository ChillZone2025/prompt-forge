# Industry Context: Accounting & Tax

## What This Industry Does

Public and private accounting encompasses bookkeeping, financial statement preparation, tax compliance and planning, audit and assurance services, payroll administration, and advisory work. Practitioners range from solo bookkeepers managing a handful of small business clients to Big Four audit teams handling multinational consolidations. The work is governed by codified standards (GAAP, IFRS, IRC), enforced by regulators (IRS, state revenue departments, SEC, PCAOB), and carries personal liability for preparers who sign returns or attest to financials.

The industry operates on a cyclical calendar: quarterly estimated payments, monthly closes, year-end adjustments, and annual filing deadlines (March 15 for S-Corps/partnerships, April 15 for individuals, extended deadlines at September 15/October 15). Miss a deadline, and penalties accrue automatically.

## Key Tools & Platforms

### Accounting & Bookkeeping
- **QuickBooks Online (QBO)** — Dominant cloud accounting platform for small-to-mid businesses. Bank feeds, invoicing, payroll, sales tax, class/location tracking. Market share ~80% for sub-$10M revenue companies.
- **QuickBooks Desktop (QBD)** — Legacy desktop version preferred by some accountants for manufacturing, inventory, and job costing features that QBO lacks.
- **Xero** — Cloud-native alternative to QBO. Stronger in multi-currency, unlimited users, bank reconciliation UX. Popular in advisory-focused firms.
- **FreshBooks** — Invoice-first platform for freelancers and micro-businesses. Time tracking, estimates, client portals.
- **Sage Intacct** — Mid-market cloud ERP. Multi-entity consolidation, dimensional reporting, revenue recognition (ASC 606). AICPA's preferred provider.
- **NetSuite (Oracle)** — Full ERP for mid-market to enterprise. GL, AP/AR, inventory, revenue recognition, multi-subsidiary consolidation.

### Accounts Payable & Expense Management
- **Bill.com (BILL)** — AP/AR automation. Invoice capture, approval workflows, ACH/check payments. Integrates with QBO, Xero, NetSuite.
- **Dext (formerly Receipt Bank)** — Receipt and invoice data extraction. Auto-categorization, accountant dashboard for multi-client management.
- **Expensify** — Expense reporting, receipt scanning, corporate card management, per diem tracking.

### Payroll
- **Gusto** — Payroll, benefits, HR for small businesses. Auto-files payroll taxes, generates W-2s/1099s.
- **ADP** — Enterprise payroll and HCM. Multi-state, multi-entity payroll processing. ADP Run for small business, Workforce Now for mid-market.
- **Paychex** — Payroll processing, tax filing, time and attendance. Strong in the 20-500 employee segment.

### Tax Preparation & Compliance
- **TurboTax / ProConnect Tax Online** — Intuit's consumer and professional tax prep. ProConnect is the cloud-based professional version.
- **Drake Software** — Professional tax prep for 1040, 1120, 1120-S, 1065, 1041, 990. Value priced, popular with small firms.
- **Lacerte** — Intuit's professional desktop tax software. Handles complex returns (multi-state, partnerships, estates).
- **UltraTax CS** — Thomson Reuters professional tax software. Deep integration with CS Professional Suite (accounting, practice management).
- **CCH Axcess Tax** — Wolters Kluwer cloud-based professional tax. Part of the CCH suite (research, workflow, document management).
- **Thomson Reuters ONESOURCE** — Enterprise tax compliance and provision (ASC 740). Transfer pricing, indirect tax, statutory reporting.

### Audit & Assurance
- **CaseWare** — Audit working papers, engagement management, financial statement preparation. Cloud and desktop versions.

### Banking & Sales Tax Integration
- **Plaid** — Bank feed connectivity. Powers the bank connections in QBO, Xero, and most fintech apps.
- **Avalara** — Sales tax calculation, filing, and remittance automation. Handles nexus determination, exemption certificates, and rate lookups across 13,000+ jurisdictions.

## Key Frameworks & Standards

### Financial Reporting
- **GAAP (US Generally Accepted Accounting Principles)** — The mandatory reporting framework for US companies. Issued by FASB (Financial Accounting Standards Board). Key ASCs: ASC 606 (revenue recognition), ASC 842 (leases), ASC 330 (inventory), ASC 350 (goodwill/intangibles), ASC 740 (income taxes).
- **IFRS (International Financial Reporting Standards)** — Global reporting framework issued by IASB. Used in 140+ countries. US public companies use GAAP; foreign private issuers may use IFRS.
- **Accrual vs. Cash Basis** — Accrual recognizes revenue when earned and expenses when incurred. Cash basis recognizes when cash changes hands. Businesses with >$29M average annual gross receipts must use accrual (IRC §448).
- **Double-Entry Bookkeeping** — Every transaction has equal debits and credits. Assets = Liabilities + Equity. Non-negotiable foundation of all accounting.
- **Chart of Accounts (COA)** — Structured list of all accounts in the general ledger. Numbering convention: 1xxx Assets, 2xxx Liabilities, 3xxx Equity, 4xxx Revenue, 5xxx COGS, 6xxx-7xxx Operating Expenses, 8xxx Other Income/Expense.

### Close & Reporting Processes
- **Month-End Close** — Recurring process: reconcile bank accounts, review AP/AR aging, post accruals/deferrals, run depreciation, review intercompany transactions, generate trial balance, produce financial statements.
- **Year-End Close** — Everything in month-end plus: finalize depreciation schedules, review inventory valuation, calculate bad debt reserves, prepare tax provision, generate audit schedules, close retained earnings.
- **Audit Trail Requirements** — Every GL entry must have a source document, date, preparer, and approval. Altered or deleted entries must be logged. Required for audit readiness and IRS examination defense.

### Internal Controls
- **COSO Framework** — Committee of Sponsoring Organizations framework for internal controls. Five components: Control Environment, Risk Assessment, Control Activities, Information & Communication, Monitoring. Standard for SOX compliance.
- **Sarbanes-Oxley Section 404** — Requires public companies to assess and report on internal controls over financial reporting. Management assessment + auditor attestation.

## Regulatory Landscape

### Federal Tax (IRS)
- **IRC Section 179** — Immediate expensing of qualifying business assets up to $1,220,000 (2024). Phase-out begins at $3,050,000 in total asset purchases.
- **IRC Section 199A (QBI Deduction)** — 20% deduction on qualified business income from pass-through entities. Phase-outs for specified service trades/businesses (SSTBs) above $191,950 single / $383,900 MFJ (2024).
- **IRC Section 280E** — Disallows deductions for businesses trafficking in controlled substances. Critical for cannabis companies that can only deduct COGS.
- **Payroll Tax Compliance** — Form 940 (annual FUTA), Form 941 (quarterly federal withholding + FICA), W-2 (annual wage reporting), 1099-NEC (non-employee compensation $600+), 1099-MISC, 1099-K.
- **Estimated Quarterly Payments** — Required when expected tax liability exceeds $1,000 (individuals) or $500 (corporations). Due April 15, June 15, September 15, January 15. Underpayment penalty under IRC §6654.
- **FBAR (FinCEN 114)** — Required when aggregate foreign financial accounts exceed $10,000 at any point during the year. Due April 15, auto-extended to October 15. Penalties: $10,000 per non-willful violation, $100,000 or 50% of balance for willful.
- **FATCA (Form 8938)** — Report foreign financial assets exceeding $50,000 (end of year) or $75,000 (any point during year) for domestic filers.
- **Statute of Limitations** — 3 years from filing for assessment. 6 years if >25% of gross income omitted. No limit for fraud or non-filing.
- **E-Filing Mandates** — Tax preparers filing 11+ returns must e-file (reduced from 250 starting 2024).
- **BOI Reporting (FinCEN)** — Beneficial Ownership Information reporting under the Corporate Transparency Act. Most LLCs and corporations must report beneficial owners to FinCEN.

### State Tax
- **State Income Tax Nexus** — Physical presence (office, employee, inventory) or economic nexus (revenue thresholds, typically $100K in sales or 200 transactions post-Wayfair).
- **Sales and Use Tax** — Destination-based in most states. Nexus rules vary. Exemption certificates must be collected and retained. Use tax applies to purchases where sales tax wasn't collected.
- **Trust Fund Recovery Penalty (TFRP)** — Personal liability imposed on "responsible persons" who willfully fail to collect/remit employment taxes (income tax withholding + employee FICA). 100% of the unpaid trust fund taxes. IRC §6672.
- **State-Specific Rules** — California: market-based sourcing for services, franchise tax minimum $800, LLC fee based on gross receipts. New York: MCTMT (Metropolitan Commuter Transportation Mobility Tax), PTET (Pass-Through Entity Tax election). Texas: franchise (margin) tax, no personal income tax.

## Essential Terminology

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **General Ledger (GL)** | Complete record of all financial transactions, organized by account |
| **Accounts Payable (AP)** | Money owed to vendors/suppliers |
| **Accounts Receivable (AR)** | Money owed by customers |
| **Journal Entry (JE)** | Record of a financial transaction with debits and credits |
| **Trial Balance (TB)** | Report listing all GL account balances; debits must equal credits |
| **Bank Reconciliation** | Process of matching GL cash balance to bank statement balance |
| **Depreciation** | Systematic allocation of an asset's cost over its useful life |
| **Amortization** | Depreciation equivalent for intangible assets |
| **Accrual** | Recognizing revenue or expense before cash changes hands |
| **Deferral** | Delaying recognition of revenue or expense until a future period |
| **COGS (Cost of Goods Sold)** | Direct costs attributable to production of goods sold |
| **Gross Margin** | Revenue minus COGS, expressed as a percentage |
| **EBITDA** | Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, and Amortization |
| **Write-Off** | Removing an uncollectible receivable or worthless asset from the books |
| **Bad Debt** | Receivable deemed uncollectible — expensed via allowance or direct write-off method |
| **Net 30** | Payment terms: full amount due within 30 days of invoice date |
| **1099-NEC** | Form reporting non-employee compensation of $600+ to the IRS |
| **W-2** | Form reporting employee wages, tips, and withholdings |
| **W-4** | Employee's Withholding Certificate — determines federal income tax withholding |
| **Self-Employment Tax** | 15.3% tax (12.4% Social Security + 2.9% Medicare) on net SE income |
| **Estimated Payments** | Quarterly tax prepayments to avoid underpayment penalties |
| **Nexus** | Sufficient connection to a state that triggers tax filing/collection obligations |
| **Use Tax** | Tax on purchases where sales tax wasn't collected (e.g., out-of-state purchases) |
| **Trust Fund Penalty** | Personal liability for failure to remit withheld employment taxes (100% of amount) |
| **Materiality** | Threshold above which a misstatement would influence a reasonable user's decisions |
| **Going Concern** | Assumption that the entity will continue operating for the foreseeable future |
| **Qualified Opinion** | Auditor's opinion that financials are fairly stated except for a specific issue |
| **Management Letter** | Auditor's communication to management about internal control deficiencies |

## Top 10 Common Mistakes

1. **Misclassifying employees as independent contractors (W-2 vs. 1099)** — IRS applies a multi-factor test (behavioral control, financial control, relationship type). Getting this wrong triggers back payroll taxes, penalties (IRC §3509), and potential state penalties. The employer owes the employee's share of FICA too.

2. **Missing quarterly estimated tax payments** — Results in underpayment penalties under IRC §6654. Penalty rate tracks federal short-term rate + 3%. For calendar year 2024: ~8%. The "safe harbor" is paying 100% of prior year's tax (110% if AGI > $150K).

3. **Failing to reconcile bank accounts monthly** — Unreconciled accounts hide errors, fraud, and timing differences. A bank reconciliation should tie GL cash to bank statement within days of month-end. Outstanding items over 90 days are red flags.

4. **Incorrect depreciation methods or useful life** — Using straight-line when MACRS accelerated is available wastes tax deductions. Assigning wrong recovery periods (5-year vs. 7-year vs. 39-year) misstates income and tax liability. Section 179 and bonus depreciation are often missed entirely.

5. **Missing state tax nexus obligations** — Post-Wayfair, economic nexus applies broadly. A company selling into 15 states may have filing obligations in all of them. Failure to collect and remit sales tax creates a growing liability plus penalties and interest.

6. **Commingling personal and business expenses** — Pierces the corporate veil for liability protection. Creates tax compliance risks. Makes audit defense nearly impossible. Every expense must have a documented business purpose.

7. **Not tracking basis in S-Corp or partnership interests** — Shareholder/partner basis limits loss deductions, determines gain/loss on disposition, and affects distribution taxability. Basis must be tracked annually — the IRS now requires it on Schedule K-1 (box 20, code AH for S-Corps).

8. **Failing to document deductions** — IRC §274 requires contemporaneous records for meals, travel, and entertainment. Vehicle use requires a mileage log (Temp. Reg. 1.274-5T). Home office requires exclusive and regular use documentation. Without records, the deduction is disallowed on audit.

9. **Missing filing deadlines** — Failure-to-file penalty: 5% per month up to 25% (IRC §6651(a)(1)). Failure-to-pay penalty: 0.5% per month up to 25%. Filing more than 60 days late: minimum penalty is the lesser of $485 or 100% of tax due. Extensions extend the filing deadline, not the payment deadline.

10. **Not retaining records for the statute of limitations period** — General rule: 3 years from filing. But 6 years if >25% of gross income omitted. Employment tax records: 4 years. Asset records: retain until statute expires for the year of disposition. Fraud: no limit. Best practice: retain everything 7 years minimum.

## Excellent vs. Dangerous Work

### What Excellent Accounting Work Looks Like
- Clean monthly reconciliations completed within 5 business days of month-end
- Financial statements that tie to the GL, tie to the tax return, and have documented adjustments for every difference
- Tax returns that maximize legitimate deductions while maintaining defensible positions (realistic chance of being sustained on the merits)
- Proactive advisory: identifying Section 179 opportunities before year-end, flagging estimated payment requirements, recommending entity structure changes when tax law changes
- Clear documentation trail: every journal entry has a memo, every adjustment has a supporting schedule, every tax position has a citation
- Client communication that explains the "why" — not just "you owe $42K" but "here's why, here's how we calculated it, and here are three things we can do before December 31 to reduce it"

### What Dangerous Accounting Work Looks Like
- Unreconciled accounts carried forward month after month — errors compound and become unfindable
- Tax positions taken without research or authority — "we've always done it this way" is not a defensible position
- Signing returns with known errors because the client pressured you — this is a Circular 230 violation and potentially criminal
- Backdating transactions to capture deductions in a prior period — this is fraud
- Failing to advise clients about compliance obligations (estimated payments, nexus, BOI reporting) — malpractice exposure
- Ignoring internal control weaknesses because "we trust the owner" — segregation of duties exists because fraud correlates with opportunity, not character

## Professional Certifications

| Certification | Issuing Body | Scope |
|--------------|-------------|-------|
| **CPA (Certified Public Accountant)** | State Boards of Accountancy | Attest services (audits, reviews, compilations), tax, advisory. Requires 150 credit hours, CPA Exam (4 parts), experience, CPE. Gold standard. |
| **EA (Enrolled Agent)** | IRS | Unlimited practice rights before the IRS. Requires SEE (3-part exam) or former IRS employee status. Specialists in tax. |
| **CMA (Certified Management Accountant)** | IMA (Institute of Management Accountants) | Management accounting, financial planning, analysis, control, decision support. Corporate finance focus. |
| **CGMA (Chartered Global Management Accountant)** | AICPA/CIMA | Global management accounting credential. Joint designation between AICPA and CIMA (UK). |
| **QuickBooks ProAdvisor** | Intuit | QBO certification. Free to obtain. Required for listing in Intuit's Find-a-ProAdvisor directory. |
| **Xero Advisor Certification** | Xero | Xero platform certification. Free. Required for Xero advisor directory listing. |
| **AFSP (Annual Filing Season Program)** | IRS | Voluntary program for non-credentialed tax return preparers. Requires 18 hours CE annually. Limited representation rights. |
| **CFE (Certified Fraud Examiner)** | ACFE (Association of Certified Fraud Examiners) | Fraud prevention, detection, and investigation. Covers financial transactions, legal elements, criminology. |
