## PLANTED ISSUES (for evaluation — agent should catch these)

### Issue 1 — Depreciation Method: Straight-Line on All Manufacturing Equipment
All assets use straight-line depreciation for both book and tax purposes. No Section 179 elections or MACRS accelerated depreciation have been claimed in any year. For the 2025 Ram ProMaster ($24,000) alone, Section 179 immediate expensing would produce a $24,000 first-year deduction vs. the $4,800 claimed under straight-line. Across the full asset base, switching qualifying assets to MACRS accelerated depreciation or claiming Section 179 on eligible purchases could generate approximately $45,000 in additional tax deductions. The owner has never been advised of these options.

### Issue 2 — Stale Outstanding Checks (90+ Days)
Three outstanding checks are over 90 days old:
- Check #4218 (09/14/2025) — $1,200.00
- Check #4234 (10/02/2025) — $4,800.00
- Check #4251 (10/28/2025) — $2,200.00
- **Total: $8,200.00**

These should be investigated. If the payees have not cashed them, the checks may need to be voided and the amounts written back to income. Most states require reporting uncashed checks as unclaimed property (escheat) after 1-3 years depending on the state. Ohio's dormancy period for payroll checks is 1 year, vendor checks is 5 years. At minimum, these should be followed up on and disclosed to the owner.

### Issue 3 — Obsolete Inventory Not Written Down
Inventory includes items with no sales activity in 18+ months:
- Specialty alloys (Inconel, Titanium): $38,500 — purchased for Project Titan, which was canceled in Q2 2024. No current customer orders use these materials.
- Specialty enclosures (Project Echo): $15,300 — last sold June 2024.
- Heat shields (legacy design): $22,200 — last sold April 2024.
- **Total potentially obsolete: $76,000** (originally stated as $62K but actual total is higher)

Under ASC 330 (Inventory), inventory must be carried at the lower of cost or net realizable value (NRV). Items with no recent sales activity and no identified future demand should be written down to estimated NRV, which for specialty items with no market may be scrap value. Failure to write down obsolete inventory overstates assets and net income.

### Issue 4 — No Bad Debt Reserve on Aged Receivables
AR aging shows $34,000 in receivables over 120 days with no allowance for doubtful accounts:
- Titan Fabrication Corp: $12,400 over 120 days (disputed, unresolved)
- Summit Mechanical: $14,200 over 120 days (phone disconnected, no contact)
- Westbrook Industries: $7,400 over 120 days (Chapter 11 filing)

The matching principle requires estimating uncollectible accounts in the period the revenue was recognized. Summit Mechanical (phone disconnected) and Westbrook Industries (bankruptcy) are likely uncollectible. Titan Fabrication's disputed balance requires investigation. Total exposure: $34,000. A reasonable allowance of $25,000-$30,000 should be established, which would reduce reported net income and total assets.

### Issue 5 — Owner's Draws Misclassified as Consulting Expense
Account 6900 "Consulting Expense" contains $78,000 in payments to the owner (Tom Reichert) described as "management consulting fees" paid to his personal LLC. This is in addition to his $145,000 officer compensation. For an S-Corporation:
- Payments to an owner-employee should be W-2 wages subject to payroll taxes, or documented shareholder distributions (Account 3300 already shows $210,000 in distributions).
- Characterizing owner draws as "consulting expense" reduces the company's reported taxable income by $78,000.
- If the IRS examines this, they will likely recharacterize it as either wages (triggering payroll taxes + penalties) or distributions (increasing S-Corp reported income and K-1 pass-through to shareholders).
- If intentional, this constitutes tax fraud. At minimum, it's a significant compliance risk.

### Issue 6 — Q3 Payroll Tax Deposit Late
The Q3 941 deposit was made on 11/03/2025 — 3 days after the 10/31/2025 due date. Under IRC §6656, late payroll tax deposits trigger penalties:
- 1-5 days late: 2% penalty
- 6-15 days late: 5% penalty
- 16+ days late: 10% penalty
- 10+ days after first IRS notice: 15% penalty

The Q3 deposit of $102,085 x 2% = $2,042 penalty, plus interest from the due date. This penalty has not been accrued in the financials. Additionally, the company should evaluate whether it meets the semi-weekly deposit threshold (>$50,000 in lookback period payroll taxes), which would require more frequent deposits and carry steeper penalties for late deposits.
