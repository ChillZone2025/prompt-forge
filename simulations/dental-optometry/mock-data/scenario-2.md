## Insurance Verification Process Audit

### Current Workflow

| Step | Timing | Method | Issue |
|------|--------|--------|-------|
| Patient schedules appointment | Day of scheduling | Patient asked "what insurance do you have?" — plan name recorded | Only plan name captured; no member ID, no group number |
| Insurance verification | **Day of visit, at check-in** | Front desk looks up benefits while patient waits | 15-20 minute delay at check-in for verification |
| Benefits communicated to patient | After exam, at checkout | Verbal summary of coverage | Patient doesn't know cost until after services rendered |
| Pre-authorization (CL, medical procedures) | Not routinely performed | Only when specifically requested by provider | Missed authorizations lead to claim denials |

### Verification Failure Impact (Q1 2026)

| Issue | Occurrences | Impact |
|-------|:----------:|-------|
| Patient rescheduled due to insurance issue discovered at check-in | **84** (15% of scheduled) | Lost exam revenue, wasted chair time |
| Claim denied — no prior authorization | 28 | $6,720 in denied claims requiring rework |
| Patient walked out — co-pay higher than expected | 16 | Lost exam + lost optical sale |
| Wrong plan billed — claim rejected | 22 | Rework time, delayed payment |
| **Total verification-related disruptions** | **150** | — |

**Note:** Insurance verification is not happening until the patient arrives for their appointment. In Q1, this caused 84 patients (15% of scheduled visits) to reschedule because their benefits weren't active, they needed a referral they didn't have, or their plan wasn't accepted. This creates a cascade: the exam slot goes unfilled (no same-day fill from waitlist because there is no waitlist), the patient may not reschedule (approximately 30% of patients asked to reschedule don't rebook), and the front desk spends 15-20 minutes per patient on day-of verification instead of managing patient flow. RevolutionEHR integrates with Eyefinity for real-time eligibility verification, which can be run 48 hours before the appointment — this feature is active in the system but the staff was never trained on how to use it.

## PLANTED ISSUES (for evaluation — agent should catch these)

1. **30% of exams billed to vision plan when medical diagnosis warrants medical billing — $245,250 annual revenue leakage.** The chart audit reveals 60 of 200 sampled encounters had documented medical diagnoses (diabetes, glaucoma suspect, dry eye, AMD, MGD) but were billed to the vision plan at $68 average reimbursement instead of medical insurance at $177 average. Extrapolated across the estimated 7,500 annual exams, this is $245,250 left on the table. Root cause: front desk sets billing category at check-in based on the insurance card the patient presents, doctors document medical diagnoses but don't trigger a billing category change, and no post-exam coding review exists. RevolutionEHR has a billing decision-support feature that isn't activated. Fix: activate RevolutionEHR billing flags, train doctors to toggle billing category when medical dx is found, implement end-of-day coding review.

2. **Optical capture rate is 48% vs. 65% benchmark — $304,150 in lost quarterly sales.** The exit survey reveals the primary driver: 28% of patients say "no one walked me over to the optical." The current workflow sends patients from exam to checkout, hands them their prescription, and points at the dispensary. There is no warm handoff protocol. Additionally, 52% cite price as the reason — but the average patient out-of-pocket is only $18 per pair, suggesting the price perception problem is about communication, not actual cost. Fix: implement a clinical-to-optical handoff protocol where the doctor or tech introduces the patient to the optician by name, the optician is pre-briefed on the Rx and any lifestyle needs discussed during the exam, and the patient never holds their own prescription until after the dispensing conversation.

3. **Contact lens annual supply rate is 22% vs. 50% benchmark — $149,760/year in lost revenue.** Opticians are presenting CL pricing per-box ("$35 per box, how many do you want?") instead of framing the annual supply with manufacturer rebate ("$380 for the year after your $100 rebate — that's $1.04/day"). The per-box framing invites patients to buy the minimum and reorder later from online competitors. Every patient who leaves with a 3-month supply is a patient who will likely buy the next 9 months from 1-800 Contacts. Fix: change the default CL presentation to annual supply with rebate, train opticians on the daily cost framing, display manufacturer rebate information at the CL dispensing station, and make annual supply the assumed default with per-box as the exception.

4. **Frame inventory turnover is 0.82x — $88,592 tied up with 34.5% over 12 months old.** The practice is carrying nearly a year's worth of inventory that it can't sell fast enough. 312 frames are over 12 months old ($30,576 in dead/dying stock). There is no markdown protocol, no vendor exchange program, no frame rep visit schedule, and some frames still bear tags from a 2023 purchase. Fix: immediate dead stock clearance event (50-70% off frames >18 months), establish vendor return/exchange agreements, implement quarterly frame board reviews with reps, set 12-month maximum frame age policy with automatic markdown at 9 months, and reduce total frame count to 600-700 (more curated, faster-turning selection).

5. **Insurance verification not happening until day-of-visit — 15% of patients reschedule.** 84 patients in Q1 had to reschedule because insurance issues weren't discovered until check-in. 30% of those patients likely never rebooked (25 lost patients). The front desk only captures the plan name at scheduling — no member ID, no group number, no pre-verification. RevolutionEHR's Eyefinity integration for real-time eligibility checking is active but staff was never trained. Fix: capture full insurance details at scheduling (plan, member ID, group number, DOB), run Eyefinity verification 48 hours before every appointment, flag exceptions for same-day resolution, and communicate benefits to patients before their visit via text/email so they arrive knowing their costs.

6. **Average frame sale is $134 against $150 average benefit — not upselling above benefit level.** When the average frame sale ($134) is below the average frame benefit ($150), the insurance is paying for the entire frame and the opticians aren't presenting anything above the covered amount. The average patient out-of-pocket for a complete pair is $18 — meaning virtually no upgrades are happening. AR coating is at 42% (vs. 80% benchmark), second-pair rate is 4% (vs. 15%), and photochromic rate is 18% (vs. 35%). The opticians are order-takers, not consultants. Fix: train opticians to present premium frames first (starting above benefit level), make AR coating the assumed default ("your lenses come with anti-reflective coating — here's why that matters"), present second-pair opportunities tied to lifestyle ("you mentioned you spend 8 hours on a computer — a dedicated pair for that would..."), and reframe insurance as a floor, not a ceiling.
