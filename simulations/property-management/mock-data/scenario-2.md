## Current Screening Process Notes (for evaluator reference)

### Planted Issues Summary

| # | Issue | Category | Key Detail |
|---|-------|----------|------------|
| 1 | **Top applicant (Whitfield) has unverifiable employment stability** | Screening Accuracy | Only 2 weeks at new job, in 90-day probationary period. Income is based on offer letter, not established earning history. No prior employer disclosed. If he loses the job during probation, income drops to zero. Previous landlord reference checks out (Bergen County) but current "landlord" reference is fraudulent. |
| 2 | **Fake landlord reference — phone number belongs to applicant** | Screening Fraud | Whitfield's "current landlord" phone number (201-555-0147) is registered to James Whitfield. The person answering identified as "Michael Reynolds" and gave a glowing reference. This is a classic screening fraud red flag. The legitimate prior reference (Bergen County Apartments) noted "a few late payments." |
| 3 | **Screening criteria not documented in writing** | Fair Housing / Disparate Impact | Criteria exist only as verbal instructions and leasing team memory. No written policy provided to applicants. If any denied applicant files a Fair Housing complaint, the property cannot demonstrate consistent, non-discriminatory application of criteria. This is a textbook disparate impact setup. |
| 4 | **Service animal treated as pet policy issue** | Fair Housing Violation | Priya Mehta's psychiatric service animal (with therapist letter) is being treated as a pet policy question. Under FHA, service animals and emotional support animals are NOT pets — pet policies, pet deposits, pet rent, and breed/size restrictions do not apply. Asking for "training certification" for a service animal is not permitted under FHA. The correct process: verify disability-related need (therapist letter suffices), approve the accommodation. |
| 5 | **Criminal background as automatic disqualifier** | Fair Housing / HUD Guidance | Marcus Thompson denied automatically for a 2014 marijuana possession felony — 12 years ago, completed probation, no subsequent history, and marijuana is now legal in NJ. HUD 2016 guidance explicitly states blanket criminal disqualification policies have disparate impact on protected classes. Must conduct individualized assessment: nature of offense, time elapsed, rehabilitation evidence, nexus to housing safety. A 12-year-old marijuana conviction with no subsequent history cannot legally justify denial. |
| 6 | **Co-applicant income not properly calculated** | Screening Methodology | David & Maria Castillo have $10,000/month combined income (5.41x rent) but are being evaluated on individual income only. No written policy addresses co-applicant methodology. Industry standard: evaluate co-applicants on combined household income when both are on the lease. Denying based on individual income when combined income clearly qualifies is inconsistent and creates disparate impact risk (penalizes married couples, co-applicants). |
