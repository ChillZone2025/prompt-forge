# Industry Context: HR & People Ops

## What This Industry Covers

Human Resources and People Operations encompasses the full lifecycle of managing an organization's workforce — from attracting and hiring talent through their entire tenure to offboarding and alumni relations. The function operates at the intersection of business strategy, employment law, organizational psychology, and data analytics.

Core domains include talent acquisition (sourcing, screening, interviewing, offer management), onboarding and integration, compensation and benefits design, performance management, employee relations and workplace investigations, HR compliance and policy administration, workforce planning, learning and development, and organizational design.

The industry's defining characteristic is that it directly affects people's livelihoods, legal rights, and workplace dignity. A flawed hiring process creates bias liability. A botched investigation enables harassment. A broken compensation structure drives attrition and invites pay equity lawsuits. Every HR decision has legal exposure, and the consequences of getting it wrong range from EEOC complaints and class-action lawsuits to reputational damage that makes future hiring nearly impossible.

Modern People Ops has shifted from administrative compliance ("personnel department") to strategic business partnership, but the compliance foundation remains non-negotiable. Companies that skip compliance to move fast end up in front of regulators, in court, or on the front page.

## Key Tools & Platforms

### HRIS / HCM (Human Resource Information Systems / Human Capital Management)
- **Workday** — Enterprise-grade HCM platform covering HR, payroll, benefits, talent management, and workforce planning. Dominant in companies with 1,000+ employees. Known for complex implementation but comprehensive reporting and analytics.
- **BambooHR** — SMB-focused HRIS handling employee records, PTO tracking, onboarding workflows, and basic reporting. Clean interface, popular with companies under 500 employees.
- **ADP Workforce Now / ADP Vantage** — Payroll-first platform expanded to full HCM. ADP processes payroll for approximately 1 in 6 US workers. Workforce Now serves mid-market; Vantage serves enterprise.
- **Paychex Flex** — Payroll and HR platform for small to mid-size businesses. Strong in payroll tax compliance and benefits administration. Competes directly with ADP in the SMB segment.
- **UKG (Ultimate Kronos Group)** — Formed from the merger of Ultimate Software and Kronos. Strong in workforce management (scheduling, time tracking, labor analytics) and HR service delivery. UKG Pro handles HCM; UKG Dimensions handles workforce management.
- **Paylocity** — Cloud HCM for mid-market companies. Strong in payroll, benefits administration, and employee engagement tools. Growing rapidly as a Workday alternative for companies that don't need enterprise complexity.
- **Namely** — Mid-market HR platform combining HRIS, payroll, benefits, and talent management. Targets companies with 50-1,000 employees.
- **Rippling** — Unified platform for HR, IT, and Finance. Handles onboarding that includes device provisioning, app access, and payroll setup in a single workflow. Growing fastest among tech-forward companies.

### Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS)
- **Greenhouse** — Structured hiring platform built around interview scorecards, hiring team calibration, and DE&I analytics. The dominant ATS in tech companies. Emphasizes reducing bias through structured interview design.
- **Lever** — ATS and CRM combination (LeverTRM). Strong in candidate relationship management and nurture campaigns. Popular with companies that emphasize proactive sourcing over inbound applications.
- **iCIMS** — Enterprise ATS serving large-volume hiring. Strong in career site management, text-based recruiting, and integration with staffing agencies. Common in healthcare, retail, and manufacturing.
- **LinkedIn Recruiter** — Sourcing platform with access to 900M+ LinkedIn profiles. Boolean search, InMail outreach, and pipeline management. The primary sourcing tool for most corporate recruiting teams.

### Performance & Engagement
- **Lattice** — Performance management, engagement surveys, OKR tracking, and compensation management. Combines continuous feedback with structured review cycles. Strong analytics dashboard.
- **15Five** — Continuous performance management with weekly check-ins, OKR tracking, 1-on-1 agendas, and engagement surveys. Emphasizes manager enablement and coaching.
- **Culture Amp** — Employee experience platform covering engagement surveys, performance reviews, and development planning. Known for benchmarking data and people science research team.

### Compensation & Benchmarking
- **Mercer** — Global compensation benchmarking, job evaluation, and total rewards consulting. Mercer's compensation surveys are the gold standard for market data across industries.
- **Radford (Aon)** — Technology-sector compensation surveys and benchmarking. The definitive data source for tech company compensation, particularly for equity compensation and startup pay practices.
- **Glassdoor for Employers** — Employer branding platform with salary data, company reviews, and interview experience ratings. Increasingly used for competitive intelligence on compensation and employer reputation.

## Key Frameworks & Methodologies

### Talent Acquisition
- **Structured Interview Design** — Interview processes built on predetermined questions, standardized scoring rubrics, and interviewer training. Research consistently shows structured interviews are 2x more predictive of job performance than unstructured interviews. Core elements: job analysis to identify critical competencies, behavioral and situational questions mapped to competencies, anchored rating scales (1-5 with behavioral descriptors), interviewer calibration sessions, and independent scoring before debrief.
- **Competency-Based Interviewing** — Interview questions designed to assess specific competencies (leadership, analytical thinking, collaboration) through behavioral evidence. Uses the STAR method (Situation, Task, Action, Result) to elicit concrete examples from candidates' past experience.
- **Adverse Impact Analysis (4/5ths Rule)** — EEOC's Uniform Guidelines on Employee Selection Procedures require that selection rates for any protected group must be at least 80% (4/5ths) of the rate for the highest-scoring group. If 60% of white applicants pass a screening step but only 40% of Black applicants pass, the selection ratio is 40/60 = 67%, which is below the 80% threshold and constitutes adverse impact.

### Compensation
- **Job Evaluation Methods** — Systematic approaches to establishing internal job worth. Point-factor method assigns points across compensable factors (skill, effort, responsibility, working conditions). Market pricing method benchmarks jobs directly against external survey data. Hybrid approaches combine internal equity analysis with external market data.
- **Pay Equity Analysis** — Statistical examination of compensation data to identify pay disparities correlated with protected characteristics (gender, race, ethnicity, age). Uses multiple regression analysis controlling for legitimate factors (job level, tenure, performance, location, education). Any unexplained gaps exceeding 2-3% warrant investigation and remediation.
- **Total Rewards Framework** — Holistic view of employee compensation including base salary, variable pay (bonus, commission), equity compensation (stock options, RSUs), benefits (health, retirement, PTO), and non-monetary rewards (development, flexibility, recognition). WorldatWork's Total Rewards model is the standard framework.
- **Compa-Ratio Analysis** — Employee's actual pay divided by the midpoint of their pay band. A compa-ratio of 1.00 means the employee is paid at midpoint. Below 0.80 signals underpayment risk; above 1.20 signals overpayment or band compression. Used to identify systemic pay issues across the organization.

### Performance Management
- **9-Box Talent Grid** — Two-axis matrix plotting performance (low/medium/high) against potential (low/medium/high). Creates 9 categories used for succession planning, development investment, and talent review. "High performance / High potential" (top-right) are succession candidates; "Low performance / Low potential" (bottom-left) are managed out. Criticized for subjective "potential" assessments that can embed bias.
- **OKRs (Objectives and Key Results)** — Goal-setting framework popularized by Intel and Google. Objectives are qualitative and aspirational; Key Results are quantitative and measurable. Typically set quarterly with mid-cycle check-ins. Designed for ambitious stretch goals — achieving 70% is considered success. Used to align individual goals with company strategy.
- **Progressive Discipline** — Escalating corrective action process: verbal warning, written warning, final written warning, termination. Each step must be documented with specific behavioral issues, expectations for improvement, timeline, and consequences of non-improvement. The documentation trail is the company's primary defense in wrongful termination claims.
- **Performance Improvement Plans (PIPs)** — Formal written plan for employees failing to meet performance expectations. Specifies performance gaps, measurable improvement targets, support/resources provided, timeline (typically 30-90 days), and consequences of non-improvement. PIPs serve dual purposes: genuine performance recovery tool and documented evidence supporting potential termination.

### Employee Relations
- **Workplace Investigation Methodology** — Structured process for investigating complaints: intake and assessment, investigation plan, witness interviews (complainant first, then witnesses, respondent last), evidence collection, credibility assessment, findings, recommendations, and documentation. Investigations must be prompt, thorough, impartial, and confidential (to the extent possible). The investigator must not be in the reporting chain of either party.
- **SHRM Competency Model** — Society for Human Resource Management's framework defining 8 behavioral competencies (Leadership, Ethical Practice, Business Acumen, Relationship Management, Consultation, Critical Evaluation, Global & Cultural Effectiveness, Communication) and HR technical expertise areas (People, Organization, Workplace). The foundation for SHRM-CP and SHRM-SCP certifications.

## Regulations

### Federal Anti-Discrimination Laws (US)
- **Title VII of the Civil Rights Act (1964)** — Prohibits employment discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, and national origin. Applies to employers with 15+ employees. Covers hiring, firing, compensation, terms, conditions, and privileges of employment. Sexual harassment (quid pro quo and hostile work environment) falls under Title VII's sex discrimination prohibition.
- **Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA, 1990 / ADA Amendments Act, 2008)** — Prohibits discrimination against qualified individuals with disabilities. Requires reasonable accommodation unless it creates undue hardship. Applies to employers with 15+ employees. The interactive process for determining reasonable accommodations is a core HR compliance obligation.
- **Age Discrimination in Employment Act (ADEA, 1967)** — Prohibits discrimination against employees and applicants 40 years and older. Applies to employers with 20+ employees. Relevant to hiring practices (age-related screening questions), RIFs (disparate impact on older workers), and compensation decisions.
- **Equal Pay Act (1963)** — Requires equal pay for substantially equal work regardless of sex. Allows pay differences based on seniority, merit, quantity/quality of production, or "any factor other than sex." The burden is on the employer to justify pay disparities.
- **Genetic Information Nondiscrimination Act (GINA, 2008)** — Prohibits discrimination based on genetic information. Restricts employer acquisition of genetic information. Relevant to wellness programs and health-related inquiries.

### Federal Employment Standards
- **Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA)** — Establishes minimum wage, overtime pay (1.5x for hours over 40/week), recordkeeping, and child labor standards. The exempt vs. non-exempt classification is one of the most litigated areas in employment law. To be exempt, employees must meet both the salary threshold ($35,568/year as of 2024) and duties tests (executive, administrative, professional, computer employee, or outside sales).
- **Family and Medical Leave Act (FMLA, 1993)** — Provides up to 12 weeks of unpaid, job-protected leave per year for qualifying medical and family reasons (birth/adoption, serious health condition of employee or family member). Applies to employers with 50+ employees within 75 miles. Employees must have worked 12 months and 1,250 hours to qualify.
- **Worker Adjustment and Retraining Notification Act (WARN Act, 1988)** — Requires 60-day advance notice of mass layoffs (50+ employees at a single site) or plant closings. Failure to provide notice results in back pay liability for the notice period.

### Federal Compliance & Reporting
- **EEOC Reporting** — Employers with 100+ employees (or federal contractors with 50+) must file annual EEO-1 Component 1 reports detailing workforce demographics by job category, race, ethnicity, and sex. Used by EEOC for enforcement targeting.
- **OFCCP Compliance** — Office of Federal Contract Compliance Programs enforces affirmative action and non-discrimination obligations for federal contractors. Requires written Affirmative Action Plans (AAPs), annual updates, and availability/utilization analysis by job group.
- **I-9 / E-Verify** — Form I-9 verifies employment authorization for all new hires. Must be completed within 3 business days of hire. E-Verify is the electronic system for verifying I-9 information against government databases — mandatory for federal contractors, voluntary for most private employers (some states mandate it).

### Benefits-Related Regulations
- **HIPAA (1996)** — Protects health information privacy. HR must maintain separation between health plan administration data and employment decisions. Violations carry civil and criminal penalties.
- **COBRA (1985)** — Requires employers with 20+ employees to offer continued health coverage to employees who lose coverage due to qualifying events (termination, hours reduction). Coverage can extend 18-36 months. Failure to provide timely COBRA notices carries statutory penalties.
- **ERISA (1974)** — Governs employer-sponsored retirement and health benefit plans. Establishes fiduciary duties, reporting requirements (Form 5500), and participant rights. ERISA preempts most state laws governing employee benefit plans.

### State-Level Regulations
- **At-Will Employment Exceptions** — While most US states follow at-will employment, many have carved exceptions: implied contract (employee handbooks creating contractual obligations), public policy (termination for refusing illegal acts or exercising legal rights), and implied covenant of good faith.
- **Salary History Bans** — Over 20 states and localities prohibit employers from asking candidates about prior salary. Designed to break the cycle of pay inequity. California, New York, Massachusetts, Illinois, and Colorado have comprehensive bans.
- **Pay Transparency Laws** — Colorado (2021), New York City (2022), California (2023), Washington (2023), and others require employers to disclose salary ranges in job postings or upon request. Changing employer obligations significantly — compensation data that was confidential is now public.

## Common Workflows

### Talent Acquisition Workflow
1. **Requisition approval** — Hiring manager submits job requisition with business justification, level, budget, and job description. Finance and HR approve headcount.
2. **Job posting and sourcing** — Post to ATS, job boards, LinkedIn. Recruiter conducts proactive sourcing for passive candidates. Employee referral program activated.
3. **Resume screening** — Initial screen against minimum qualifications. AI-assisted screening (if used) must be validated for adverse impact.
4. **Phone/video screen** — Recruiter conducts 30-minute screen for basic qualifications, compensation expectations, and logistics.
5. **Interview loop** — Structured interviews with hiring team. Each interviewer assesses specific competencies using standardized scorecards.
6. **Debrief and decision** — Hiring team meets to share independent assessments. Consensus decision documented.
7. **Offer and negotiation** — Compensation within approved band, equity (if applicable), start date, and contingencies (background check, references).
8. **Background check and onboarding** — Background check completes before start date. Onboarding workflow triggered in HRIS.

### Annual Compensation Review Workflow
1. **Market data refresh** — Purchase current survey data (Radford, Mercer, or industry-specific surveys). Benchmark all jobs against market.
2. **Pay equity analysis** — Run regression analysis controlling for legitimate factors. Identify statistically significant gaps by gender, race, ethnicity.
3. **Budget allocation** — Finance approves total compensation adjustment budget (typically 3-5% of total payroll).
4. **Manager recommendations** — Managers propose individual increases based on performance, market position, and equity considerations.
5. **Calibration** — HR and leadership calibrate recommendations across departments for consistency and equity.
6. **Approval and communication** — Final approvals, compensation letters, and manager talking points for employee conversations.

### Workplace Investigation Workflow
1. **Complaint intake** — Receive complaint (written, verbal, anonymous hotline). Document initial details. Assess severity and urgency.
2. **Assessment and planning** — Determine scope, identify investigator (internal HR or external counsel), assess need for interim measures.
3. **Interim protective measures** — Implement measures to protect complainant and preserve evidence. May include temporary reassignment, no-contact directive, or administrative leave (for respondent, not complainant).
4. **Witness interviews** — Interview complainant first, then corroborating witnesses, then respondent last. Use open-ended questions. Document verbatim where possible.
5. **Evidence collection** — Gather documentary evidence (emails, Slack messages, access logs, calendar entries). Preserve evidence chain.
6. **Analysis and findings** — Assess credibility, weigh evidence, determine whether policy violation occurred based on preponderance of evidence standard.
7. **Recommendations and action** — Recommend corrective action proportional to findings. Document rationale.
8. **Communication and follow-up** — Notify parties of outcome (without disclosing specific discipline). Monitor for retaliation. Follow up with complainant.

## Common Terminology

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **FTE** | Full-Time Equivalent — a unit of measurement equal to one full-time employee's workload. Two half-time employees = 1.0 FTE. |
| **Headcount** | Total number of employees on payroll, regardless of full-time or part-time status. Distinguished from FTE. |
| **Attrition Rate** | Percentage of employees who leave the organization over a period (voluntary + involuntary). Typically annualized. |
| **Turnover Rate** | Often used interchangeably with attrition rate, but some organizations distinguish voluntary turnover (resignations) from total attrition. |
| **Regrettable Turnover** | Voluntary departures of employees the organization wanted to retain — high performers and key talent. The most important turnover metric. |
| **Cost-Per-Hire** | Total recruiting costs divided by number of hires. SHRM standard includes internal costs (recruiter salary, referral bonuses) and external costs (agency fees, job boards, background checks). Average US cost-per-hire is approximately $4,700. |
| **Time-to-Fill** | Calendar days from requisition opening to offer acceptance. SHRM benchmark is approximately 36-42 days. Varies dramatically by role and industry. |
| **Time-to-Productivity** | Time from hire date until employee reaches full productivity. Harder to measure than time-to-fill but more important for business impact. |
| **Offer Acceptance Rate** | Percentage of offers extended that are accepted. Below 85% signals compensation, employer brand, or candidate experience problems. |
| **Compa-Ratio** | Employee's actual pay divided by the midpoint of their pay range. 1.00 = at midpoint. Used to assess individual pay position within the band. |
| **Pay Band (Pay Range)** | The minimum, midpoint, and maximum salary for a job level or grade. Typically structured with 40-60% spread (max is 40-60% above minimum). |
| **Range Penetration** | Where an employee falls within their pay band, expressed as a percentage. (Actual pay - Range minimum) / (Range maximum - Range minimum). |
| **Pay Compression** | When there is little meaningful difference in pay between employees at different levels, tenures, or performance levels. Often caused by market increases for new hires outpacing internal adjustments. |
| **Exempt vs. Non-Exempt** | FLSA classification. Exempt employees are salaried and not eligible for overtime. Non-exempt employees must be paid overtime (1.5x) for hours exceeding 40/week. Misclassification is a major litigation risk. |
| **Total Compensation (Total Cash)** | Base salary plus variable pay (bonus, commission). Distinguished from Total Rewards, which includes benefits and non-cash compensation. |
| **Equity Compensation** | Stock options, Restricted Stock Units (RSUs), performance shares, or other ownership-based compensation. Key retention tool in tech and growth-stage companies. |
| **Vesting Schedule** | Timeline over which equity grants become the employee's property. Standard is 4-year vesting with 1-year cliff (25% vests after year 1, remainder monthly or quarterly). |
| **Merit Increase** | Annual salary adjustment based on individual performance. Distinguished from market adjustment (alignment to external benchmarks) and equity adjustment (correcting internal disparities). |
| **Variable Pay** | Compensation tied to performance outcomes — annual bonus, sales commission, profit sharing, spot bonuses. Expressed as target percentage of base salary (e.g., "20% target bonus"). |
| **Adverse Impact** | When a facially neutral employment practice disproportionately affects a protected group. Measured using the 4/5ths (80%) rule from the EEOC Uniform Guidelines. |
| **Bona Fide Occupational Qualification (BFOQ)** | Narrow legal defense allowing discrimination based on a protected characteristic when it is reasonably necessary to the normal operation of a particular business. Does not apply to race. |
| **Right to Work** | State laws prohibiting mandatory union membership or dues payment as a condition of employment. Not related to employment authorization (common misconception). |
| **At-Will Employment** | Employment relationship that can be terminated by either party at any time, for any reason not prohibited by law, with or without notice. The default in most US states. |
| **Constructive Discharge** | When an employer makes working conditions so intolerable that a reasonable person would feel compelled to resign. Treated legally as an involuntary termination. |
| **Retaliation** | Adverse action against an employee for engaging in legally protected activity (filing a complaint, participating in an investigation, taking FMLA leave). Retaliation claims are the #1 charge filed with the EEOC. |
| **Interactive Process** | ADA-required dialogue between employer and employee to identify reasonable accommodations for a disability. Failure to engage in the interactive process is itself an ADA violation, even if no accommodation is ultimately required. |
| **Reduction in Force (RIF)** | Planned elimination of positions due to business reasons (restructuring, cost reduction). Subject to WARN Act, ADEA/OWBPA requirements (for employees 40+), and adverse impact analysis across protected categories. |
| **Separation Agreement** | Legal agreement between employer and departing employee typically including severance pay in exchange for a general release of claims. Must comply with OWBPA for employees 40+ (21-day consideration period, 7-day revocation period). |
| **HRIS** | Human Resource Information System — centralized database for employee records, payroll, benefits enrollment, and compliance reporting. |
| **People Analytics** | Application of statistical methods and data analysis to HR data for workforce planning, turnover prediction, engagement analysis, and compensation modeling. |
| **Employer of Record (EOR)** | Third-party entity that serves as the legal employer for tax and compliance purposes, typically used for international hiring without establishing a local entity. |
| **PEO (Professional Employer Organization)** | Co-employment arrangement where the PEO handles payroll, benefits, and HR compliance while the client company retains day-to-day management of employees. Common for companies with 10-150 employees. |

## 5 Most Common Mistakes

1. **Inconsistent documentation of performance issues** — Managers give positive performance reviews for years, then want to terminate an employee "for cause." Without documented performance concerns, written warnings, and a PIP, the termination looks pretextual. In litigation, the employee's attorney will use those positive reviews as evidence that the real reason for termination was discriminatory. HR must coach managers that documentation is not optional — it is the company's primary legal defense.

2. **Conducting workplace investigations without trained investigators** — Assigning a manager or untrained HR generalist to investigate a harassment complaint. The investigator fails to maintain neutrality, asks leading questions, interviews witnesses in the presence of management, doesn't implement interim protective measures, or takes months to complete the investigation. A poorly conducted investigation can be worse than no investigation — it creates evidence of institutional indifference that plaintiffs' attorneys will use to establish liability.

3. **Using stale market data for compensation decisions** — Running an annual compensation review using survey data that is 12-18 months old. In fast-moving markets (particularly tech), compensation benchmarks can shift 5-10% in a year. Stale data creates a false sense of market competitiveness, leading to preventable attrition of high performers who receive competing offers at current market rates. Compensation data older than 12 months should be aged (adjusted upward by market movement estimates) before use.

4. **Ignoring pay equity until litigation forces action** — Waiting for an EEOC charge, a class-action lawsuit, or a state audit to analyze pay equity data. Proactive pay equity analysis is both a legal risk mitigation strategy and a retention tool. Under the Equal Pay Act and state pay equity laws, the employer bears the burden of justifying pay disparities. Companies that discover and remediate gaps proactively can invoke the affirmative defense of good faith compliance efforts.

5. **Failing to maintain separation between HR investigations and employment decisions** — Using information gathered during a workplace investigation to make unrelated employment decisions (e.g., discovering during a harassment investigation that the complainant has been interviewing elsewhere, then including them in a subsequent RIF). Any adverse action following a complaint triggers retaliation scrutiny. The investigation file must be walled off from routine HR decision-making, and any coincidental adverse action must have independent, documented business justification.

## Relevant Certifications

| Certification | Issuing Body | Focus |
|--------------|-------------|-------|
| **SHRM-CP (Certified Professional)** | Society for Human Resource Management | Operational HR — policy implementation, employee relations, compliance, talent acquisition. Requires a combination of education and HR experience. The most widely recognized HR generalist certification. |
| **SHRM-SCP (Senior Certified Professional)** | Society for Human Resource Management | Strategic HR — organizational leadership, HR strategy, business acumen, policy development. Requires significant strategic-level HR experience. |
| **PHR (Professional in Human Resources)** | HR Certification Institute (HRCI) | US-focused operational HR — workforce planning, employee relations, compensation and benefits, business management. Strong emphasis on US employment law and compliance. |
| **SPHR (Senior Professional in Human Resources)** | HR Certification Institute (HRCI) | US-focused strategic HR — organizational strategy, policy development, workforce planning at scale. Requires extensive senior-level HR experience. |
| **CCP (Certified Compensation Professional)** | WorldatWork | Compensation design — job analysis, market pricing, pay structure design, variable pay, executive compensation, pay equity. The gold standard for compensation specialists. |
| **CEBS (Certified Employee Benefit Specialist)** | International Foundation of Employee Benefit Plans / Wharton School | Employee benefits — health plan design, retirement plan administration, total rewards strategy, regulatory compliance (ERISA, COBRA, HIPAA). |
| **AIRS Certifications (CIR, CDR, CSSR, ACIR)** | AIRS (an ADP company) | Recruiting specializations — Certified Internet Recruiter (CIR), Certified Diversity Recruiter (CDR), Certified Social Sourcing Recruiter (CSSR), Advanced Certified Internet Recruiter (ACIR). Focus on sourcing techniques and recruiting technology. |
| **GPHR (Global Professional in Human Resources)** | HR Certification Institute (HRCI) | International HR — global talent acquisition, mobility, compensation in multinational environments, cross-border compliance. |
