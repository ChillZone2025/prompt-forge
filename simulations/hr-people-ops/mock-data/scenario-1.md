## PLANTED ISSUES (for evaluation — agent should catch these)

1. **Pay equity flag — two female engineers significantly underpaid:** Sarah Chen (E-102) and Rachel Torres (E-106) are both female L3 Senior Software Engineers with 4.5 and 4.7 years tenure, both rated "Exceeds Expectations." They earn $108,500 and $109,200 respectively. Their male peers at the same level — James Morrison (4.8 years, Exceeds, $128,000) and Michael Rivera (3.9 years, only Meets Expectations, $121,000) — earn 15-17% more. Even Brian Jackson (3.2 years, Meets Expectations, $119,000) out-earns both women. There is no documented justification for these disparities. Under the Equal Pay Act and state pay equity laws, these gaps create significant legal exposure. The Mercer analysis explicitly flagged a 5.0% controlled gender gap concentrated in Engineering L3/L4 — these two employees are the most acute examples.

2. **Pay band compression between L5 and L6:** The L5 band is $155,000-$218,000 and the L6 band is $190,000-$260,000. The overlap zone is $190,000-$218,000 — an $28,000 range where employees at both levels have the same pay. This means 80% of the L6 range minimum overlaps with the L5 range. A promotion from L5 to L6 may come with no meaningful pay increase if the employee is already in the upper portion of the L5 band. This destroys the incentive value of promotion, undermines the leveling framework, and creates retention risk for high-performing L5s who see no financial reason to take on L6 responsibility. The standard best practice is no more than 25-30% overlap between adjacent bands.

3. **Stale market data:** The Radford survey data is dated January 2025, making it 14 months old by the time the April 2026 compensation adjustments take effect. The company's policy states "data within 12-month window" but the data will be 15 months old at implementation. Per Radford's own Q4 2025 Pulse Survey, the tech sector experienced 4.8% base salary movement since the data was collected. NovaTech has applied no aging factor to the data, which means every market comparison in this review understates the current market by approximately 4.8%. The company believes it is targeting the 60th percentile, but with unadjusted data, it is likely targeting the 50th percentile or lower for many roles.

4. **Employee above band maximum without justification:** Wei Zhang (E-116) is a Principal Engineer at L5 earning $266,000. The L5 band maximum is $218,000. His salary is 22% above the band maximum (compa-ratio of 1.43). While his performance rating is "Outstanding," there is no documented justification for his above-range pay — no retention agreement, no critical skills premium, no formal exception approval. This creates multiple problems: it signals either favoritism or an ad-hoc retention counter-offer that was never formalized; it compresses his pay against L6 and even some L7 employees; and it sets a precedent that bands are negotiable, undermining the entire compensation structure. If other employees discover this outlier, it will erode trust in the system's fairness.

5. **Merit budget below market movement:** The approved merit increase budget is 3.5% of total payroll. Radford's Q4 2025 data shows the tech market moved 4.8% year-over-year. This 1.3 percentage point shortfall means that even employees receiving the maximum merit increase will barely keep pace with market, and the majority of employees (receiving 2.5-3.5% for "Meets Expectations") will fall further behind. Over two years, this compounds to approximately 2.6% additional lag. The equity adjustment budget of 0.5% ($191,000) is wholly inadequate to address the identified pay equity gaps — the Mercer analysis identified a 5.0% gender gap and 4.5-5.7% ethnicity gaps that together would require far more than $191,000 to remediate.

6. **Equity refresh grants concentrated in engineering — no retention mechanism for non-engineering:** Equity refresh grants are awarded exclusively to Engineering and Product employees (152 of 298 headcount). Sales (52 people), Operations (31 people), Customer Success (18 people), Marketing (22 people), Finance (11 people), Legal (4 people), and People Ops (8 people) have no equity retention mechanism. For a Series C company with potential liquidity event in 1-3 years, this creates a massive retention gap — the 146 non-engineering employees have no financial incentive tied to company value creation. Sales retention is particularly concerning because account executives have portable skills and client relationships, and competitors offer equity as standard. The absence of any equity for Operations and Customer Success means the people responsible for customer retention and service delivery have no long-term alignment with company outcomes.
