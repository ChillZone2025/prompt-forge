## PLANTED ISSUES (for evaluation — agent should catch these)

1. **DeShawn Parker's minutes and load management crisis:** Parker is averaging 36.1 MPG (36.8 over the last 5 games), and his load data is alarming. His ACWR is 1.62 — well above the 1.5 threshold that research consistently links to elevated injury risk. His HRV has dropped from 58ms to 42ms over 4 weeks (a 28% decline indicating poor recovery). Force plate left-right asymmetry has nearly doubled from 8% to 14%, exceeding the 10% threshold for concern. He's listed as "general soreness" but the objective data indicates he is a significant injury risk. Coach Tillman has not reduced his minutes despite having the data. If Parker goes down with a soft-tissue injury in the final stretch, the team's NCAA hopes are over. The data is screaming for a 4-6 minute reduction in game minutes and modified practice loads.

2. **Three-point attempt rate leaving points on the table:** Ridgemont's 3PA rate (28.2%) is dramatically below the conference average (35.4%) and national average (37.8%). The analysis note in the data shows that the marginal value of shifting possessions from 2PA to 3PA is positive — the team is leaving approximately 4-5 points per game on the table. More critically, Terrence Allen is shooting 38.4% from three on limited attempts, and Marcus Webb is at 36.1%. These two players should be getting more three-point looks. The coaching staff appears to be running an offense optimized for 2010, not 2026.

3. **Bench production is a roster construction emergency:** Ridgemont's bench is last or second-to-last in the conference in every meaningful category: points (11th), efficiency (11th), plus-minus (dead last at -8.2 per game), and assist-to-turnover ratio (dead last). The all-bench lineup (Dawkins/Allen/Simmons/Foster/Chen) has a catastrophic -28.6 net rating. When the starters rest, the team hemorrhages leads. This isn't a "bench development" issue — it's a rotation construction problem. The coach needs to stagger his starters so that at least 2-3 are always on the floor, rather than substituting in 5-man bench units.

4. **Opponent scouting report is dangerously stale:** The Riverview Hawks scouting report is dated March 8 — three games and one major injury ago. The Hawks lost their starting PG, switched from man to zone defense, and saw their offensive rating drop 12+ points. Ridgemont's game plan would be built around a team that no longer exists. A student manager prepared the report and no one updated it. This is a basic operational failure that could cost the team a critical game against a conference contender.

5. **Terrence Allen is severely underutilized:** Allen is a freshman averaging 18.1 MPG off the bench, but his advanced stats are the best on the team: 19.4 PER (team high), 59.2% TS% (team high), +9.8 per-40 plus/minus (team high), 5.2 BPM (team high by a wide margin). His 114.8 offensive rating leads the team. He's a more efficient scorer than Parker (who gets twice the minutes) and a better defender (98.2 DRtg vs. Parker's 105.2). The data makes a strong case for Allen starting or, at minimum, playing 26-28 MPG — which would also help solve the Parker load management problem by reducing Parker's minutes.

6. **Second-half defensive collapse is a conditioning and rotation problem:** The team's defensive rating is 11.8 points worse in the second half (96.4 vs. 108.2). Opponent FG% jumps 7.6%, turnovers forced drop by half, and opponent free throw attempts spike (indicating fatigue-related fouling). The data explicitly shows that in games where starters play 34+ minutes, the 2H defensive deterioration is 12.2 points — but when starters stay under 32 minutes, it's only 3.8 points. This is not a scheme problem — it's a minutes/conditioning problem. The coaching staff is running a 7-man rotation in a sport that rewards 9-10 man rotations, and the team is physically breaking down in the second half. This directly connects to issues #1 (Parker's load), #3 (bench quality), and #5 (Allen's underutilization).
