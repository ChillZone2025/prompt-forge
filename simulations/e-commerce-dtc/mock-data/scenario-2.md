## PLANTED ISSUES (for evaluation — agents should catch these)

1. **ACOS on Sponsored Products is 45% for a product with 30% margin — losing money on every ad-driven sale.** The Radiance Serum has a 45.2% ACOS against a 30.1% Amazon gross margin (after FBA fees, referral fee, COGS, and return cost allocation). Every ad-attributed sale of the serum loses $9.82. The serum receives 57.7% of total SP spend because it has the highest absolute revenue — but advertising it at this ACOS is not building a flywheel, it's subsidizing sales at a loss. The correct response is either (a) reduce ACOS targets to below gross margin through bid optimization and negative keyword management, (b) shift ad budget to SKUs with positive ad-driven margins (Gentle Cleanser at 29.6% ACOS vs. 34.2% margin), or (c) accept short-term negative ACOS only if there's evidence it's building organic rank (which TACOS at 25% with no downward trend does not support).

2. **Two ASINs suppressed for 3+ weeks with zero impressions and no remediation.** The Vitamin C Booster (B0D9012IJK) and Retinol Night Treatment (B0D0123JKL) have been suppressed since March 6 and March 8 respectively for missing product attribute data. These were previously selling ~180 units/month combined ($9,200/mo). For 22-24 days, they have generated zero impressions and zero revenue. The fix is straightforward — add the missing attributes (Item Form, Skin Type, Active Ingredients, Usage Direction) in Seller Central. This should have been caught within 24-48 hours by anyone monitoring listing status. Meanwhile, the 740 units of dead inventory in FBA are accumulating $1,980/month in storage fees. This is a basic operational failure — no listing health monitoring process exists.

3. **Buy Box ownership is only 68% on the hero SKU — a 3P seller is winning on price by $0.50.** GlowDeals is an FBA seller offering the Radiance Serum at $64.49 vs. Luma's $64.99. With comparable fulfillment metrics and seller ratings, the $0.50 price difference is enough to win the Buy Box approximately 32% of the time. This represents ~$25,200/month in revenue diverted to a third-party seller. The brand needs to investigate (a) how GlowDeals is sourcing authentic Luma inventory (wholesale partner leaking, retail arbitrage, unauthorized distribution), (b) whether to enforce MAP pricing through distribution agreements, (c) whether to match or undercut GlowDeals temporarily while addressing the supply chain leak, and (d) whether to file a complaint through Brand Registry if the seller is unauthorized.

4. **Product reviews average 4.1 stars all-time but the most recent 30 reviews average 3.2 stars — quality trend decline.** The Radiance Serum's review trajectory has declined sharply since December 2025. Multiple reviewers reference a formula change — "not the same product," "changed formula," "different consistency." This pattern is consistent with either (a) an actual formulation change (new ingredient source, manufacturing process change), (b) a counterfeit/diversion problem (GlowDeals or other sellers providing non-authentic product that gets reviewed on the brand's listing), or (c) a batch quality issue from the manufacturer. If the 3.2★ recent trend continues, the all-time average will drop below 4.0★ within 2-3 months, which will significantly impact conversion rates. This requires immediate investigation into manufacturing records, batch consistency, and whether the 3P seller's inventory source is contributing to the negative reviews.

5. **FBA long-term storage fees are $4,200/month for slow-moving SKUs that should have been removed.** Five active SKUs and two suppressed SKUs are incurring aged inventory surcharges totaling $4,200/month ($50,400/year). The two suppressed ASINs alone account for $1,980/month for zero-revenue inventory. The slow-moving SKUs (Enzyme Mask, Hydrating Toner, Starter Kit) were over-ordered relative to their Amazon velocity — likely based on DTC sell-through rates without adjusting for lower Amazon demand. The correct actions are: (a) immediately create removal orders for suppressed ASIN inventory, (b) run liquidation campaigns (coupons, Lightning Deals) for overstocked slow-movers, (c) establish inventory health monitoring with alerts for aging thresholds, and (d) revise reorder quantities based on Amazon-specific velocity, not DTC velocity.

6. **Competitor analysis compares against the wrong competitive set — mass market vs. actual clean beauty prestige competitors.** Luma's brand strategy benchmarks against CeraVe, Neutrogena, and The Ordinary ($7-$29 mass market), but Amazon's own shopper data shows actual comparison shopping happens against Herbivore, Biossance, Youth to the People, and Drunk Elephant ($34-$88 clean beauty prestige). This mismatch leads to: wrong pricing strategy (thinking $64.99 is "overpriced" when it's mid-range for the actual category), wrong keyword targeting (competing for "best face serum" against CeraVe's 48K reviews instead of "clean beauty serum" where review counts are 2K-8K), wrong A+ Content messaging (emphasizing "affordable" when the brand should emphasize ingredients, sourcing, and clean formulation credentials), and wrong conversion benchmarks (expecting 12%+ conversion rates typical of mass market when prestige skincare converts at 4-8% on Amazon). Every strategic decision downstream of competitor analysis is compromised.
