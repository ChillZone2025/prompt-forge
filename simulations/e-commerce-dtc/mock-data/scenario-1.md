## PLANTED ISSUES (for evaluation — agents should catch these)

1. **CAC increased 62% YoY but LTV has not been recalculated since launch.** The financial model uses an LTV of $142 based on 2021-2022 launch cohorts. Current cohorts (Q1 2026) are tracking toward 12-month LTV of $88-92 — a 38% decline. The reported LTV:CAC ratio of 4.6:1 is actually closer to 2.8:1 and falling. If CAC continues rising while LTV continues declining, the brand approaches a 1:1 LTV:CAC ratio within 2-3 quarters, at which point every new customer acquired is unprofitable. The business is making strategic decisions (ad spend scaling, hiring, inventory purchases) based on an LTV assumption that is 38% too high.

2. **Hero SKU (Radiance Serum) has a 23.4% return rate with uninvestigated "didn't like it" returns.** The Radiance Serum accounts for 52% of revenue but has a return rate 3-4x higher than every other product. Returns are categorized as "Didn't like it" (41.2%) without any sub-categorization or follow-up. Combined with "Not as described" (22.8%), over 64% of returns suggest either a product-market mismatch (marketing overpromises relative to reality), a formulation issue (texture, scent, absorption), or targeting the wrong customer segment. The return cost ($50,180/mo) reduces the Radiance Serum's net revenue by 26.6%. This is likely either a product problem or a marketing problem — but without investigating, the brand doesn't know which, and continues spending to acquire customers for a product that nearly 1 in 4 will return.

3. **Email list grew 15% but open rates dropped from 28% to 19% — list health is declining.** The list grew from 162K to 186K (+15%) but active subscribers (opened/clicked in 90 days) dropped from 61.6K to 52.2K (-15%). Open rates fell 32%, click rates fell 44%, and spam complaint rate increased 167%. The brand is not adequately sunsetting inactive subscribers — the sunset flow processes only 1,200 subscribers/month against a list that likely has 130K+ inactive subscribers. The deliverability damage from poor list health is now hurting engagement with active subscribers. Flow revenue dropped from 38% to 24% of email total, suggesting automated sequences are landing in spam/promotions tabs. At current trajectory, email as a channel will continue degrading and the brand will become even more dependent on paid acquisition.

4. **Meta ads ROAS reported as 4.2x using inflated 28-day click attribution window.** The Meta account is using a 28-day click, 1-day view attribution window — a setting that was manually re-enabled (Meta's default changed to 7-day click after iOS 14.5). Using 28-day click attribution inflates reported ROAS by 40-80% compared to 7-day click for most DTC brands. The actual 7-day ROAS is likely 2.3-2.6x, not 4.2x. At $68K/month Meta spend, this means the brand is significantly over-attributing revenue to Meta and making budget allocation decisions on inflated numbers. Combined with Google and TikTok also claiming attribution credit, the sum of platform-reported revenue likely exceeds actual revenue by 2-3x. Blended MER ($498K revenue / $130K ad spend) = 3.8x — plausible but still includes organic and returning customer revenue that shouldn't be attributed to ads.

5. **Subscription program has 45% churn by month 3 — likely interval mismatch.** The subscription default is 30 days, but the #1 cancellation reason (34.2%) is "I have too much product." For a 30ml serum used twice daily (typical application ~0.5ml), a bottle lasts approximately 30 days — but customers using it once daily (common with serums), less frequently, or as part of a rotation with other products would have a 45-60 day usage cycle. The massive month 2-3 churn spike (from 1,000 to 548) indicates subscribers realize they have too much product by the time the second or third shipment arrives. Changing the default interval to 45 or 60 days, adding a "skip" prompt before each shipment, and analyzing actual usage data would likely reduce churn by 25-40%.

6. **52% revenue concentration in a single SKU with no mitigation plan.** The Radiance Serum (LB-SRM-001) accounts for 52.4% of DTC product revenue. Top 2 SKUs represent 73% of revenue. This extreme concentration means: (a) any supply chain disruption to the serum's key ingredients halts majority of revenue, (b) a competitor launching a similar product at lower price creates existential risk, (c) a viral negative review or social media incident involving the serum can collapse sales overnight, (d) seasonal or trend fluctuations in serum demand directly impact total business health. There is no visible product diversification strategy, no evidence of new product pipeline, and no risk mitigation plan. The Starter Kit (LB-SET-001) at $48 is the closest thing to a diversification play but it functions more as a trial vehicle that funnels customers to the Radiance Serum, reinforcing rather than mitigating the concentration.
