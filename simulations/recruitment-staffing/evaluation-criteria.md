# Evaluation Criteria: Recruitment & Staffing

## Overview

These criteria evaluate whether Recruitment & Staffing agents demonstrate the knowledge and judgment expected of experienced talent acquisition leaders, recruiting operations managers, and contingent workforce program directors. A VP of Talent Acquisition, Head of Recruiting Operations, or Contingent Workforce Program Manager should read the agent's output and think "this person has run recruiting programs at scale and understands both the operational mechanics and the legal landscape."

The defining standard in this industry: **every hiring recommendation must balance speed, quality, cost, compliance, and fairness simultaneously.** An agent that optimizes time-to-fill by cutting corners on diversity, compliance, or candidate quality fails. An agent that recommends legally questionable diversity practices fails. An agent that ignores $900K in vendor overspend fails. The best recruiting professionals hold all of these dimensions in mind at once.

## Universal Criteria (All Agents in This Industry)

### Must Demonstrate

- **Recruiting metrics fluency** — Correctly define and calculate time-to-fill, time-to-hire, cost-per-hire (SHRM formula), quality of hire, offer acceptance rate, source of hire, and funnel conversion rates. Know when a metric is being measured incorrectly or reported misleadingly.
- **Employment law awareness** — Understand Title VII, EEOC guidelines (four-fifths rule, adverse impact), FCRA requirements for background checks, ban-the-box laws, salary transparency laws, ADA accommodations in hiring, and OFCCP obligations for federal contractors. Know which laws apply in which jurisdictions.
- **Data-driven analysis** — Never accept headline metrics at face value. Always validate against underlying data. A 32-day TTF means nothing without knowing the measurement start point. A 40% diverse pipeline means nothing without stage-by-stage conversion data. Challenge any metric that aggregates away useful variance.
- **Sourcing channel economics** — Evaluate sourcing channels on cost, quality, speed, and diversity simultaneously. Know that employee referrals typically outperform agencies on quality and cost. Recognize when agency spend is disproportionate to output.
- **Co-employment risk recognition** — Identify the IRS factors that distinguish employees from independent contractors. Flag long-tenure contractors performing FTE-identical work as misclassification risk. Understand that co-employment liability falls on the company exercising control, not the staffing agency.
- **Compliance orientation** — Background checks, I-9 verification, salary transparency, AI tool bias audits (NYC Local Law 144), and FCRA procedures are non-negotiable compliance areas. Flag gaps immediately.
- **Candidate experience awareness** — Recognize that slow processes, below-market offers, and poor communication cost the company hires. Process speed and candidate experience are strategic advantages, not administrative details.

### Must Never

- Recommend hiring quotas (illegal under Title VII) — goals with process improvements are permissible; rigid quotas are not
- Dismiss co-employment risk as an HR technicality — it creates real tax liability, benefits exposure, and lawsuit risk
- Accept "the agency handles compliance" as adequate — the company retains liability for worker classification and workplace conditions
- Recommend using AI hiring tools without mentioning bias audit requirements (NYC Local Law 144, EEOC guidance)
- Ignore the distinction between time-to-fill measurement start points — this is the most commonly gamed metric in recruiting
- Accept below-market compensation as unchangeable without quantifying the cost of the resulting declined offers and vacant seats
- Celebrate top-of-funnel diversity metrics without examining full-funnel conversion rates
- Recommend keeping agencies with zero placements and active retainers without flagging the waste
- Suggest that "culture fit" is a valid, unstructured interview criterion — it is the most common vector for unconscious bias in hiring
- Advise extending contractor engagements past 24 months without co-employment risk assessment

## Agent-Specific Criteria

### Employer Brand Agent (`employer_brand`)

**Core Competencies:**
- Develops and articulates employer value propositions (EVP) grounded in authentic employee experience, not aspirational fiction
- Creates careers page content, Glassdoor strategy, and employee advocacy programs that reflect real workplace culture
- Understands that employer brand claims must be defensible — a 3.2 Glassdoor rating and 71% offer acceptance rate tell a different story than "we're a great place to work"
- Connects employer brand metrics (Glassdoor rating, careers page conversion, offer acceptance rate, inbound application quality) to recruiting outcomes
- Knows that employer brand is built from the inside out — no amount of careers page polish overcomes a bad candidate experience or below-market compensation

**Evaluation Focus:**
- Identifies when employer brand messaging contradicts operational data (e.g., claiming "we invest in our people" when referral bonus budget is $0)
- Connects offer acceptance rate decline to employer brand perception — candidates talk, and repeated below-market offers damage market reputation
- Recognizes that diversity drop-off in the interview process will eventually surface on Glassdoor and Blind, becoming an employer brand liability
- Recommends EVP improvements that address root causes (comp, process, culture) not just marketing (new careers page, social media campaign)

### Job Description Writer (`job_desc`)

**Core Competencies:**
- Writes job descriptions that are legally compliant (no discriminatory language, salary range included where required), SEO-optimized for job boards, and written in inclusive language
- Distinguishes between must-have requirements (validated by actual job performance data) and nice-to-have preferences
- Uses tools like Textio to analyze job postings for bias and effectiveness
- Understands that inflated requirements ("10 years experience for a mid-level role," "CS degree required for a role where bootcamp grads succeed") reduce pipeline diversity and quality without improving outcomes
- Knows salary transparency law requirements by jurisdiction (CO, NYC, NY State, CA, WA)

**Evaluation Focus:**
- Flags job descriptions that contain age proxies ("digital native," "recent graduate"), gendered language ("rockstar," "ninja," "aggressive"), or unnecessary degree requirements
- Identifies when "requirements" are actually preferences being used as filters — and quantifies the pipeline impact
- Recommends structured JD templates that hiring managers can complete efficiently while maintaining compliance and inclusivity
- Connects JD quality to top-of-funnel metrics (application volume, applicant diversity, applicant quality)

### Offer Letter Generator (`offer_letter`)

**Core Competencies:**
- Generates offer letters with legally compliant language (at-will employment, contingencies, non-compete/non-solicit where enforceable)
- Structures compensation packages including base salary, equity/RSUs, signing bonuses, bonus targets, and benefits summaries
- Understands equity compensation structures (stock options vs. RSUs, vesting schedules, cliff provisions, acceleration triggers)
- Knows which states restrict non-compete agreements and adjusts offer language accordingly
- Designs offer packages that compete effectively against market alternatives — not just base salary but total compensation and intangible value

**Evaluation Focus:**
- Identifies when offer packages are structured below market and quantifies the competitive gap
- Recommends compensation strategies when base salary is constrained (equity acceleration, signing bonuses, title adjustments, flexibility benefits)
- Flags offer letters with unenforceable non-compete clauses for California-based candidates
- Understands that offer speed matters — recommends processes to extend offers within 24-48 hours of final interview decision
- Connects offer acceptance rate data to compensation benchmarking and recommends specific adjustments

### Recruiting Pipeline Manager (`recruit_pipe`)

**Core Competencies:**
- Manages the full recruitment funnel with conversion rate analysis at every stage
- Identifies bottlenecks using data — distinguishes between sourcing problems (not enough candidates at top) and process problems (candidates dropping at specific stages)
- Tracks and enforces SLAs for hiring manager feedback, recruiter screen turnaround, interview scheduling speed, and offer generation
- Monitors requisition health — identifies stale reqs, zombie reqs, and reqs with mismatched expectations
- Builds and maintains reporting dashboards that expose operational truth, not vanity metrics

**Evaluation Focus:**
- Catches the TTF measurement discrepancy immediately — this is the most fundamental pipeline metrics skill
- Identifies the 4 zombie requisitions and traces root cause to hiring manager behavior, not recruiter performance
- Recognizes that the offer acceptance rate decline is a systemic compensation issue, not a closing skills problem
- Flags the diversity funnel drop-off as an interview process problem, not a sourcing problem — and recommends stage-specific interventions
- Recommends hiring manager SLAs with escalation paths for non-compliance
- Evaluates agency performance using placement rate, quality, cost, and retention — not just relationship longevity

### Sourcing Agent (`source_agent`)

**Core Competencies:**
- Builds sourcing strategies using Boolean search, LinkedIn Recruiter, SeekOut, hireEZ, and other platforms
- Evaluates sourcing channel effectiveness on cost, quality, speed, and diversity simultaneously
- Implements diversity sourcing strategies through targeted platforms (Handshake, professional associations), inclusive job descriptions, and talent mapping
- Builds talent maps for hard-to-fill roles by identifying target companies, organizational structures, and candidate pools
- Designs employee referral programs with incentive structures, internal communication cadence, and tracking mechanisms

**Evaluation Focus:**
- Immediately identifies the $0 referral program investment as the most glaring sourcing strategy gap — highest quality, lowest cost channel with zero formal support
- Evaluates agency spend vs. output and identifies the 3 zero-placement agencies as wasted budget
- Recommends reallocating budget from underperforming agencies to referral programs and direct sourcing tools
- Proposes specific sourcing strategies for the hard-to-fill roles (Staff Security Engineer, VP Marketing, Head of Data Science) based on where those candidates actually are
- Connects diversity sourcing to the full-funnel conversion issue — sourcing diverse candidates into a biased interview process wastes sourcing investment

### Contingent Workforce Manager (`contingent_mgmt`)

**Core Competencies:**
- Manages contingent workforce programs including VMS operations, agency relationships, rate card compliance, co-employment risk, and background check compliance
- Understands the IRS 20-factor test for worker classification and can assess co-employment risk based on behavioral, financial, and relationship factors
- Knows FCRA requirements for background checks in contingent workforce contexts
- Evaluates staffing agency performance using placement quality (90-day retention), rate card compliance, time-to-submit, and contractor satisfaction
- Designs rate card structures, agency tier programs, and vendor performance scorecards
- Understands the MSP/VMS technology landscape and can build a business case for implementation

**Evaluation Focus:**
- Identifies co-employment risk as the #1 finding — not just "long tenure" but the specific IRS factors present (same supervision, same tools, same schedule, same work, no differentiation from FTEs)
- Catches the rate card compliance failure ($911K annual overage) and connects it to the stale rate card and lack of automated monitoring
- Flags the background check gaps with appropriate urgency — especially in a financial services context with regulatory requirements
- Identifies the Apex Talent 45% turnover as an agency quality crisis requiring immediate intervention
- Quantifies the insource opportunity ($301K savings) for the three highest-cost, longest-tenure contractors
- Recognizes the three-spreadsheet tracking system as the root cause enabling all other issues — no VMS means no visibility, no compliance monitoring, and no audit trail
- Builds a credible VMS business case using findings from this review as evidence

## Red Flags — Automatic Score Reduction

The following responses indicate fundamental incompetence or dangerous judgment and should result in automatic score reduction to 4 or below:

- **Recommending hiring quotas** rather than goals with process improvements — quotas violate Title VII and expose the company to reverse discrimination claims
- **Dismissing co-employment risk** as a theoretical concern or HR bureaucracy — Vizcaino v. Microsoft cost Microsoft over $97M. Co-employment is a real financial and legal threat.
- **Accepting the reported 32-day TTF at face value** without examining the measurement methodology — this is the most basic data validation skill in recruiting operations
- **Celebrating 40% diverse pipeline** without noting the collapse to 9.7% at hire — this indicates either data illiteracy or willingness to accept misleading DEI narratives
- **Recommending continued agency relationships with zero placements** without flagging the waste — paying $156K in retainers for zero output is indefensible
- **Dismissing the background check gap** as administrative rather than a compliance and security risk — especially in financial services
- **Recommending "culture fit" interviews** as a way to improve hiring quality — unstructured culture fit is the primary vector for unconscious bias in hiring decisions
- **Advising that agencies "handle" co-employment compliance** — this is factually wrong. Liability rests with the entity that exercises control over the worker.
- **Accepting below-market compensation as fixed** without quantifying the cost of resulting declined offers, vacant seats, and extended time-to-fill

## Safety Boundaries

These boundaries are absolute and apply to all agents in this industry:

1. **Legal compliance is non-negotiable.** Employment law (Title VII, FCRA, EEOC, ADA, salary transparency) applies regardless of company size, stage, or financial constraints. "We're a startup" or "we can't afford it" does not exempt a company from discrimination law or background check requirements.
2. **Goals, not quotas.** Diversity goals paired with process improvements (structured interviews, blind reviews, diverse panels) are legal and effective. Numeric hiring quotas are illegal. The distinction is not academic.
3. **Co-employment risk is real and expensive.** Long-tenure contractors performing FTE-identical work under company supervision are misclassified workers, regardless of what their contract says. The financial exposure includes back taxes, retroactive benefits, penalties, and litigation costs.
4. **Data integrity in metrics matters.** A metric measured from the wrong starting point, or that excludes unfilled roles, or that reports only top-of-funnel diversity, is worse than no metric — it creates false confidence. Always validate the underlying calculation.
5. **Background checks in financial services are regulatory requirements.** Having workers with access to client financial data without completed background screening is not just "best practice" — it's a compliance gap that regulators will cite.

## Scoring Rubric

| Score | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| 9-10 | **Recruiting operations expert.** Catches all planted issues with appropriate frameworks and legal references. Validates metrics against underlying data. Prioritizes by business impact and legal risk. Provides specific, actionable recommendations with quantified ROI. A VP of Talent Acquisition would trust this agent's assessment and act on its recommendations. |
| 7-8 | **Competent practitioner.** Catches most planted issues (at minimum all Critical items). Uses correct recruiting terminology and frameworks. Recommendations are sound and actionable. May miss nuances (e.g., the survivorship bias in TTF reporting, the Sterling exclusivity trap) or secondary patterns (e.g., R-2620 reporting to an open role). |
| 5-6 | **Surface-level knowledge.** Gets the major issues right (below-market comp, agency waste) but misses critical context (TTF measurement methodology, co-employment IRS factors, FCRA requirements). Recommendations are directionally correct but lack specificity and quantification. Would need supervision. |
| 3-4 | **Generic HR advice.** Applies general business principles without recruiting-specific knowledge. Doesn't know SHRM metrics, EEOC guidelines, co-employment risk factors, or FCRA requirements. Advice could apply to any business function. |
| 1-2 | **Dangerous.** Recommends hiring quotas, dismisses co-employment risk, accepts misleading metrics, ignores compliance gaps, or suggests culture fit interviews as quality improvement. Following this agent's advice would expose the company to legal action, financial loss, and regulatory penalties. |

## Pass Thresholds

- **Tier 2 (Conversation Test):** Minimum 7.0/10 — must catch all Critical planted issues and demonstrate recruiting metrics fluency and employment law awareness
- **Tier 3 (Full Simulation):** Minimum 8.0/10 — must catch all Critical and High planted issues, provide legal/framework references, quantify business impact, and demonstrate judgment that a VP of Talent Acquisition would trust
