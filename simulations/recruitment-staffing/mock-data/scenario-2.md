## PLANTED ISSUES (for evaluation — agent should catch these)

1. **15 contractors in same role 2+ years — critical co-employment risk and likely misclassification.** Fifteen contractors have tenure of 19+ months (and 12 have 25+ months), all performing identical work to FTEs in the same roles. They use company email, have company badges, attend all-hands meetings, follow the same schedules, report to the same managers, and are supervised identically to employees. Under the IRS 20-factor test, 13 of 16 factors indicate an employer-employee relationship. This is textbook worker misclassification. If the IRS, DOL, or a state labor board investigates (or if a contractor files a complaint), Meridian faces back taxes (employer share of FICA for all misclassified workers for the full duration), benefits liability, penalties, and potential class action exposure. Financial services firms face additional regulatory scrutiny — FINRA and state regulators take a dim view of workforce compliance failures.

2. **Rate card compliance at 64% — agencies charging above approved rates on 36% of placements.** 43 of 120 contractors are billed above the approved rate card, with an estimated $911,000 in annual overage. The rate card hasn't been updated since July 2024 (20 months old), giving agencies a plausible argument that market rates have shifted — but agencies are unilaterally billing above approved rates rather than formally requesting renegotiation. The spreadsheet-based tracking system has no automated rate comparison, so invoices are paid without exception flags. TechBridge (64% non-compliant) and AlphaStaff (50% non-compliant) are the worst offenders. A formal rate card renegotiation paired with automated rate compliance checking would either validate higher rates (with proper approvals) or recover hundreds of thousands in overpayments.

3. **Apex Talent has 45% turnover in first 90 days — systemic quality problem unaddressed.** Five of Apex Talent's last 11 placements left within 90 days — nearly 4x the 8.4% average of other agencies. The excuses vary (no-show, skills mismatch, ghosted), which is itself a red flag — a consistent root cause would be less concerning than five different failure modes. Apex has the lowest contractor satisfaction score (2.8/5) across all agencies. Despite this, Apex remains an active vendor with no performance improvement plan, no contract review, and no reduction in job orders. The $24,500 in estimated replacement costs doesn't account for the productivity loss, manager time, and team disruption from 5 failed placements. NextGen Staffing shows a similar pattern (45% turnover) that also warrants investigation.

4. **8 contractors have no current background check on file.** Eight contractors have no background check on file at all — not expired, but never conducted. This includes C-008 (Amara Osei, working 26 months with no check), C-025 (Jessica Taylor, Executive Assistant to the CEO with access to confidential documents), and C-039b (a temp admin not even in the main tracking spreadsheet). In a financial services company where workers access client data, financial systems, and confidential information, this is a serious compliance gap. FCRA governs background check procedures, but the more immediate risk is regulatory — FINRA and state financial regulators expect all personnel with access to client information to be properly screened. An additional 8 contractors have checks expired over 24 months.

5. **Three contractors performing identical FTE work at 35%+ cost premium — $301K annual insource opportunity.** C-001 (Sr. Java Developer, 38 months, $135/hr), C-003 (Data Engineer, 36 months, $140/hr), and C-005 (DevOps Engineer, 31 months, $145/hr) perform work identical to FTEs at the same level but cost 47-65% more per hour. The fully-loaded FTE equivalent cost is $88-$95/hr vs. contractor bill rates of $135-$145/hr. Converting these three to FTE would save an estimated $301,000 annually. Beyond cost, these contractors know the systems, have onboarded other team members, and are de facto employees in everything but classification. Not converting them is both financially wasteful and legally risky (their long tenure and FTE-identical treatment strengthens any misclassification claim).

6. **No VMS — tracking across 3 spreadsheets with conflicting data.** Meridian manages 120 contractors and $18.4M in annual spend using three disconnected Excel spreadsheets maintained by three different people (HR, IT, Procurement). The spreadsheets disagree on contractor count (118 vs. 52 vs. 120), spend totals, and bill rates. One contractor (C-039b) was not in any tracking spreadsheet until this review discovered them. Rate increases applied by agencies were captured in Procurement's invoice log but not in HR's master list. This means: (a) no single person can answer "how many contractors do we have?" accurately, (b) rate card compliance cannot be monitored without manual cross-referencing, (c) background check status is tracked nowhere comprehensively, (d) co-employment risk is invisible because no system flags tenure thresholds, and (e) spend analysis requires reconciling three conflicting sources. At $18.4M in annual contingent spend, the lack of a VMS is an operational and compliance failure.
