## PLANTED ISSUES (for evaluation — agent should catch these)

1. **Time-to-fill measured from wrong starting point.** The Head of Talent reports Q1 TTF as 32 days and claims improvement over Q4. However, the detailed breakdown shows TTF is measured from requisition approval date — not from when the role was actually posted. For 52% of hires, there was a gap of 10+ days between approval and posting (average 6.2 days across all hires). The true TTF from approval to acceptance averages 34.4 days. From posting to acceptance, it's 28.2 days. Neither number is 32 days. The reported figure appears to use a hybrid calculation that understates reality. The actual experience for hiring managers — from "I submitted this req" to "candidate accepted" — is 34.4 days, not 32 days. A rigorous agent should identify the measurement discrepancy and recommend standardizing the definition.

2. **Employee referrals have the highest quality but zero investment.** Referrals produced 25.8% of hires with the highest quality score (4.4/5), fastest TTF (24 days), and best retention (95% at 6 months) — yet the company has allocated $0 to a referral program. No referral bonuses, no structured submission process, no internal communication about open roles. Meanwhile, staffing agencies receive $680K annually (36.8% of budget) and produce only 12.9% of hires at the lowest quality score (3.0/5). A $50K referral program budget (1/13th of agency spend) could plausibly double referral hires and dramatically improve overall quality-of-hire and cost-per-hire.

3. **Four requisitions open 90+ days with zero pipeline due to hiring manager disengagement.** R-2605, R-2609, R-2614, and R-2620 have been open 91-105 days with zero or near-zero active candidates. In each case, the root cause is hiring manager behavior — not reviewing candidates, not providing feedback, not scheduling interviews, or (in R-2620's case) not having a hiring manager at all because the role reports to another open position. These zombie requisitions consume recruiter and agency resources, distort pipeline metrics, and should either be staffed with engaged hiring managers or formally put on hold.

4. **Offer acceptance rate dropped from 88% to 71% due to below-market compensation.** The offer acceptance rate dropped from 87.5% (Q4) to 73.8% (Q1) — a 13.7 percentage point decline. Of 11 declined offers, 7 cited compensation as the primary factor. For three key roles (Staff Security Engineer IC5, Sr. Account Executive IC4, Enterprise AE IC5), Helios offers were 12-15% below Radford market median. Two different candidates for the same Staff Security Engineer role both declined citing below-market comp. The company is spending recruiter time, interview hours, and candidate goodwill generating offers that the market predictably rejects.

5. **Diversity drops from 40% at top of funnel to 8% at offer — interview process is the bottleneck.** Helios sources 40% diverse candidates at top of funnel and maintains parity through phone screen (40.1%). The massive drop-off occurs at onsite interview (26.9%) and offer (14.3%). Diverse candidates convert from phone screen to onsite at 23.2% vs. 34.6% overall (11.4 point gap) and from onsite to offer at 20.7% vs. 38.9% (18.2 point gap). The Head of Talent celebrates top-of-funnel diversity without acknowledging the interview-stage collapse. This pattern strongly suggests bias or structural barriers in the interview process — interviewer calibration, scoring rubric consistency, panel composition, or "culture fit" assessments that penalize difference. The sourcing team's work is being wasted.

6. **Agency spend of $340K/year with 23% utilization — 3 agencies have zero placements but active contracts.** Five staffing agencies are engaged, but only two (TechTalent Partners and Apex) have made any placements. Pinnacle Search (9 months, 3 candidates submitted, 0 hires), Sterling Executive (6 months, $96K in retainers paid, 0 hires on exclusive searches), and NovaTech Staffing (5 months, 0 placements) are generating zero return. Sterling Executive holds exclusive search contracts for VP Marketing and Head of Data Science — roles that remain open because the hiring managers (CEO and S. Nguyen) aren't engaging with submitted candidates, meaning Sterling cannot succeed even if their candidates are strong. The company is paying $156K in retainers to agencies with zero placements while having $0 for employee referral bonuses.
