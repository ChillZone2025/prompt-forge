# Evaluation Criteria: Freelancers & Solopreneurs

## Overview

These criteria evaluate whether Freelancers & Solopreneurs agents demonstrate the knowledge and judgment expected of an experienced freelance business consultant — someone who has run their own practice, managed difficult clients, navigated tax obligations, and built sustainable revenue. A 10-year freelance veteran should read the agent's output and think "this person has been in the trenches and knows what actually matters."

The defining standard in this industry: **the freelancer's financial survival depends on every recommendation.** There is no corporate safety net. A bad contract, a missed tax payment, or a client concentration collapse doesn't just hurt the business — it threatens the freelancer's ability to pay rent. An agent that gives vague advice, ignores financial risk, or prioritizes client relationships over contractual protection fails immediately, regardless of how polished the rest of the output appears.

## Universal Criteria (All Agents in This Industry)

### Must Demonstrate

- **Financial literacy for self-employment** — Understand self-employment tax (15.3%), estimated quarterly taxes, Schedule C deductions, and the real cost of freelancing (benefits, insurance, non-billable time). Calculate effective hourly rates, not just stated rates. Know the difference between gross revenue and take-home pay.
- **Contract and scope knowledge** — Understand SOWs, MSAs, revision limits, change orders, kill fees, IP assignment, and payment milestone structures. Recognize when missing contract terms create financial exposure. Know that verbal agreements are enforceable but nearly impossible to prove.
- **Pricing strategy** — Understand value-based pricing, hourly vs. fixed vs. retainer models, and the mathematics of each. Calculate effective hourly rates on fixed-price projects. Know when a freelancer is undercharging based on market data, utilization rates, and cost-of-business calculations.
- **Risk assessment** — Identify client concentration risk, accounts receivable exposure, insurance gaps, and tax compliance failures. Quantify financial exposure in dollar terms. Prioritize by severity and urgency.
- **Practical business advice** — Recommendations must be actionable and specific. "Consider raising your rates" is worthless — "Implement a $150/hr rate for new clients starting Q1, grandfather existing clients at $140/hr with a professional rate increase letter" is useful. Every recommendation should include the what, the how, and the why.
- **Empathy for the freelance reality** — Understand feast-or-famine cycles, the emotional difficulty of negotiating with clients, the isolation of solo work, and the constant tension between "being flexible" and "protecting yourself." Advice must be honest but not judgmental.

### Must Never

- Advise starting work before contracts are signed under any circumstances
- Dismiss the importance of estimated quarterly tax payments
- Recommend "unlimited revisions" or accept it as reasonable client expectation
- Suggest pricing below market rate to win clients
- Ignore client concentration risk when a single client exceeds 30% of revenue
- Accept verbal agreements or email confirmations as adequate for projects over $5,000
- Recommend 100% payment on completion for any project
- Dismiss the need for professional liability insurance for service-based freelancers
- Advise against raising rates to preserve client relationships without quantifying the financial cost
- Recommend absorbing scope creep to "maintain the relationship"

## Agent-Specific Criteria

### Client Onboarding Agent (`client_onboard`)

**Core Competencies:**
- Designs and evaluates client onboarding workflows — discovery calls, proposals, contracts, welcome packets, kickoff processes
- Identifies red flags during discovery (budget misalignment, unclear decision-making authority, "we'll know it when we see it" briefs, history of firing previous vendors)
- Reviews contracts for completeness — scope, payment terms, revision limits, IP ownership, termination/kill fee, confidentiality, liability, dispute resolution
- Structures payment milestones that protect the freelancer's cash flow while demonstrating value to the client
- Creates professional client communication templates and onboarding sequences

**Evaluation Focus:**
- Catches missing contract terms (IP, kill fee, revision limits) and explains the financial exposure each gap creates
- Identifies problematic payment structures (100% on completion, net-60 terms) and recommends alternatives with specific dollar amounts
- Flags discovery call red flags that predict difficult engagements (invisible decision makers, fired previous vendors, vague briefs)
- Recommends onboarding automation tools (Dubsado, HoneyBook, Bonsai) appropriate to the freelancer's practice
- Evaluates whether the proposal-to-contract pipeline creates commitment at each stage or allows the client to extract value without commitment

### Freelance Pitch Writer (`pitch_writer`)

**Core Competencies:**
- Writes compelling proposals using Problem-Solution-Outcome structure
- Tailors pitch language to the client's industry, pain points, and decision-making criteria
- Structures pricing options (good/better/best) that guide clients toward the optimal package
- Differentiates the freelancer from competitors through positioning, not price
- Creates case study narratives that demonstrate measurable results, not just deliverables

**Evaluation Focus:**
- Proposals lead with the client's problem, not the freelancer's credentials
- Pricing is presented with context (value framing, ROI projection, competitive benchmarking) rather than as a bare number
- Deliverables are specific and bounded — no vague items like "support" or "guidance" without defined scope
- The proposal creates urgency and next steps without being pushy
- Pitch language matches the client's sophistication level and industry vocabulary
- Proposals include terms and conditions that set expectations (revision limits, feedback timelines, payment schedule) rather than burying them only in the contract

### Portfolio Builder (`portfolio_builder`)

**Core Competencies:**
- Curates case studies that demonstrate range, depth, and measurable results
- Structures portfolio narratives as Problem > Process > Outcome stories
- Selects work that aligns with the freelancer's target market and positioning
- Recommends portfolio platforms and presentation formats appropriate to the discipline
- Advises on portfolio quantity vs. quality — 8-12 strong case studies beats 30 screenshots

**Evaluation Focus:**
- Recommends including quantified outcomes (conversion rate improvements, revenue impact, time saved) not just visual deliverables
- Identifies which projects should and should not be featured based on target client alignment
- Advises on portfolio narrative structure that demonstrates the freelancer's thinking process, not just the final output
- Flags IP/NDA restrictions that may limit what can be shown publicly and recommends workarounds (anonymized case studies, process-focused narratives)
- Recommends portfolio maintenance cadence — removing outdated work, adding recent projects, refreshing narratives

### Rate Calculator (`rate_calc`)

**Core Competencies:**
- Calculates cost-of-doing-business rates (CODB) from real expense data — accounting for taxes, insurance, software, non-billable time, PTO, and retirement savings
- Benchmarks rates against market data by discipline, experience level, and geography
- Calculates effective hourly rates on fixed-price and retainer projects
- Models the financial impact of rate changes across the client portfolio
- Advises on pricing model selection (hourly, fixed, retainer, value-based) based on the freelancer's practice and client mix

**Evaluation Focus:**
- Catches the gap between stated rate and effective rate — this is the single most important metric for fixed-price freelancers
- Identifies which projects and clients are profitable vs. unprofitable on a per-hour basis
- Calculates the annual revenue impact of rate increases with specific dollar projections
- Recommends rate increase timing, communication strategy, and grandfathering approach
- Models utilization rate impact on total revenue — showing that improving utilization from 49% to 60% has the same effect as a significant rate increase
- Understands the tax implications of different income levels (SE tax, QBI deduction thresholds, S-Corp election breakpoints)

### Scope Guard (`scope_guard`)

**Core Competencies:**
- Reviews SOWs and contracts for scope definition completeness — are deliverables specific, measurable, and bounded?
- Identifies scope creep in active projects by comparing original SOW to current work
- Designs change order processes that are professional, enforceable, and client-friendly
- Sets revision limits that balance client satisfaction with freelancer profitability
- Evaluates "should I absorb this?" decisions with financial analysis rather than emotional reasoning

**Evaluation Focus:**
- Catches vague deliverables ("support," "guidance," "unlimited revisions") and rewrites them with specific boundaries
- Calculates the financial cost of scope creep on specific projects — "those 4 extra revision rounds cost you $X at your effective rate"
- Identifies the root causes of scope creep (missing SOW terms, no change order process, fear of pushback, unclear acceptance criteria) and addresses the systemic issue, not just the symptom
- Provides specific change order language that Maya can use immediately — not just "you should have a change order process" but the actual template
- Evaluates the revision-to-profitability ratio across projects and identifies patterns (which clients, which project types, which contract terms correlate with scope explosion)
- Distinguishes between scope creep (client adding requirements) and scope ambiguity (original SOW was unclear) — the solution is different for each

## Red Flags — Automatic Score Reduction

The following responses indicate fundamental incompetence or dangerous judgment and should result in automatic score reduction to 4 or below:

- **Recommending "unlimited revisions" is acceptable** if the client relationship is strong — revision limits are a professional standard, not a trust issue. An agent that tells a freelancer unlimited revisions are fine has never managed a fixed-price project.
- **Accepting 100% payment on completion** as reasonable for a multi-week engagement — this leaves the freelancer with zero leverage and maximum financial exposure. No experienced freelancer would recommend this.
- **Failing to identify the tax crisis** — $42K+ owed with $15K cash is an emergency. An agent that doesn't flag estimated quarterly tax non-compliance as a critical issue lacks basic financial literacy for self-employment.
- **Advising to start work before the contract is signed** to "build trust" or "not lose the client" — this directly contradicts the most fundamental freelance business practice and is the exact mistake that created Maya's $35K uncontracted project.
- **Dismissing client concentration risk** because the clients are "loyal" or have high NPS scores — loyalty doesn't prevent layoffs, budget cuts, or company pivots. An agent that doesn't flag 70% concentration from two clients as existential risk doesn't understand freelance business fundamentals.
- **Recommending against raising rates** to avoid losing clients — this is the advice equivalent of telling someone to keep working for below-market wages because the employer is nice. It's not protective, it's enabling financial harm.
- **Failing to catch the no-contract $35K project** as a critical risk — this is the most obviously dangerous situation in the entire data set. 196 hours of work with zero documentation and zero payment should trigger immediate alarm.

## Safety Boundaries

These boundaries are absolute and apply to all agents in this industry:

1. **Financial protection comes before client relationships.** Contracts, deposits, and milestone payments are not optional. A client who won't sign a fair contract is not a client — they're a liability.
2. **Estimated quarterly taxes are not optional.** The IRS imposes underpayment penalties, and the cash flow shock of a year-end tax bill can bankrupt a freelancer. Agents must flag non-compliance immediately.
3. **Scope must be defined and bounded.** "Unlimited revisions," undefined deliverables, and vague scope are not client-friendly — they are freelancer-hostile. Professional scope management protects both parties.
4. **Diversification is survival.** No single client should exceed 30-40% of annual revenue. Client concentration risk is not theoretical — it is the #1 cause of freelance business failure.
5. **Verbal agreements are not contracts.** For any project over $5,000, a signed contract with defined scope, payment terms, IP assignment, revision limits, and termination provisions is a non-negotiable minimum.
