## PLANTED ISSUES (for evaluation — agent should catch these)

1. **Effective hourly rate destruction on fixed-price projects:** Maya's stated rate is $125/hr, but her weighted average effective hourly rate across all 12 fixed-price projects is approximately $85/hr. On her worst projects (Nexus Dashboard at $62.72/hr and Nexus Mobile App at $70.97/hr), she's earning barely above a junior designer's rate. The root cause is scope creep — 7 and 9 revision rounds respectively with no revision limits in her contract and no change order process. She's working at a premium rate on paper but an entry-level rate in practice. The Nexus projects, which are her largest client, are also her least profitable per hour.

2. **Extreme client concentration risk:** Nexus Technologies represents $72,000 (42.6%) and Bloom Health represents $46,500 (27.5%) of annual revenue. Combined, two clients account for 70.1% of total revenue. If either client churns, Maya faces an immediate 27-43% revenue loss with only $86 hours spent on business development all year (3% of time). Her pipeline is thin — only 4 opportunities, two of which depend on the same two clients continuing. If Nexus's VP of Product (her champion) leaves the company, she could lose 42.6% of her income overnight.

3. **No estimated quarterly taxes paid — $42K+ tax bomb incoming:** Maya has paid $0 in estimated quarterly taxes on $142,170 of net self-employment income. Her total tax liability is approximately $42,571, plus $1,200-1,800 in IRS underpayment penalties. She has $15,200 in total cash ($11,000 business + $4,200 personal). She cannot pay her tax bill. She will need to set up an IRS installment agreement (Form 9465) and will accrue interest and penalties until paid. This is a financial crisis that could have been avoided by setting aside ~$10,600 per quarter.

4. **Catastrophic utilization rate:** Maya works 55.3 hours per week (no vacation in 52 weeks — burnout risk) but only invoices for the equivalent of 27 billable hours per week (48.8% utilization). Industry benchmark for healthy freelance businesses is 60-70% utilization. She's spending 14.3% of her time on unbilled revision work and only 3% on business development. She's overworking and underearning simultaneously — the classic freelance burnout trajectory.

5. **Stale pricing — 3 years without a rate increase:** Maya has charged $125/hr since she started freelancing in 2023. Market rates for mid-career UX designers with her portfolio and experience have increased approximately 20-25% over that period ($150-160/hr is the current Austin market rate for comparable work). At her current revenue of $169K, a 20% rate increase across all projects would represent approximately $33,800 in additional annual revenue — assuming the same volume of work. She has strong NPS scores (8-10 from her best clients) that support a rate increase.

6. **$35,000 project with no contract:** The Nexus Technologies "settings redesign" (Project 12.5) is a $35,000 engagement with no written contract, no SOW, no deposit, no milestone payments, and no invoicing schedule. Maya has already invested 196 hours (worth $24,500 at her stated rate) with $0 collected. Her sole proof of the engagement is a Slack thread and a verbal commitment from a VP. If that VP leaves, or if Nexus disputes the scope/price, Maya has virtually no legal recourse. This single exposure represents 20.7% of her annual revenue. Combined with the 90-day overdue Meridian invoice ($4,500), Maya has $39,500 in at-risk receivables — 23.4% of annual revenue with inadequate or nonexistent documentation.
