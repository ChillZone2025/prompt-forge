## Inclusion Criteria

Patients must meet ALL of the following:

1. Male or female, age 18-75 years inclusive at time of screening
2. Diagnosis of type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) for >= 6 months prior to screening
3. On stable dose of metformin >= 1,500 mg/day (or maximum tolerated dose >= 1,000 mg/day) for >= 8 weeks prior to screening
4. HbA1c >= 7.5% and **<= 12.0%** at screening (central lab)
5. Fasting plasma glucose (FPG) <= 270 mg/dL at screening
6. Body mass index (BMI) >= 25.0 and <= 45.0 kg/m2 at screening
7. Estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) >= 30 mL/min/1.73m2 (CKD-EPI equation) at screening
8. Willing and able to self-administer weekly SC injections (or have a caregiver administer)
9. Females of childbearing potential must use highly effective contraception throughout the study and for 5 half-lives after last dose
10. Able to provide written informed consent and comply with study requirements

## Exclusion Criteria

Patients meeting ANY of the following are excluded:

1. Type 1 diabetes mellitus or history of diabetic ketoacidosis
2. Use of any anti-diabetic medication other than metformin within 12 weeks prior to screening (including insulin, sulfonylureas, SGLT2 inhibitors, DPP-4 inhibitors, thiazolidinediones, or any GLP-1 receptor agonist)
3. History of pancreatitis or active gallbladder disease
4. History of medullary thyroid carcinoma or personal/family history of Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia syndrome type 2 (MEN2)
5. History of major depressive disorder with suicidal ideation or attempt within the past 2 years
6. Significant cardiovascular event (MI, stroke, hospitalization for unstable angina or heart failure) within 6 months prior to screening
7. ALT or AST > 3x upper limit of normal (ULN) at screening
8. Triglycerides > 500 mg/dL at screening
9. Known hypersensitivity to any GLP-1 RA or study excipients
10. Pregnant, breastfeeding, or planning pregnancy during the study period
11. Any condition that, in the investigator's opinion, would compromise patient safety or study integrity

## Safety Monitoring Plan

### Adverse Event Reporting

| Category | Reporting Timeline |
|----------|-------------------|
| Non-serious AEs | Documented at each study visit in the EDC |
| Serious Adverse Events (SAEs) | Within 24 hours of site awareness, reported to sponsor via safety fax/email |
| SUSARs | Sponsor reports to FDA/EMA within 15 calendar days (7 days if fatal/life-threatening) |

### Laboratory Safety Alerts — Automatic Notification to Investigator

| Parameter | Alert Threshold | Action Required |
|-----------|----------------|-----------------|
| HbA1c | **> 10.0%** | Investigator notification; consider rescue therapy |
| Fasting Plasma Glucose | > 300 mg/dL on two consecutive measurements | Investigator notification; rescue therapy per protocol |
| ALT or AST | > 5x ULN | Discontinue study drug; follow until resolution |
| Lipase | > 3x ULN | Evaluate for pancreatitis; discontinue if confirmed |
| Calcitonin | > 50 pg/mL or doubling from baseline | Thyroid evaluation (ultrasound, referral to endocrinology) |
| eGFR | Decrease > 30% from baseline | Investigator notification; evaluate for dehydration/AKI |
| PHQ-9 Total Score | >= 15 (moderately severe) | Referral to mental health professional |
| C-SSRS | Any positive response on ideation or behavior items | Immediate referral to mental health; consider discontinuation |

### Data Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB)

**Composition:** 3 independent members (endocrinologist [chair], biostatistician, cardiologist)

**Meeting Schedule:**
- After 100 patients complete Week 12
- After 300 patients complete Week 26 (pre-specified interim analysis for futility)
- After 600 patients complete Week 52 (prior to database lock)
- Ad hoc meetings at sponsor or DSMB request

**Stopping Rules:**
- Futility: Conditional power < 20% at interim analysis
- Safety: Greater than 2% incidence of confirmed pancreatitis in either active arm; greater than 3x increase in MACE vs. placebo; any unexpected serious safety signal

## Statistical Analysis Plan (Summary)

### Primary Analysis
- **Estimand:** Treatment policy estimand — change from baseline in HbA1c at Week 52, regardless of rescue therapy or treatment discontinuation
- **Method:** **Last Observation Carried Forward (LOCF)** for missing data, with sensitivity analyses using mixed model for repeated measures (MMRM)
- **Primary comparison:** Each GLX-440 dose vs. placebo, tested sequentially (10mg first, then 5mg) to control Type I error
- **Alpha:** Two-sided 0.05, split sequentially (10mg tested at 0.05; if significant, 5mg tested at 0.05)

### Sample Size Justification
- **Assumed treatment difference (HbA1c):** -0.8% (GLX-440 10mg vs. placebo), -0.6% (GLX-440 5mg vs. placebo)
- **Assumed standard deviation:** 1.2%
- **Power:** 90% for 10mg comparison, 80% for 5mg comparison
- **Dropout rate assumed:** 15%
- **Enrollment target:** 900 (300 per arm), yielding **765 evaluable patients** (255 per arm)

### Key Secondary Analyses
- HbA1c < 7.0% responder rate: Cochran-Mantel-Haenszel test stratified by baseline HbA1c and region
- Body weight change: MMRM (not LOCF)
- FPG change: ANCOVA with LOCF
- Multiplicity: Hochberg procedure across key secondary endpoints

### Interim Analysis
- Pre-specified futility analysis at 50% information fraction (after 450 patients reach Week 26)
- Non-binding futility boundary using conditional power approach
- No efficacy interim analysis (to preserve alpha)

## Informed Consent Form — Key Excerpts

### Section 4: Risks and Discomforts

> **Gastrointestinal Effects:** The most common side effects seen in earlier studies of GLX-440 include nausea (reported in 25-35% of patients), vomiting (10-15%), diarrhea (12-18%), and decreased appetite (8-12%). These effects are usually mild to moderate, occur most commonly in the first 4-8 weeks of treatment, and tend to improve over time. In some patients, gastrointestinal side effects may be severe enough to require discontinuation of the study drug.
>
> **Injection Site Reactions:** Mild redness, itching, or swelling at the injection site occurred in approximately 5-8% of patients in earlier studies. These reactions are generally mild and resolve on their own.
>
> **Low Blood Sugar (Hypoglycemia):** When used with metformin alone, the risk of low blood sugar with GLX-440 is low (approximately 2-4%). However, you should be aware of the symptoms of low blood sugar (sweating, shakiness, fast heartbeat, hunger, confusion) and know how to treat it.
>
> **Thyroid Risk:** GLP-1 receptor agonists have been associated with thyroid C-cell tumors in rodent studies. While this has not been confirmed in humans, patients with a personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma or MEN2 syndrome are excluded from this study. Your calcitonin levels will be monitored throughout the study.
>
> **Heart Rate Increase:** A small increase in resting heart rate (2-4 beats per minute) has been observed with GLP-1 receptor agonists. Your heart rate will be monitored at each study visit.
>
> **Pancreatic Risk:** There is an uncertain association between GLP-1 receptor agonists and pancreatitis (inflammation of the pancreas). Symptoms include severe abdominal pain that may radiate to the back, nausea, and vomiting. If you experience these symptoms, contact the study site immediately. Your lipase levels will be monitored.
>
> **Mental Health Effects:** Some weight-loss medications have been associated with mood changes, including depression and suicidal thoughts. While this has not been observed with GLX-440 specifically, your mood will be monitored using questionnaires (PHQ-9 and C-SSRS) at each visit. If you experience persistent sadness, hopelessness, or thoughts of self-harm, contact the study site or seek emergency care immediately.

### Section 5: Benefits

> You may or may not benefit directly from participation in this study. Possible benefits include improved blood sugar control and weight loss. The information learned from this study may help develop better treatments for type 2 diabetes in the future.

### Section 8: Confidentiality

> Your medical records and study data will be kept confidential to the extent permitted by law. Your records may be reviewed by the study sponsor (Galen Therapeutics), the CRO (PharmaVista Clinical Sciences), regulatory authorities (FDA, EMA), and the IRB/IEC. Your name will not appear in any publication resulting from this study. All study data will be stored in compliance with HIPAA and applicable local data protection regulations.

## PLANTED ISSUES (for evaluation — agent should catch these)

1. **Inclusion criteria allow HbA1c up to 12.0% but safety monitoring only alerts at HbA1c > 10.0%:** Inclusion Criterion #4 allows enrollment of patients with HbA1c up to 12.0%. However, the Safety Monitoring Plan's laboratory alert threshold for HbA1c is > 10.0%. This means a patient enrolled with an HbA1c of 11.5% who is randomized to placebo could maintain an HbA1c between 10.1% and 12.0% (or higher, if their diabetes worsens) and trigger an alert, but a patient whose HbA1c stays at or below 10.0% — still severely uncontrolled — would not trigger any safety alert even if they receive no active treatment for 52 weeks. More critically, a placebo patient entering at HbA1c 12.0% is at significant risk for hyperglycemic complications (diabetic ketoacidosis is rare in T2DM but can occur, and persistent severe hyperglycemia causes osmotic symptoms, dehydration, and accelerated microvascular damage). The gap between enrollment ceiling (12.0%) and alert threshold (10.0%) creates a 2-percentage-point zone where severely uncontrolled patients could remain on placebo without triggering any rescue mechanism. A responsible protocol would either: (a) lower the HbA1c inclusion ceiling to 10.5%, (b) lower the alert threshold to match clinical relevance (e.g., > 9.0% with mandatory rescue), or (c) implement a pre-specified rescue therapy trigger at HbA1c > 10.0% regardless of study arm.

2. **Visit 4 fasting blood draw coincides with holiday periods — compliance risk:** Visit 4 (Week 12) requires a fasting blood draw for HbA1c and fasting plasma glucose. The visit window is Day 77-91 (+/- 7 days from Day 84). For the large cohort of patients expected to be randomized during peak enrollment (September-December 2026), Visit 4 falls during either the December holiday period (Christmas/New Year) or the March-April period (Easter/spring break). Many clinical trial sites in EU and Latin America have reduced operations or closures during these periods. Fasting compliance is already a challenge in diabetes trials — patients must fast for 8-10 hours before the blood draw. Holiday-period visits compound this with scheduling difficulties, patient travel, and disrupted routines. The risk is twofold: (a) protocol deviations from out-of-window visits, and (b) non-fasting samples that compromise FPG data integrity. The protocol does not address holiday-period scheduling or provide guidance for sites on managing visit windows that fall during closures. A mitigation would be a protocol-specified extended window for Visit 4 (e.g., +/- 14 days) or explicit guidance that sites may schedule Visit 4 at the early end of the window to avoid holidays.

3. **Statistical analysis plan uses LOCF instead of MMRM as primary method:** The primary analysis uses Last Observation Carried Forward (LOCF) for handling missing data, with MMRM as a sensitivity analysis. This is a significant methodological concern. The FDA (particularly the Office of Biostatistics within CDER) has explicitly and repeatedly discouraged LOCF as the primary analysis method in diabetes trials. The FDA's 2008 guidance "Diabetes Mellitus — Evaluating Cardiovascular Risk in New Antidiabetic Therapies to Treat Type 2 Diabetes" and subsequent communications have stated that MMRM is the preferred approach. LOCF is biased because it assumes the last observed value represents what would have occurred — in a diabetes trial, this means a patient who drops out at Week 12 with an HbA1c reduction is assumed to maintain that reduction through Week 52, which is biologically implausible (HbA1c will revert toward baseline after discontinuing an active drug). The FDA has issued Complete Response Letters (CRLs) to sponsors who used LOCF as the primary method in diabetes trials. If this protocol is submitted as written, the FDA will almost certainly require a protocol amendment to make MMRM the primary analysis and LOCF the sensitivity analysis — potentially delaying the program by 6-12 months. The sponsor's biostatistics team should address this before the protocol is finalized.

4. **Informed consent omits known gallbladder risk from Investigator's Brochure:** The Investigator's Brochure (Edition 5) identifies acute gallbladder events (cholecystitis, cholelithiasis) as an Identified Risk (Section 5.3.2), with 4 events across 828 patients in Phase I/II (0.48% incidence). This is not a theoretical or class-level risk — it is a drug-specific, observed adverse effect. However, the informed consent form's Section 4 (Risks and Discomforts) does not mention gallbladder events anywhere. It discusses GI effects, injection site reactions, hypoglycemia, thyroid risk, pancreatic risk, heart rate increase, and mental health effects — but omits gallbladder disease entirely. This is a violation of ICH E6 R2 Section 4.8.10 (informed consent must include reasonably foreseeable risks) and 21 CFR 50.25(a)(2) (description of reasonably foreseeable risks). An IRB reviewing this consent should catch this omission, but if it passes IRB review unchallenged, every enrolled patient is providing consent without being informed of a known, drug-specific risk that has required surgical intervention (cholecystectomy) in one case. The consent form must be amended to include gallbladder events before any patient is enrolled.

5. **Site budget does not include screen failure costs:** The site budget specifies a $850 payment for the screening visit (V1) but applies this only to patients who proceed to randomization. The budget section "Enrollment Assumptions" notes a target of 10-12 patients per site but does not specify a screen-to-randomization ratio and states screen failure costs are "not addressed." In Phase III diabetes trials with HbA1c entry criteria of 7.5-12.0%, the screen failure rate is typically **30-40%** (common reasons: HbA1c outside range at central lab, eGFR below threshold, incidental findings on screening labs). For a site targeting 12 randomized patients with a 35% screen failure rate, approximately 18-19 patients would need to be screened, generating 6-7 screen failures. At $850 per screening visit (physician time, lab draws, ECG, informed consent process), this represents approximately **$5,100-$5,950 in unreimbursed screening costs per site**. Sites will either: (a) absorb the cost and resent the sponsor (damaging the site relationship and motivation), (b) cut corners on screening (rushing consent, skipping assessments), or (c) decline to participate. Experienced sites will identify this budget gap during the feasibility assessment and request amendment. The budget should include a screen failure payment (typically $400-$600 per screen failure) to cover the site's actual costs.

6. **Primary endpoint analysis powered for population that doesn't match enrollment target:** The sample size calculation assumes 900 enrolled patients with a 15% dropout rate, yielding 765 evaluable patients (255 per arm). The primary analysis uses LOCF, which by definition uses the intent-to-treat (ITT) population — all randomized patients. If LOCF is applied to the full ITT population (N=900), the power calculation is correct. However, if the FDA requires MMRM (as they almost certainly will — see Issue #3), the analysis will be based on observed data only. MMRM is more efficient than LOCF for handling missing data, but its power depends on the actual amount and pattern of missing data. The sample size was calculated assuming LOCF methodology — not MMRM. If the dropout rate exceeds 15% (which is common in 52-week diabetes trials — actual rates in comparable studies like SUSTAIN, PIONEER, and SURPASS trials range from 15-25%), the MMRM analysis may be underpowered. Specifically, if 20% of patients drop out (a realistic scenario), the effective sample size for MMRM could be lower than planned, and the study risks failing its primary endpoint not because the drug doesn't work but because it wasn't adequately powered for the analysis FDA will require. The sponsor should re-power the study under MMRM assumptions with 20-25% dropout scenarios and consider increasing enrollment to 1,000-1,050 patients as a buffer.
