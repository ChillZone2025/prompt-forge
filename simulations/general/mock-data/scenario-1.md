## PLANTED ISSUES

### Issue 1: Inflated Net Revenue Retention (NRR)
**Location:** Section 1, NRR Calculation Detail
**The problem:** The reported NRR of 118% includes $55,000 in one-time implementation fees from 3 enterprise accounts. Implementation fees are non-recurring and should be excluded from NRR calculations. True NRR is approximately 111.5% — still healthy but materially different from the reported figure and below the 115% target.
**What catching it looks like:** Agent identifies that NRR should only include recurring revenue components (subscription expansion, contraction, and churn), not one-time professional services or implementation fees.

### Issue 2: Vanity Metric — Total Signups Without Activation
**Location:** Section 7, User Growth
**The problem:** "Total Signups (cumulative): 8,420" is presented as a headline metric, but the trial-to-paid conversion rate is only 8.5% and declining (from 10.1% a year ago). The ratio of paying accounts (480) to total signups (8,420) is only 5.7%. Total signups is a vanity metric — what matters is activation rate, trial-to-paid conversion, and the trend direction (which is negative).
**What catching it looks like:** Agent flags that total signups is not a meaningful KPI, recommends replacing it with activation rate or qualified trial starts, and notes the declining conversion trend as a strategic concern.

### Issue 3: Pipeline Coverage Doesn't Support Revenue Target
**Location:** Section 2, Pipeline Coverage
**The problem:** Pipeline coverage is 1.5x ($1,809,000 weighted pipeline against $1,200,000 Q1 target). Industry standard for SaaS is 3x coverage minimum, with 2x being a red flag. At current win rates (15-28% depending on segment), the pipeline cannot mathematically produce the target. Back-of-envelope: 108 deals x blended 22% win rate x $40K avg deal = ~$950K — well short of the $1.2M target.
**What catching it looks like:** Agent identifies that 1.5x coverage is critically low, calculates the expected yield from the pipeline using the company's own win rates, and flags the gap between pipeline math and the revenue forecast.

### Issue 4: NPS Survey Has Inadequate Sample Size
**Location:** Section 4, NPS
**The problem:** NPS of 52 is based on only 38 responses out of 480 customers — a 7.9% response rate. With this sample size, the margin of error is approximately ±15 points (at 95% confidence), meaning the true NPS could be anywhere from 37 to 67. The score is statistically unreliable. Additionally, NPS surveys with low response rates tend to have self-selection bias (satisfied customers respond more).
**What catching it looks like:** Agent calculates or flags the margin of error, notes the response rate is far below the 30-40% threshold needed for reliable NPS, and recommends increasing response rates before using NPS as a strategic metric.

### Issue 5: Recruiting Costs Miscategorized
**Location:** Section 6, P&L Snapshot
**The problem:** "Recruiting & Onboarding" is listed as an operating expense at $185,000 (85% over budget of $100,000). However, recruiting costs for revenue-generating roles (sales reps) should be partially attributed to Sales & Marketing CAC calculations. If the 3 new sales reps' recruiting costs (~$60,000 estimated) are reclassified into S&M, the CAC number increases and the LTV:CAC ratio decreases. The current categorization flatters both the S&M budget variance and the CAC metric.
**What catching it looks like:** Agent notes that recruiting costs for revenue-generating roles should be included in CAC calculations, recalculates CAC with the adjustment, and flags that the current P&L categorization obscures true customer acquisition costs.

### Issue 6: Headcount Plan Contradicts Burn Rate / Runway
**Location:** Section 5, Hiring Plan vs. Burn Rate + Section 6, Cash Position
**The problem:** The company has $4.2M cash and 3.6 months of runway at current burn. The plan calls for hiring 14 people in Q1 2026, which would increase monthly burn to $1,355,000. At the new burn rate, runway drops to approximately 3.1 months. The company would need to either raise additional funding by April 2026 or significantly accelerate revenue growth — neither of which is addressed in the QBR. Additionally, engineering velocity is declining (from 62.3 to 54.8 story points/sprint), suggesting the team is already stretched, and adding headcount won't produce immediate productivity gains due to onboarding ramp time.
**What catching it looks like:** Agent performs the runway calculation with the proposed headcount increase, identifies the funding gap, and flags that the hiring plan is unsustainable without either a funding round or a dramatic change in revenue trajectory. Agent also notes that declining engineering velocity suggests an absorption capacity problem, not just a headcount problem.
