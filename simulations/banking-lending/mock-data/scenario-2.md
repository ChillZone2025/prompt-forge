## PLANTED ISSUES (for evaluation — agent should catch these)

### Issue 1 — Structuring Pattern (31 USC §5324)
33 cash deposits over 90 days, every single one below $10,000, totaling $294,700. Average deposit: $8,930. Zero deposits at or above the $10,000 CTR threshold. This is a textbook structuring pattern — deliberately breaking cash deposits into amounts below the reporting threshold to evade Currency Transaction Report filing. Structuring is a federal crime under 31 USC §5324, even if the underlying funds are legitimate. The use of multiple branches reinforces the pattern. A SAR must be filed.

### Issue 2 — OFAC Partial Match Not Resolved
Dmitri Volkov generates a partial OFAC/SDN match — same name, DOB off by one year (1982 vs. 1983), birthplace Moscow, Russia. A one-year DOB discrepancy is within the range of documentation errors, aliases, or deliberate obfuscation. This partial match requires resolution: the bank must gather additional identifying information (birthplace, passport, prior addresses) to confirm or clear the match. The account should NOT have been opened or should have been restricted until the OFAC match was dispositioned. Operating an account for an SDN-listed individual is a strict liability violation under OFAC regulations — no intent required, penalties up to $20M per violation.

### Issue 3 — Business Activity Doesn't Match Account Activity
The application describes "wholesale distribution — electronics, consumer goods, and automotive parts." Wholesale distributors typically receive payments from customers (retailers, other distributors) and pay suppliers. The account shows the opposite pattern: large cash deposits (not typical for wholesale — retailers pay by check, wire, or ACH, not cash) and outgoing wires to foreign entities. Incoming customer payments total only $40,500 vs. $294,700 in cash and $305,500 in outgoing wires. There's no documentation of actual goods being purchased or sold — no invoices, no BOLs, no customs documentation. The stated business model does not match the transaction pattern.

### Issue 4 — Wire Transfers to High-Risk Jurisdictions
Outgoing wires to China ($159,000), UAE ($83,500), and Estonia ($63,000) with vague descriptions ("consulting services," "advisory fees," "PO" references without supporting invoices). Estonia is flagged by FATF and FinCEN as a higher-risk jurisdiction for money laundering. The UAE (particularly free zone entities like "FZE" — Free Zone Establishment) has been cited in multiple FinCEN advisories for trade-based money laundering. "Consulting services" and "advisory fees" from a wholesale electronics company to Estonian and UAE entities have no apparent business rationale.

### Issue 5 — Missing Business Documentation
The account was opened without: Ohio foreign LLC registration (required — Wyoming LLC doing business in Ohio must register with Ohio SOS), business lease (business address may be a virtual office), business license, supplier invoices, product catalogs, or financial statements. A high-risk customer with a newly formed entity and significant expected activity should have been subject to Enhanced Due Diligence BEFORE account opening. The CDD Rule requires the bank to understand the nature and purpose of the customer relationship — this cannot be done without verifying the business actually operates as described.

### Issue 6 — Adverse Media Hit Not Investigated
The adverse media screen for Dmitri Volkov returned a 2023 news article connecting a "Dmitri Volkov" to a money services business shut down by Florida state regulators. The bank's note says "cannot confirm same individual" — but no investigation was conducted. Given the partial OFAC match (Issue 2), the adverse media hit should have triggered enhanced investigation: request passport or prior state IDs, check previous addresses against Florida, ask the customer directly about the article. An uninvestigated adverse media hit on a beneficial owner of a high-risk account is a BSA/AML program deficiency.
