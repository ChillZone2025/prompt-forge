# Industry Context: Architecture & Engineering

## What This Industry Does

Architecture and engineering (A/E) encompasses the design, documentation, and construction administration of buildings and infrastructure. This includes architectural design, civil/structural/mechanical/electrical/plumbing (MEP) engineering, construction documentation, cost estimation, project coordination, and regulatory compliance. Practitioners deliver projects through phases — Schematic Design (SD), Design Development (DD), Construction Documents (CD), and Construction Administration (CA) — producing drawings, specifications, and administrative documents that translate a client's vision into a buildable, code-compliant, and budget-feasible reality.

The industry operates under strict professional licensing requirements, regulatory codes, and contractual frameworks. Errors don't just cost money — they create life-safety hazards, trigger litigation, and end careers.

## Key Tools & Platforms

### Design & Modeling
- **AutoCAD** — 2D drafting and detailing. Still the backbone for civil site plans, utility layouts, and construction details. DWG file format is the industry standard exchange format.
- **Revit (BIM)** — 3D parametric building information modeling. Central file/worksharing model. Families, schedules, and clash detection. Required on most institutional and commercial projects.
- **SketchUp** — Conceptual 3D modeling for early design and client presentations. Fast iteration on massing and spatial relationships.
- **Rhino / Grasshopper** — Freeform 3D modeling with parametric scripting. Used for complex geometry, facades, and computational design workflows.
- **Tekla Structures** — Structural steel and concrete detailing. Produces shop drawings, fabrication models, and erection plans. Interfaces with CNC machines.
- **ETABS / SAP2000 / STAAD.Pro** — Structural analysis and design software. ETABS for buildings, SAP2000 for general structures, STAAD.Pro for steel/concrete design to multiple international codes.

### Construction Documentation & Review
- **Bluebeam Revu** — PDF markup, takeoff, and collaboration for construction documents. Studio Sessions for real-time multi-user review. Layers, custom tool sets, and measurement tools.
- **Navisworks** — Clash detection and 4D scheduling. Aggregates models from multiple disciplines (architectural, structural, MEP) to find conflicts before construction.
- **BIM 360 / Autodesk Build** — Cloud-based project management for BIM workflows. Document management, model coordination, field issues, and RFIs.

### Project Management & Scheduling
- **Procore** — Construction project management platform. RFIs, submittals, change orders, daily logs, punch lists, drawing management. Industry-dominant platform.
- **PlanGrid (Autodesk Build)** — Field-focused document management. Drawings on tablets, photo documentation, punch lists, and task tracking.
- **Primavera P6** — Enterprise project scheduling for large/complex projects. Critical path method (CPM), resource leveling, earned value analysis.
- **Microsoft Project** — Gantt chart scheduling for smaller projects. Baseline tracking and resource assignment.
- **Newforma** — Project information management. Email linking to projects, submittal tracking, RFI management, meeting minutes.
- **Submittal Exchange** — Cloud-based submittal and closeout document management. Submittal logs, review workflows, and document packaging.

### Cost Estimation
- **RSMeans (Gordian)** — Industry-standard cost data. Square-foot models, assembly costs, and unit prices. City cost indexes for location adjustment. Updated annually.
- **PlanSwift** — On-screen digital takeoff and estimating. Measures quantities directly from digital plans.
- **HCSS HeavyBid** — Heavy civil construction estimating. Crew-based estimating with equipment, labor, and material breakdowns.

## Key Frameworks & Methodologies

### Project Delivery Methods
- **Design-Bid-Build (DBB)** — Traditional: architect designs, owner bids, contractor builds. Clear separation of design and construction. Most common for public projects. Risk: adversarial relationships, change orders, lowest-bidder quality issues.
- **Design-Build (DB)** — Single entity designs and builds. Faster delivery, single point of responsibility. Risk: owner has less control over design decisions after contract.
- **CM-at-Risk (CMAR)** — Construction Manager provides a Guaranteed Maximum Price (GMP) during design. Enables early contractor input. Balances owner control with cost certainty.
- **Integrated Project Delivery (IPD)** — Multi-party agreement sharing risk and reward. Architect, owner, and contractor collaborate from day one. Requires high trust and BIM maturity.

### BIM Workflow
- **LOD (Level of Development)** — LOD 100 (conceptual) through LOD 400 (fabrication). Defines what information is in the model at each phase.
- **Clash Detection** — Hard clashes (physical interference), soft clashes (clearance violations), and workflow clashes (scheduling conflicts). Run in Navisworks or Revit.
- **BIM Execution Plan (BEP)** — Defines modeling standards, file naming, LOD requirements, coordination schedule, and responsibilities for each discipline.

### Specification & Classification Systems
- **CSI MasterFormat** — 50-division organizational standard for construction specifications. Division 01 (General Requirements) through Division 49 (Process Integration). Every spec section has a 6-digit number (e.g., 03 30 00 = Cast-in-Place Concrete).
- **Uniformat II** — Building element classification by function rather than product. Used for conceptual estimating (e.g., B10 = Superstructure, D30 = HVAC).
- **AIA Contract Documents** — Standardized contract forms. A101 (Owner-Contractor Agreement), A201 (General Conditions), B101 (Owner-Architect Agreement). Define roles, responsibilities, payment terms, dispute resolution, and change order procedures.

### Design & Construction Phases
- **Schematic Design (SD)** — 15-20% design. Massing, spatial relationships, preliminary code analysis. Client approval of overall concept.
- **Design Development (DD)** — 30-60% design. Major systems defined, structural grid established, MEP coordination begins. Basis of design established.
- **Construction Documents (CD)** — 90-100% design. Fully detailed drawings and specifications for bidding/permitting. All code requirements addressed.
- **Construction Administration (CA)** — Architect's role during construction. RFI responses, submittal review, site visits, change order review, substantial completion inspection, punch list.

### Value Engineering & Constructability
- **Value Engineering (VE)** — Systematic review to reduce cost while maintaining function. Should happen during DD phase, not after bids come in. VE during CA is usually just cost-cutting that degrades quality.
- **Constructability Review** — Contractor reviews design documents for buildability before bidding. Identifies sequencing issues, access problems, and unrealistic details.

## Regulations & Codes

### Building Codes
- **IBC (International Building Code)** — Model code adopted (with local amendments) by most US jurisdictions. Covers occupancy classification, construction type, fire resistance, egress, structural loads, accessibility.
- **Local Building Codes** — Jurisdictions amend the IBC. NYC has its own building code. California uses CBC (California Building Code). Always check the specific adopted edition and local amendments.
- **NFPA (National Fire Protection Association)** — NFPA 101 (Life Safety Code), NFPA 13 (Sprinkler Systems), NFPA 72 (Fire Alarm). Required for fire protection system design.

### Accessibility
- **ADA / ADAAG** — Americans with Disabilities Act and Accessibility Guidelines. Applies to public accommodations and commercial facilities. Covers accessible routes, restrooms, parking, signage.
- **Fair Housing Act (FHA)** — Applies to residential buildings with 4+ units built after March 1991. Requires Type A and Type B accessible units. More stringent than ADA for multifamily housing.
- **ICC/ANSI A117.1** — Technical standard for accessible design referenced by building codes. Defines Type A (fully accessible), Type B (adaptable), and Type C units.

### Safety & Environmental
- **OSHA 29 CFR 1926** — Construction safety standards. Fall protection, scaffolding, excavation, electrical, cranes, hazardous materials. Violations carry significant fines and project shutdowns.
- **ASHRAE 90.1** — Energy standard for commercial buildings. Sets minimum efficiency for HVAC, lighting, building envelope, and power systems. Code-required in most jurisdictions.
- **NEPA / CEQA** — National/California Environmental Policy Act. Environmental impact review for projects receiving federal/state funding or on public land.
- **NPDES / SWPPP** — Stormwater management permits and plans. Required for any construction site disturbing 1+ acres. Erosion control, sediment basins, construction entrance protocols.

### Historic & Zoning
- **Section 106 (NHPA)** — Review process for projects affecting historic properties. Required when federal funding, permits, or licenses are involved.
- **Zoning Ordinances** — Local land use regulations. Setbacks, height limits, lot coverage, parking requirements, use restrictions. Variances require public hearing.

## Industry Terminology

- **RFI (Request for Information)** — Formal question from contractor to architect/engineer seeking clarification on drawings or specifications. Contractually tracked with required response timelines.
- **RFP (Request for Proposal)** — Solicitation document for professional services or construction bids.
- **Submittal** — Product data, shop drawings, samples, or test reports submitted by contractor for architect review. Action: Approved, Approved as Noted, Revise and Resubmit, Rejected.
- **Shop Drawing** — Contractor-produced detailed drawings showing fabrication and installation specifics. More detailed than construction documents.
- **Change Order (CO)** — Formal modification to the construction contract scope, cost, or time. Requires owner signature.
- **Punch List** — List of incomplete or deficient work items identified near project completion. Must be resolved before final payment.
- **Substantial Completion** — Date when the project is sufficiently complete for the owner to occupy and use for its intended purpose. Triggers warranty periods and final retainage release timeline.
- **Certificate of Occupancy (CO)** — Permit from AHJ (Authority Having Jurisdiction) authorizing building occupancy. Requires passing all inspections.
- **AHJ (Authority Having Jurisdiction)** — The building official, fire marshal, or other regulatory body with enforcement authority.
- **As-Built** — Drawings updated to reflect actual field conditions and changes made during construction. Required for closeout.
- **Spec Section** — A specific section within the project manual organized by CSI MasterFormat number. Contains Part 1 (General), Part 2 (Products), Part 3 (Execution).
- **NIC (Not in Contract)** — Work shown on drawings for coordination but excluded from the current contract scope.
- **VIF (Verify in Field)** — Instruction to contractor to confirm existing conditions before fabrication or installation.
- **EOR (Engineer of Record)** — Licensed engineer responsible for the design and sealed drawings of a specific discipline.
- **Addendum** — Document modifying the bid documents before bid opening. Becomes part of the contract documents.
- **Bid Alternate** — Optional scope item priced separately. Owner can accept or reject after reviewing base bid.
- **Allowance** — Budget placeholder in the contract for undefined scope. Owner pays actual cost; difference adjusts via change order.
- **Contingency** — Funds reserved for unforeseen conditions. Design contingency (owner's budget), construction contingency (contract), and owner's project contingency (soft costs).
- **Retainage** — Percentage (typically 5-10%) withheld from each payment as security for completion. Released at or after substantial completion.
- **Liquidated Damages (LDs)** — Pre-agreed daily rate charged to contractor for late completion. Must reflect actual estimated damages, not penalties.
- **Performance Bond** — Surety bond guaranteeing contractor completion. Typically 100% of contract value. Required on most public projects.
- **GMP (Guaranteed Maximum Price)** — Maximum contract amount in CMAR delivery. Savings below GMP may be shared; overruns are contractor's risk.
- **LEED (Leadership in Energy and Environmental Design)** — Green building certification system by USGBC. Certified, Silver, Gold, Platinum levels. Points-based across categories.
- **Commissioning (Cx)** — Systematic process verifying that building systems perform per design intent. Required by many codes and LEED. Includes functional performance testing.

## Common Mistakes (What Goes Wrong)

1. **Incomplete interdisciplinary coordination** — MEP ductwork clashing with structural beams, plumbing routes conflicting with shear walls, electrical panels blocked by architectural partitions. Costs $10K-$500K+ in field rework per clash missed in coordination.

2. **Underestimating project costs by omitting contingency** — At 60% DD, design contingency should be 10-15%. At 100% CD, 5-7%. Projects that carry 3% contingency at DD phase will overrun budget 90% of the time.

3. **Failing to respond to RFIs within contractual timeline** — Most contracts require 7-10 day responses. Unanswered RFIs become delay claims. A single critical-path RFI can generate $50K+/day in delay damages.

4. **Specification sections conflicting with drawings** — Specs call for one product; drawings detail a different one. Contractor builds per whichever is cheaper, then files a change order when told to redo it.

5. **Not documenting field conditions** — Existing conditions differ from survey/record drawings. Without photographic documentation and written reports, disputes become he-said-she-said.

6. **Ignoring code changes between design start and permitting** — A project designed under IBC 2018 may need to comply with IBC 2021 by the time it reaches permit review. New energy code or accessibility requirements can trigger significant redesign.

7. **Inadequate site investigation** — Subsurface conditions (rock, groundwater, contaminated soil) discovered during excavation that weren't identified in the geotechnical report. Can add 10-20% to foundation costs.

8. **Missing ADA/Fair Housing compliance in renovations** — Renovation projects trigger accessibility upgrade requirements based on scope and cost thresholds. Missing these means failed inspections and expensive retrofits.

9. **Not tracking change order schedule impacts** — Every change order has both a cost and a time impact. Approving costs without addressing schedule creates ambiguous delay liability.

10. **Failing to conduct constructability review before bidding** — Contractors find problems during bidding that should have been caught in design. Results in RFI storms, inflated bids (risk pricing), or unrealistically low bids from contractors who missed the issues.

## Excellent vs. Dangerous Work

### What Excellent Work Looks Like
- Cost estimates with line-item traceability to spec sections, unit costs benchmarked against RSMeans with city cost index adjustments, and escalation factors reflecting current market conditions
- RFI responses that reference specific drawing sheets, spec sections, and applicable code provisions — with sketches or revised details when needed
- Submittal reviews that verify compliance with both the specification and the applicable code, not just rubber-stamping
- Site reports that include geo-tagged photos, reference specific drawing details, and document deviations from design with proposed resolutions
- Spec reviews that cross-reference drawings for consistency, verify code edition alignment, and flag coordination gaps between divisions
- Proactive identification of issues during design that would become expensive change orders during construction

### What Dangerous Work Looks Like
- Rubber-stamping submittals without verifying code compliance or spec conformance — transfers liability to the reviewer
- Accepting cost estimates without questioning missing scope, unrealistic contingencies, or suspiciously low bids
- Approving material substitutions without engineering review because the contractor says it's "equivalent"
- Responding to RFIs with "per plans and specs" when the plans and specs are ambiguous or contradictory
- Ignoring life safety code requirements (fire ratings, egress, accessibility) to save money or avoid redesign
- Not documenting field conditions and relying on verbal agreements with contractors

## Relevant Certifications & Licenses

- **PE (Professional Engineer)** — State license required to seal engineering drawings. Requires accredited degree, FE exam, 4 years experience, PE exam. Each state requires separate licensure or reciprocity.
- **RA / AIA (Registered Architect)** — State license required to seal architectural drawings. Requires accredited degree (NAAB), AXP experience (3,740 hours), ARE exam (6 divisions). AIA is a professional membership, not a license.
- **LEED AP (Leadership in Energy and Environmental Design Accredited Professional)** — Demonstrates green building expertise. Specialties: BD+C (Building Design & Construction), O+M (Operations & Maintenance), ID+C (Interior Design), ND (Neighborhood Development).
- **PMP (Project Management Professional)** — PMI certification. Common for project managers and owners' representatives in construction.
- **CCM (Certified Construction Manager)** — CMAA certification for construction management professionals. Requires experience and exam.
- **CPC (Certified Professional Constructor)** — AIC certification. Demonstrates construction management competency through exam and experience.
- **OSHA 30 (30-Hour Construction Safety)** — Required for construction site supervisors in many jurisdictions. Covers OSHA standards, hazard recognition, and safety planning.
- **WELL AP** — Demonstrates expertise in WELL Building Standard for health and wellness in buildings.
- **Envision SP (Sustainability Professional)** — ISI certification for sustainable infrastructure design. Rating system for civil infrastructure similar to LEED for buildings.
