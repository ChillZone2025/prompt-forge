## PLANTED ISSUES (for evaluation — agent should catch these)

### Issue 1: General Conditions Severely Underpriced
Bridgetown Builders shows General Conditions at $612,000 (6% of hard costs). The other two bidders are at $1,180,000 (10.3%) and $1,050,000 (9.3%). Industry standard for a 14-month, 65,000 SF mixed-use project is 10-14% of hard costs. At 6%, the GC is either absorbing these costs to win the bid (unsustainable — they'll recover through change orders) or has omitted critical general conditions items like project management staff, temporary facilities, insurance, and cleanup. This should be investigated with a line-item breakdown of what's included in their general conditions.

### Issue 2: No Escalation Factor on a 14-Month Schedule
Construction materials have been escalating 3-5% annually. A 14-month construction schedule starting May 2026 means materials purchased in early 2027 will cost more than today's prices. With $10.1M in hard costs, a 4% escalation on materials purchased in the back half of the project could add $200K-$300K. Neither the GC's estimate nor the owner's budget includes an escalation line item. This will surface as budget pressure during construction.

### Issue 3: HVAC Bid 30% Below Competitors — Scope Gap
Bridgetown's HVAC number ($485,000) is 30-32% below the other two bids ($710,000 and $690,000). The HVAC scope summary reveals why: AirFlow Mechanical excluded ductwork insulation ("by others") and controls/BAS ("to be provided under Division 25"). But there is no Division 25 line item in the estimate. The actual HVAC cost with insulation and controls would likely be $680,000-$720,000, in line with competitors. This scope gap will become a change order of $200K+ during construction.

### Issue 4: 3% Contingency at 60% Design Development — Dangerously Low
The design is at 60% DD with a 3% contingency ($303,960). Industry standard contingency at 60% DD is 10-15%. The design is less than two-thirds complete — significant decisions about finishes, MEP systems, structural connections, and building envelope details haven't been made. At 100% CD, 5% contingency would be appropriate. At 60% DD, the estimate should carry at least $1M-$1.5M in design contingency. This budget will overrun.

### Issue 5: No Site Work / Earthwork Line Item — Groundwater Risk Ignored
There is no Division 31 (Earthwork) or Division 32 (Exterior Improvements) line item despite the project requiring excavation to 14 feet below grade for parking, with groundwater at 12 feet. Dewatering, shoring for adjacent buildings, soil export, and backfill are not priced. Given the groundwater conditions, dewatering alone could cost $150,000-$300,000. Shoring for adjacent buildings adds another $200,000-$400,000. Combined site work/earthwork is likely a $400,000-$700,000 gap in the estimate.

### Issue 6: ADA/Fair Housing Accessibility Costs Not Itemized
The project includes 40 residential units in a new building, which triggers Fair Housing Act requirements. Per FHA, all ground-floor units and all units in buildings with elevators must meet Type B accessibility requirements (ICC/ANSI A117.1). This building has an elevator, so all 40 units must be Type B accessible. Type A units may also be required by Oregon state code. Accessible unit layouts affect door widths, bathroom clearances, kitchen layouts, and blocking for grab bars. None of these accessibility requirements are itemized in the estimate, and the cost impact on finishes, doors/hardware, and millwork could be $80,000-$150,000. More importantly, missing these requirements means failed inspections and expensive retrofits after drywall is closed.
