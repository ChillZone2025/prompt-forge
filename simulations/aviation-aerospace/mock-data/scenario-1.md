## PLANTED ISSUES (for evaluation — agent should catch these)

### Issue 1 — Overdue Airworthiness Directive (AD 2023-08-22)
The elevator feel shift module AD has a compliance deadline of 03/15/2025. The current date is March 2026 — the AD is **one year overdue**. This is a serious airworthiness violation. The aircraft has been operating for 12 months in non-compliance with a mandatory AD. This must be accomplished before return to service. Additionally, the operator must conduct a records review to determine how this was missed, self-disclose to the FAA (voluntary disclosure under AC 00-58B is the recommended path to mitigate enforcement action), and implement corrective action in their AD tracking system to prevent recurrence. Operating an aircraft not in compliance with applicable ADs violates 14 CFR §91.403(a) and §91.7.

### Issue 2 — HPT Blade Life Limit Documentation Gap (AD 2024-12-15)
Engine #2's HPT Stage 1 blade records are incomplete. The life limit AD requires tracking individual blade cycles (CSN). The engine logbook says "new HPT Stage 1 blades installed" at 12,400 engine CSN but does not record:
- Individual blade serial numbers (required for life-limited parts per 14 CFR §43.10)
- 8130-3 tag numbers for the new blades (traceability)
- The blade part number 1862M68P03 life limit vs. individual blade CSN

Without individual blade serial numbers and installation records, the blades cannot be confirmed as within life limits. The agent must flag this as a potential life-limited part compliance issue and require documentation verification before return to service. If the records cannot be reconstructed, the blades must be assumed at engine CSN (22,100) and would be past the 20,000 CSN life limit — requiring immediate removal.

### Issue 3 — Unsigned Logbook Entry (RII Task)
The logbook entry for task C4-002 (wing lower skin inspection, dated 03/16/2026) has a blank "Inspected by" field. Wing lower skin detailed inspection (eddy current) is a Required Inspection Item (RII) under Meridian Air's CAMP. RII tasks require dual inspection — the performing mechanic and an independent RII-qualified inspector must both sign. The missing inspector signature means the task is not properly documented as complete, and the work cannot be accepted for return to service. Per 14 CFR §121.371(a), each person performing a required inspection item must be authorized to do so, and the inspection must be documented with a signature.

### Issue 4 — Self-Inspection on Aft Bulkhead Fasteners
The logbook entry for NR-003 (aft bulkhead fastener replacement, dated 03/18/2026) shows T. Williams as both the performing mechanic AND the inspector. While §43.3 allows an A&P to approve return to service for the work they perform (for non-RII items), Meridian Air's quality manual likely requires independent inspection for structural repairs on the pressure vessel. Aft pressure bulkhead fastener replacement is a structural task on the primary pressure boundary — most Part 121 operators classify this as an RII or at minimum require independent inspection per their approved quality system. The agent should flag this for review against Meridian's RII list and quality procedures.

### Issue 5 — Missing 8130-3 for Aft Bulkhead Fasteners
WO-4402 shows NAS1580-4 fasteners sourced from "AOG Supplies Inc." with "No 8130-3" listed. While standard hardware (AN, NAS, MS) from approved distributors may not always require individual 8130-3 tags, the parts must come from an FAA-approved source with traceability to the original manufacturer. "AOG Supplies Inc." is not identified as a known distributor — the agent should verify this is an FAA-authorized distributor and that conformity documentation (certificate of conformity, test reports, or 8130-3) exists. Installing parts without proper traceability documentation on a Part 121 aircraft violates §43.13 and creates an unapproved parts risk. Unapproved parts are a top FAA enforcement priority (AC 21-29D).

### Issue 6 — Engine #2 Vibration Trend
Engine #2 shows N1 vibration increasing from 0.6 to 1.4 IPS and N2 from 0.8 to 1.5 IPS over 90 days, accompanied by a sudden increase in oil consumption (0.06 to 0.12 qt/hr). This combination — rising vibration plus increasing oil consumption — indicates a potential mechanical deterioration (bearing wear, blade damage, or seal failure). While the vibration levels are not yet at the AMM limit for removal (typically 4.0-5.0 IPS), the **rate of change** is the concern. CFM SB 72-0770 and the operator's reliability program should trigger enhanced monitoring or borescope inspection. The agent should recommend this engine be borescoped before return to service given the trend, especially in combination with the HPT blade documentation gap (Issue 2). Not monitoring this trend could lead to an in-flight shutdown or uncontained engine failure.
