## PLANTED ISSUES (for evaluation — agent should catch these)

### Issue 1 — Captain Exceeds Flight Duty Period Limit
Captain Torres reports at 0545 EDT. Per Part 117 Table B, a flightcrew member reporting between 0500-0559 with 2 planned flight segments has a maximum FDP of 12 hours. The planned FDP for the round-trip (MA447 + MA448) ends at 1845 EDT = 13 hours 00 minutes total FDP. This exceeds the 12-hour limit by 1 hour. The flight cannot be dispatched as planned unless:
- The second leg is reassigned to a different crew
- The schedule is adjusted to reduce total FDP to within limits
- A Part 117.19 FDP extension is invoked (requires favorable conditions, pre-approval, and is limited to 2 hours max — but the captain must concur and conditions must support it)

The dispatcher's release note says "crew legal per Part 117" — this is incorrect. The agent must catch that the planned FDP exceeds limits. This is a Part 117 violation if the flights operate as scheduled.

### Issue 2 — Fuel Planning Uses Wrong Regulatory Standard
The fuel plan calculates reserves as "alternate fuel + 30 minute final reserve at 1,500 ft." This is the EASA/ICAO fuel planning methodology, NOT the FAA Part 121 domestic requirement. Per 14 CFR §121.639 (domestic operations), the fuel requirement is: fuel to destination **plus 45 minutes at normal cruising fuel consumption** (no alternate fuel required for domestic, but alternate must be listed if weather is below certain minimums per §121.619). The current plan may be adequate in total quantity but uses the wrong regulatory framework for the calculation. If the alternate fuel amount happens to make up the difference, it works mathematically but the documentation methodology is wrong — and a POI audit of the dispatch release would cite this.

Correct Part 121 domestic calculation:
- Trip fuel: 22,060 lbs
- Reserve: 45 minutes at normal cruise (~5,400 lbs for 737-800)
- Taxi: 807 lbs
- Total minimum: ~28,267 lbs
The planned 29,590 lbs exceeds this minimum, but the dispatcher must use the correct regulatory framework.

### Issue 3 — Alternate Airport May Become Unavailable
The alternate (KCOS) TAF shows: FM221400 32020G38KT 5SM BLSN BKN050, with TEMPO 2216/2222 33025G48KT 2SM BLSN OVC030. The planned arrival at DEN is around 0905 MDT (1505Z). If a diversion to KCOS is needed, it would occur around 1500-1600Z — exactly when the TAF forecasts gusts to 48 knots and visibility dropping to 2 SM in blowing snow with a 3,000 ft ceiling. Additionally, NOTAM FDC 6/3012 makes the ILS RWY 35R approach NA when visibility is below 1 mile. If conditions deteriorate to 2SM BLSN per the TEMPO, the alternate remains technically available (vis > 1SM), but the trend is deteriorating and could drop below minimums. The agent should flag this as an alternate adequacy concern and recommend monitoring or selecting a more robust alternate (e.g., KPUB Pueblo, KAPA Centennial, or KABQ Albuquerque).

### Issue 4 — MEL 26-01-03 Fire Detection Loop B — Dispatch Restriction
MEL item 26-01-03 (Engine #1 fire detection loop B inoperative) is Category A — which means it has specific dispatch conditions that must be met. The typical MEL condition for a single fire detection loop inoperative allows dispatch for **one flight only to a maintenance base** where repairs can be made. The planned day includes TWO legs (MA447 ATL→DEN and MA448 DEN→ATL). If Tulsa is the maintenance base, neither Denver is the repair destination. The agent should verify:
- Is DEN a maintenance base where this MEL can be cleared?
- If not, the aircraft cannot legally operate the second leg (MA448) — the MEL authorizes one flight to a maintenance base, not continued revenue service.
- If DEN has no 737 maintenance capability, the aircraft must be ferried to a maintenance base or the repair must be accomplished in DEN before the return flight.

### Issue 5 — NOTAM: ILS 34L Glidepath Out of Service at KDEN
NOTAM FDC 6/2847 shows the ILS RWY 34L glidepath is OTS, leaving only the localizer approach available. With the forecast winds at DEN (320° at 20G35, later 25G45), Runway 34L is the primary landing runway (nearly direct headwind). The localizer-only approach has higher minimums than the full ILS (typically 1,000-1,200 ft ceiling and 2-3 SM visibility vs. 200 ft / 1/2 SM for ILS). With the MEL weather radar Captain's side inoperative and conditions forecasting blowing snow with reduced visibility, the agent should:
- Verify the LOC-only minimums are adequate for the forecast conditions
- Consider whether the weather radar MEL restriction ("avoid flight into known areas of thunderstorm activity") combined with the SIGMET creates additional risk
- Note that if DEN goes below LOC-only minimums, the flight must divert — and the alternate (KCOS) is also forecast to deteriorate

### Issue 6 — Presidential TFR Affecting Return Leg
NOTAM FDC 6/2901 establishes a TFR around KATL from 1300-1700 EDT on March 22. The return flight MA448 departs DEN at 1345 MDT (1545 EDT) and arrives ATL at 1845 EDT. The TFR expires at 1700 EDT, so the arrival at 1845 is clear. However, if the return leg departs early or if the schedule changes, the TFR could affect the KATL arrival. More importantly, if there are delays and the crew needs to operate a third leg or if the schedule is adjusted to address the FDP issue (Issue 1), the TFR timing must be factored into any alternative plan. The agent should note the TFR and confirm it doesn't affect the planned operation — and flag it as a constraint for any schedule changes.
