## Section 1: CEO Letter to the Board

Dear Board Members,

Q1 2026 was a quarter of meaningful progress for CloudMetrics. I'm pleased to share highlights from the period.

**Product:** We launched CloudMetrics Insights 3.0, our next-generation analytics dashboard, on February 12. Early adoption metrics show 34% of existing enterprise customers have activated the new features within 6 weeks of launch. The product team executed well against an aggressive timeline.

**Team:** We completed 28 new hires across engineering and go-to-market functions. Our engineering team now stands at 145, up from 117 at year-end. Head of APAC Sales, Priya Nandakumar, joined from Datadog in February and is already building pipeline in Singapore and Tokyo.

**Partnerships:** We signed a co-selling agreement with AWS that gives us access to their enterprise sales team for joint motions in financial services. Early pipeline from the partnership is $4.2M in qualified opportunities.

**Customer Activity:** Our net logo count increased to 842 from 810 at year-end. We saw continued strength in financial services and healthcare verticals. Unfortunately, we lost two enterprise customers during the quarter -- National Federal Bank ($2.1M ARR) and Ridgeline Health Systems ($1.1M ARR). National Federal consolidated to a competitor platform as part of a broader vendor rationalization, and Ridgeline's budget was cut following a change in CFO. Our customer success team has documented lessons learned from both losses and is implementing enhanced early-warning indicators.

**Looking Ahead:** We are presenting a proposal for APAC market expansion at this meeting and request board approval for a $20M investment. Details are in Section 6.

Respectfully,
Sarah Chen, CEO

## PLANTED ISSUES (for evaluation -- agent should catch these)

1. **Misleading OKR status indicators:** Three of five key results under Objective 1 are marked "ON TRACK" (green) despite the actual numbers showing they are significantly behind target. KR1 (net new ARR) is at 80% of target with the quarter already over -- that's a miss, not "on track." KR2 (enterprise logos) hit 9 of 15 (60%) -- that's clearly RED/off track. KR3 (net revenue retention) is 112% vs. 120% target -- an 8-point gap that's worsening quarter-over-quarter. The color coding is actively misleading the board about performance. Additionally, KR3 under Objective 3 (Adjusted EBITDA positive for 2 consecutive quarters) shows 1 of 2 quarters achieved and is marked green, but this is the final quarter of the OKR period -- it's mathematically impossible to achieve 2 consecutive quarters if you only have 1. A competent board prep agent must flag that the OKR scorecard is painting a false picture of health.

2. **Adjusted EBITDA excludes stock-based compensation:** The financial dashboard prominently features "Adjusted EBITDA" of $1.8M positive, but the footnote reveals it excludes $4.2M in stock-based compensation. On a GAAP basis, the company has an operating loss of $5.1M. For a pre-IPO company, presenting adjusted metrics to the board without equal prominence for GAAP figures is problematic -- the SEC requires GAAP reconciliation in S-1 filings, and boards need to evaluate the real economic picture. A $4.2M quarterly SBC exclusion is material, especially when it's growing ($2.9M to $3.8M to $4.2M over three quarters). The board should understand that "profitability" is an accounting construct, not a cash flow reality -- the cash burn of $5.7M/quarter tells the real story.

3. **Stale risk register:** The risk register was last updated September 2025 -- six months ago. It completely misses: (a) the loss of two enterprise customers worth $3.2M combined ARR (customer concentration risk materialized and isn't reflected), (b) the AI/ML feature accuracy problem (Project Copilot at 78% vs. 95% target -- a product risk that could affect competitive positioning), (c) the declining net revenue retention trend (121% to 118% to 112% over three quarters -- a deteriorating unit economics risk), (d) the deteriorating burn multiple (1.3x to 1.8x -- capital efficiency risk for a pre-IPO company), and (e) the VP of Investor Relations hire that hasn't closed (execution risk for IPO timeline). A September risk register presented to an April board is governance malpractice.

4. **APAC expansion resolution lacks decision criteria:** The board is being asked to approve $20M with no success criteria, no milestone-based funding gates, no kill criteria, and no decision to revisit. What happens if Phase 1 delivers $0 in pipeline after 6 months? The resolution authorizes "up to $20,000,000" as a single approval rather than stage-gating the investment. Professional board governance would require: (a) specific milestones that trigger Phase 2 and Phase 3 funding, (b) defined kill criteria (e.g., "if Phase 1 pipeline < $2M, board review required before Phase 2"), (c) quarterly progress reporting to the board, and (d) a named executive accountable for APAC P&L. The financial projections ($3M/$8M/$18M ARR) have no underlying assumptions, comparable company benchmarks, or sensitivity analysis.

5. **CEO letter buries negative news:** The loss of two enterprise customers worth $3.2M combined ARR ($2.1M + $1.1M) is buried in paragraph 4 of the CEO letter, after the product launch, hiring updates, and partnership news. This represents 4% of total ARR lost in a single quarter. For a pre-IPO company, the board needs this information upfront. The Minto Pyramid Principle (and basic board communication best practice) dictates that material negative developments should be disclosed early and prominently, not buried under positive news. The letter also fails to quantify the ARR impact in the paragraph where the losses are mentioned -- a reader has to do the math themselves. The framing ("unfortunately") minimizes what could signal a churn trend.

6. **No executive session on the agenda:** The board meeting agenda does not include an executive session (closed session for independent directors without management present). NYSE and NASDAQ listing rules require regular executive sessions for listed companies, and pre-IPO companies preparing for listing should already be practicing this governance standard. Executive sessions allow independent directors to discuss CEO performance, compensation, succession planning, and any concerns about management candor -- exactly the kind of oversight needed when the CEO letter and OKR scorecard have the transparency issues described above. The absence of an executive session is a governance gap that any experienced board director or governance professional should flag.
