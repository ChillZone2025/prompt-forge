# Industry Context: Executive & Leadership

## What This Industry Does

Executive and leadership advisory encompasses C-suite decision-making, board governance, strategic planning, and organizational transformation. Practitioners include chief of staff professionals, executive coaches, board advisors, management consultants, corporate secretaries, and governance professionals.

The work spans board meeting preparation and governance, executive decision framing, OKR design and tracking, strategic communications to boards and stakeholders, and executive briefing for time-constrained leaders. At the core, this field translates complexity into clarity so that leaders with limited bandwidth can make high-stakes decisions with confidence.

## Key Frameworks & Methodologies

### Objectives & Key Results (OKR)
- **Doerr OKR methodology** -- Objectives are qualitative and inspirational. Key Results are quantitative and measurable. 3-5 objectives per cycle, 2-5 key results per objective. Scored 0.0-1.0, where 0.7 is success on aspirational OKRs.
- **Commit vs. Aspirational OKRs** -- Commits are must-hit targets (1.0 expected). Aspirational OKRs stretch the organization (0.7 is strong). Confusing these is the #1 OKR failure mode.
- **Cascading vs. Aligning** -- Cascading (top-down dictation) fails. Alignment (bidirectional negotiation where teams set their own KRs that ladder up) succeeds. Andy Grove's original Intel model was alignment, not cascade.

### Decision Frameworks
- **RAPID (Bain & Company)** -- Recommend, Agree, Perform, Input, Decide. Clarifies decision roles to prevent consensus paralysis. The "D" (Decide) is one person, never a committee.
- **Cynefin (Dave Snowden)** -- Categorizes decisions into Clear, Complicated, Complex, and Chaotic domains. Each domain requires a different leadership approach: sense-categorize-respond vs. probe-sense-respond.
- **OODA Loop (Boyd)** -- Observe, Orient, Decide, Act. Military decision framework applied to competitive strategy. Speed through the loop is the competitive advantage.
- **Eisenhower Matrix** -- Urgent/Important 2x2 grid for executive time management and delegation. Quadrant 2 (important, not urgent) is where strategic work lives.
- **First Principles Thinking** -- Decomposing problems to fundamental truths rather than reasoning by analogy. Prevents "we've always done it this way" executive decision traps.
- **Pre-mortem Analysis (Gary Klein)** -- Assume the decision has failed. Work backward to identify what caused failure. More effective than risk registers because it leverages prospective hindsight.
- **Red Team / Blue Team** -- Designating a team to challenge the recommendation (Red) while another defends it (Blue). Prevents groupthink in executive committees.
- **After Action Review (AAR)** -- Structured post-decision review: What was supposed to happen? What actually happened? Why the gap? What will we do differently? Originated in US Army, adopted by McKinsey and Bridgewater.

### Organizational Design
- **McKinsey 7S Framework** -- Strategy, Structure, Systems, Shared Values, Skills, Style, Staff. Used for organizational alignment during transformation. All 7 elements must be coherent.
- **RACI Matrix** -- Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, Informed. Clarifies ownership for cross-functional initiatives. Only one "A" per decision.
- **Balanced Scorecard (Kaplan & Norton)** -- Four perspectives: Financial, Customer, Internal Process, Learning & Growth. Translates strategy into measurable objectives across all four.

### Board Governance
- **Consent Agenda** -- Routine board items bundled for single vote (minutes approval, committee reports, standard resolutions) to reserve discussion time for strategic matters.
- **Executive Session** -- Board meeting segment without management present. Independent directors discuss CEO performance, succession, and sensitive governance matters. Required by NYSE and NASDAQ listing rules.
- **Board Evaluation** -- Annual assessment of board effectiveness, individual director contributions, and committee performance. NACD best practice since 2003.

## Regulations & Compliance Frameworks

### Securities & Governance
- **SEC Reporting Requirements** -- 10-K (annual), 10-Q (quarterly), 8-K (material events), proxy statements (DEF 14A). Board members have personal liability for material misstatements.
- **Sarbanes-Oxley Act (SOX)** -- Section 302 (CEO/CFO certification of financial statements), Section 404 (internal controls assessment), Section 906 (criminal penalties for false certification). Applies to all US public companies.
- **Regulation FD (Fair Disclosure)** -- Prohibits selective disclosure of material nonpublic information. If a CEO tells one analyst, they must tell all investors simultaneously. Board members bound by same rules.
- **Proxy Statement Rules (DEF 14A)** -- Requires disclosure of executive compensation, director qualifications, related-party transactions, and audit committee reports. Say-on-pay votes mandatory since Dodd-Frank.

### Fiduciary Standards
- **Fiduciary Duty of Care** -- Directors must act with the care a reasonably prudent person would use. Requires informed decision-making (reading board materials, attending meetings, asking questions).
- **Fiduciary Duty of Loyalty** -- Directors must act in the corporation's best interest, not their own. Requires disclosure of conflicts and recusal from conflicted votes.
- **Business Judgment Rule** -- Courts presume directors acted in good faith if they were informed, disinterested, and rational. Protects directors who followed proper process even if the outcome was bad.
- **D&O Insurance** -- Directors and Officers liability insurance. Covers defense costs and settlements for shareholder lawsuits, SEC investigations, and derivative actions.

### ESG Reporting
- **GRI (Global Reporting Initiative)** -- Sustainability reporting standards. Requires materiality assessment and stakeholder engagement.
- **SASB (Sustainability Accounting Standards Board)** -- Industry-specific ESG disclosure standards. Now merged into IFRS Foundation (ISSB).
- **TCFD (Task Force on Climate-related Financial Disclosures)** -- Governance, strategy, risk management, and metrics/targets for climate risk. Mandatory in UK, EU; expected in US via SEC climate rule.

## Common Mistakes in This Field

1. **Confusing outputs with outcomes in OKRs** -- "Launch product X" is an output (activity). "Increase customer retention to 92%" is an outcome (result). OKRs built on outputs become task lists, not strategic instruments. The entire point of Key Results is measurable impact, not deliverable completion.

2. **Burying the lead in board presentations** -- Board members have 30-60 minutes of attention per topic. Starting with background context and building to the ask means the ask gets rushed or skipped. Lead with the decision needed, then provide supporting analysis. The Minto Pyramid Principle (answer first, then evidence) is the gold standard.

3. **Failing to quantify strategic options** -- Presenting "Option A: Expand to APAC" without estimated investment, projected return, timeline, probability-weighted outcomes, and kill criteria. Boards cannot make informed decisions on narrative alone. Every strategic option needs a financial model, even if rough.

4. **Ignoring stakeholder alignment** -- Launching an initiative that the CEO supports but the CFO hasn't reviewed, the CHRO hasn't staffed, and the board hasn't been briefed. Cross-functional alignment must happen before the board vote, not after.

5. **Cascading objectives without context** -- Telling a team "your OKR is to increase NPS by 10 points" without explaining why, how it connects to the company objective, or what resources are available. Context-free cascading produces compliance, not commitment.

6. **Treating board prep as admin, not strategy** -- Assembling a board package by collecting slides from department heads and stapling them together. Professional board prep curates, challenges, and frames information for governance decisions. The board package is a strategic document, not a status report.

7. **Presenting problems without a decision framework** -- Bringing a problem to the executive team without structuring the options, criteria, and recommendation. Executives don't have time to frame problems -- that's the chief of staff's job. Every problem should arrive as "here are 3 options, here's my recommendation, here's what I need from you."

8. **Overloading executives with detail** -- A 60-page board package when 15 pages would suffice. A 20-slide deck when 5 would land the point. Executive communication is about compression, not completeness. The appendix exists for supporting detail.

9. **Neglecting succession planning** -- Operating without a documented plan for CEO, CFO, and key executive succession. Boards are required to have succession plans, and activist investors target companies without them. This is a governance failure, not an HR task.

10. **Communicating in jargon to non-domain boards** -- A CTO presenting infrastructure risks in technical language to a board of finance and legal professionals. Executive communication must translate domain expertise into business impact language that any intelligent non-specialist can act on.

## Excellent vs. Dangerous Work

| Dimension | Excellent Work | Dangerous Work |
|-----------|---------------|----------------|
| **Board packages** | Curated narrative with decision frameworks, quantified options, risk registers, and clear asks. Board leaves informed and decisive. | Slide dump from department heads with no narrative thread. Board leaves confused, rubber-stamps out of fatigue. |
| **OKR design** | Outcome-based key results with measurable targets, owner accountability, and clear connection to strategy. Teams are motivated by purpose. | Output-based task lists disguised as OKRs. Teams game metrics or ignore them entirely. No strategic alignment. |
| **Executive briefs** | One-page synthesis with clear recommendation, supporting evidence, risks, and next steps. CEO can decide in 5 minutes. | Three-page email with buried recommendation, missing context, and no decision framework. CEO forwards it and nothing happens. |
| **Decision framing** | Structured options analysis with weighted criteria, financial models, scenario analysis, and explicit trade-offs. Decision is defensible. | Single-option recommendation with confirmation bias, missing alternatives, no financial model, and no kill criteria. Decision is fragile. |
| **Strategic comms** | Audience-adapted messaging with Minto Pyramid structure, data visualization, and clear call to action. Stakeholders align and act. | One-size-fits-all messaging with buried conclusions, wall-of-text format, and no call to action. Stakeholders skim and forget. |
| **Risk management** | Living risk register with probability/impact scoring, mitigation plans, owners, and regular review cadence. Board sees emerging risks early. | Static risk register last updated 6 months ago. Risks are generic ("competitive pressure") with no scoring or mitigation. Board is surprised by preventable crises. |
| **Governance** | Executive sessions scheduled, committee charters current, conflict-of-interest disclosures on file, succession plan documented. Fiduciary duties met. | No executive sessions, stale committee charters, undisclosed conflicts, no succession plan. Board exposes itself to liability. |

## Certifications & Credentials

### Board Governance
- **NACD Board Leadership Fellow** -- National Association of Corporate Directors. Requires ongoing governance education. The standard credential for US corporate board service.
- **ICD.D (Institute of Corporate Directors Designation)** -- Canadian board governance certification. Covers fiduciary duties, strategy oversight, risk governance, and board effectiveness.
- **Certified Board Advisor (CBA)** -- Advisory credential for professionals who support boards without serving as directors. Covers governance best practices and board dynamics.

### Executive Education
- **Harvard Business School (HBS)** -- Advanced Management Program (AMP), Owner/President Management (OPM), and executive education in corporate governance and strategy.
- **Wharton Executive Education** -- Programs in strategic leadership, board governance, and organizational transformation.
- **INSEAD** -- International Directors Programme (IDP) for board governance. Strong in cross-border governance and global board composition.
- **Stanford GSB** -- Executive Program in Strategy and Organization. Corporate governance and leadership programs.

### Executive Coaching & Leadership
- **ICF (International Coaching Federation)** -- Three levels: ACC (Associate), PCC (Professional), MCC (Master Certified Coach). PCC requires 500+ coaching hours; MCC requires 2,500+.
- **Marshall Goldsmith Stakeholder Centered Coaching** -- Executive coaching methodology focused on behavioral change with stakeholder feedback loops.
- **Center for Creative Leadership (CCL)** -- Leadership assessment and development programs. 360-degree feedback instruments (Benchmarks suite).

### Strategy & Management Consulting
- **CMC (Certified Management Consultant)** -- International credential requiring consulting experience, education, peer review, and adherence to ethical standards.
- **Balanced Scorecard Certified Professional** -- Palladium Group certification for implementing Kaplan & Norton's strategic management system.
