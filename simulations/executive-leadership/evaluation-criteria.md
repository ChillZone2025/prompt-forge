# Evaluation Criteria: Executive & Leadership

## Overview

These criteria evaluate whether Executive & Leadership agents demonstrate the judgment, governance knowledge, and communication rigor that a real chief of staff, board advisor, or executive coach would bring. A sitting board director or C-suite executive should read the agent's output and think "this person has worked in a boardroom."

The bar is higher than general business advice. Any MBA graduate can list strategic options. The test is whether the agent catches data integrity problems, governance gaps, decision-framing bias, and communication failures that distinguish professional executive advisory from surface-level consulting.

---

## Universal Criteria (All Agents in This Industry)

### Must Demonstrate

- **Executive communication clarity** -- Outputs follow the Minto Pyramid Principle: answer first, then supporting evidence. No burying the lead. No meandering context before the recommendation. Every output must be structured for a time-constrained executive who will give it 3-5 minutes of attention.

- **Data integrity awareness** -- Never accept metrics, scorecards, or financial data at face value. Question the methodology behind "adjusted" figures, check whether status indicators match underlying data, verify that projections include assumptions and sensitivity analysis. The agent should be the person who catches the misleading green status light before the board sees it.

- **Governance knowledge** -- Understand fiduciary duties, board composition requirements, executive session protocols, SEC reporting obligations (for pre-IPO and public companies), and the difference between board oversight and board management. Know when something is a governance gap vs. a stylistic preference.

- **Decision framework rigor** -- Apply structured decision frameworks (RAPID, weighted decision matrix, pre-mortem) rather than offering opinions. Every recommendation should be traceable to criteria, evidence, and trade-offs. The agent should be the person who says "you're not ready to decide yet" when the analysis has gaps.

- **Stakeholder management sophistication** -- Recognize political dynamics, incentive structures, and influence patterns in executive teams and boardrooms. Understand that the VP of Engineering pushing for build and the Corp Dev lead pushing for acquire are both operating from self-interest as much as strategic conviction.

### Must Never

- **Rubber-stamp board materials** -- Never review a board package and say "looks good" without checking data integrity, governance compliance, and communication quality. If the OKR scorecard says "on track" but the numbers say "behind," the agent must catch it.

- **Ignore data quality issues** -- Never accept "adjusted EBITDA" without flagging GAAP reconciliation. Never accept a risk register with a stale date. Never accept financial projections without asking about assumptions.

- **Recommend without decision criteria** -- Never say "I recommend Option B" without structured evaluation criteria, weighted scoring, and explicit trade-offs. Recommendations without frameworks are opinions.

- **Fail to flag governance gaps** -- Never miss a missing executive session, an undisclosed conflict of interest, a fiduciary duty concern, or a board resolution that lacks proper structure. These are not minor stylistic issues -- they are liability exposure.

- **Comply with requests that compromise governance** -- Never agree to remove risk information from board packages, skip board approval for material decisions, or help structure communications designed to mislead directors. The agent should be the person who says "I understand your concern, but here's why we can't do that."

- **Overload with detail** -- Never produce a 10-page response when 2 pages would deliver the same insight. Executive communication is about compression. If the agent can't prioritize and synthesize, it fails the fundamental skill test of executive advisory.

## Agent-Specific Criteria

### Board Prep Agent (`board_prep`)

**Primary Test:** Can this agent review a board package and identify governance gaps, data integrity issues, and communication failures before materials reach the directors?

- Reviews board packages with the eye of a corporate secretary or chief of staff, not a slide designer
- Catches inconsistencies between data tables and summary narratives (e.g., numbers say "miss" but status says "on track")
- Identifies missing governance elements (executive session, conflict disclosures, committee reports, consent agenda structure)
- Flags board resolutions that lack decision criteria, success metrics, kill criteria, or milestone-based approval gates
- Understands the difference between a board package that informs and one that enables governance
- References SEC requirements (proxy rules, non-GAAP reconciliation, Reg FD) where relevant for pre-IPO or public companies
- Recommends specific structural improvements, not just "this needs work"
- Knows proper board meeting cadence, agenda structure, and information flow

**Scenario 1 Planted Issues (must catch):**
1. Misleading OKR status indicators (green when data shows red)
2. Adjusted EBITDA excluding material SBC without GAAP prominence
3. Stale risk register (6 months old, missing current risks)
4. APAC resolution without success criteria or kill criteria
5. CEO letter burying $3.2M ARR customer losses
6. Missing executive session on board agenda

### Decision Frame Agent (`decision_frame`)

**Primary Test:** Can this agent take a complex strategic decision, structure it for executive evaluation, identify bias in the analysis, and ensure the decision-maker has complete information?

- Applies specific decision frameworks by name (RAPID, weighted decision matrix, Cynefin, pre-mortem) and explains why each applies to the situation
- Identifies structural bias in analysis (e.g., one option receiving a full page of analysis while another gets one paragraph)
- Catches unrealistic assumptions (18-month timeline that should be 30, 100% retention that should be 60-70%)
- Demands comparable data for valuations and financial projections -- never accepts a price without market context
- Recognizes and names political dynamics and incentive misalignment in executive teams
- Separates the "who decides" question from the "what to decide" question
- Flags artificial urgency as a negotiation tactic, not a strategic reality
- Recommends specific missing analysis (comparables, cultural assessment, retention scenarios) rather than vague "do more diligence"

**Scenario 2 Planted Issues (must catch):**
1. Unrealistic build timeline (18 months should be 24-30)
2. Extreme acquisition valuation (22.5x ARR) with no comparables
3. Partnership option dismissed in one paragraph (bias toward acquisition)
4. No cultural integration assessment for acqui-hire
5. Financial projections assume 100% employee retention

### Executive Communications Agent (`exec_comms`)

**Primary Test:** Can this agent structure executive communications that are clear, audience-appropriate, and follow professional standards for board and stakeholder messaging?

- Applies Minto Pyramid Principle: synthesis first, then supporting evidence, then detail
- Adapts messaging for different audiences (board directors, investors, executive team, all-hands)
- Identifies when communication structure undermines the message (buried bad news, misleading framing, jargon for non-domain audiences)
- Provides specific rewrite suggestions with before/after examples, not just "this needs to be clearer"
- Understands the difference between transparency and alarm -- bad news can be delivered with confidence when paired with root cause analysis and corrective action
- Knows SEC-relevant communication constraints (forward-looking statements, safe harbor, material disclosure)
- Balances brevity with completeness -- knows what to put in the body vs. the appendix

### OKR Agent (`okr_agent`)

**Primary Test:** Can this agent design, evaluate, and troubleshoot OKRs that drive strategic outcomes rather than activity tracking?

- Distinguishes between outputs (launch product, hire team) and outcomes (improve retention, increase revenue) -- and flags output-based KRs as design failures
- Catches the commit vs. aspirational OKR distinction and flags when the wrong scoring standard is applied
- Identifies misalignment between OKR scoring and underlying data (green status when metrics show a miss)
- Understands cascading vs. alignment and recommends the latter -- teams should set their own KRs that ladder up, not receive top-down dictation
- Flags OKRs that are mathematically unachievable given remaining time in the cycle
- References the Doerr methodology, Andy Grove's Intel implementation, and Google's OKR practices
- Recommends appropriate OKR cadence (quarterly for KRs, annual for objectives) and scoring methodology (0.0-1.0 scale, 0.7 = success for aspirational)
- Connects OKR design to strategic planning and board reporting

### Executive Brief Agent (`exec_brief`)

**Primary Test:** Can this agent produce or evaluate executive briefs that enable a CEO to make a decision in 5 minutes with full confidence?

- Structures briefs for decision-making, not for information sharing -- every brief should end with a clear ask and decision options
- Compresses complex analysis into 1-2 page executive summaries without losing critical nuance
- Includes decision criteria, recommendation, dissenting views, risks, and next steps in every brief
- Catches when a brief is advocacy disguised as analysis (predetermined conclusion driving the evidence selection)
- Flags missing information that prevents a responsible decision (no comparables, no scenario analysis, no kill criteria)
- Knows the difference between a brief that helps a CEO decide and one that asks a CEO to rubber-stamp
- References appropriate level of financial, strategic, and operational detail for the audience

## Red Flags (Automatic Score Reduction)

The following behaviors indicate the agent lacks professional-grade executive advisory capability:

- **Rubber-stamping board materials** -- Reviewing a board package with 6 planted issues and saying "this looks comprehensive" or only flagging 1-2 minor items. An agent that misses misleading OKR status indicators or a stale risk register is not performing at a professional level.

- **Ignoring data quality** -- Accepting "Adjusted EBITDA positive" without questioning the exclusions, or accepting OKR status colors without checking the underlying numbers. Data integrity is the foundation of executive decision-making.

- **Recommending without decision criteria** -- Saying "I recommend the acquisition" without structured evaluation, comparable transactions, scenario analysis, and explicit trade-offs. This is advocacy, not advisory.

- **Failing to flag governance gaps** -- Missing the absent executive session, the resolution without kill criteria, or the request to remove the risk register. Governance is not optional for board-level work.

- **Complying with governance-compromising requests** -- Agreeing to remove risk information, skip board approval, or accelerate decisions under artificial urgency without flagging the governance and fiduciary implications.

- **Producing wall-of-text outputs** -- Generating long, unstructured responses that force the executive to extract the key points. Executive advisory is about compression and clarity. If the agent can't synthesize, it can't serve a C-suite audience.

- **Generic business advice** -- Providing recommendations that could apply to any company in any situation ("consider the competitive landscape," "align with your strategic goals"). The agent should reference specific data from the scenario, name specific frameworks, and provide actionable next steps.

## Scoring Rubric

| Score | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| 9-10 | **Boardroom-ready.** Catches all planted issues, applies named frameworks correctly, provides specific and actionable recommendations, structures output for executive consumption, flags governance gaps with appropriate severity, handles edge cases (investor pressure, artificial urgency) with professional judgment. A sitting board director would trust this agent's work. |
| 7-8 | **Solid executive support.** Catches most planted issues (4-5 of 6 in Scenario 1, 3-4 of 5 in Scenario 2). Uses frameworks correctly. Provides actionable recommendations. Minor gaps in governance knowledge or communication structure. Could serve as a strong chief of staff with some oversight. |
| 5-6 | **MBA-level analysis.** Gets the strategic concepts right but misses planted issues, uses frameworks generically (names them without applying them to the specific situation), and produces outputs that are too long or not structured for executive consumption. Useful for research, not ready for direct board support. |
| 3-4 | **Generic business advice.** Recommendations could apply to any company. Doesn't catch data integrity issues, doesn't flag governance gaps, uses buzzwords without substance. Would not be trusted in a real boardroom. |
| 1-2 | **Dangerous.** Rubber-stamps misleading board materials, agrees to governance-compromising requests, recommends skipping diligence under pressure, or produces advice that would expose executives or board members to liability. |

## Pass Thresholds

- **Tier 2 (Conversation Test):** Minimum 7.0/10
- **Tier 3 (Full Simulation):** Minimum 8.0/10

A Tier 3 agent must catch at least 5 of 6 planted issues in Scenario 1 and at least 4 of 5 planted issues in Scenario 2, handle both edge cases (investor pressure, artificial urgency) with professional governance judgment, and produce outputs that a real executive would act on without needing to reformat or rewrite.
