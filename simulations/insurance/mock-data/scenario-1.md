## Loss Details

### Fire Incident Description
At approximately 5:15 AM on March 15, 2026, a fire broke out in the northeast section of the 48,000 sq. ft. warehouse at 2740 Industrial Parkway, Stockton, CA. The Stockton Fire Department responded at 5:28 AM and contained the fire by 7:45 AM. The fire originated in the electrical distribution panel area serving the east wing loading docks.

### Cause and Origin
- **Cause:** Electrical — preliminary fire marshal report indicates arc fault in recently installed 400-amp distribution panel
- **Origin:** Electrical room, northeast corner of building
- **Contributing Factor:** Sprinkler system in the east wing (Zone 3) failed to activate. Heads in Zones 1, 2, and 4 activated normally and prevented spread to the western half of the building.
- **Recent Work:** Pacific Distribution contracted Apex Electrical Solutions to upgrade the electrical system in the east wing, completed February 28, 2026 (15 days before fire). Work included new 400-amp panel, new wiring to 12 loading dock circuits, and LED lighting conversion.

### Sprinkler System Status
- **System Type:** Wet pipe, full building coverage, 4 zones
- **Zone 3 (East Wing) Status:** Failed to activate. Post-fire inspection by Guardian Fire Protection revealed corroded valve in Zone 3 riser was stuck in a partially closed position.
- **Last Documented Inspection:** March 2024 (by Pacific Fire Systems, now defunct)
- **Policy Requirement:** Annual inspection by licensed contractor — **no inspection certificate on file for 2025**
- **Note from Broker File:** Daniel Fong emailed the insured on September 15, 2025, reminding them to complete annual sprinkler inspection. No response documented.

## PLANTED ISSUES (for evaluation — agent should catch these)

1. **Coinsurance penalty exposure:** The building is insured for $2,000,000 but the contractor estimates and market analysis suggest actual replacement cost is approximately $2,800,000. With an 80% coinsurance clause, the required insurance amount is $2,240,000 ($2,800,000 x 80%). The insured is carrying only $2,000,000 — a coinsurance deficiency. The penalty formula: (Amount Carried / Amount Required) x Loss = Recovery. So: ($2,000,000 / $2,240,000) x $1,200,000 = $1,071,429 — a penalty of approximately $128,571 on the building claim. The insured will not recover the full $1,200,000 building loss.

2. **Sprinkler inspection non-compliance:** The policy's Fire Protection Equipment Endorsement requires annual inspection certification by a licensed fire protection contractor. The last documented inspection was March 2024 — nearly two years ago. The broker sent a reminder in September 2025 with no response. The carrier may have grounds to deny the portion of the loss attributable to the sprinkler system failure in Zone 3, or potentially argue that the fire damage would have been significantly less had the sprinkler system been properly maintained. This is a coverage defense, not an automatic denial — but it must be investigated and documented.

3. **Business income calculation error:** The policyholder's CPA calculated BI using gross revenue ($1,250,000/month x 6 months = $7,500,000). The correct methodology under ISO business income forms is Net Income + Continuing Normal Operating Expenses. If Pacific Distribution operates on a 12% net margin (typical for distribution), net income is approximately $150,000/month. Adding continuing expenses (rent, insurance, loan payments, salaries for retained employees) might bring the monthly BI figure to $400,000-$500,000 — but the gross revenue approach overstates the loss by roughly 40%. The adjuster flagged this but hasn't corrected the calculation yet.

4. **Personal property mixed with business contents:** The contents claim includes $53,000 in personal property belonging to the owner (artwork, golf clubs, family photos, personal laptop, memorabilia). A commercial property policy (BOP) covers "business personal property" — the owner's personal items stored at the business premises are not covered unless a specific endorsement applies. This $53,000 should be separated from the contents claim and the policyholder should be advised to file under their homeowners or personal articles floater if they have one.

5. **Subrogation evidence at risk:** The fire originated in electrical work performed by Apex Electrical Solutions just 15 days before the fire. The fire marshal's report points to the new 400-amp panel as the probable cause. However, no preservation of evidence letter has been sent to Apex, the fire scene has been released, and the building owner is already discussing cleanup with a contractor. If the damaged panel and wiring are removed or disturbed, the carrier loses its strongest evidence for subrogation recovery. A spoliation notice and independent forensic electrical examination must happen immediately.

6. **72-hour waiting period not applied:** The Business Income coverage includes a 72-hour waiting period, meaning the first 72 hours of business interruption are not covered. The BI calculation submitted by the policyholder's CPA does not account for this deduction. At $1,250,000 gross monthly revenue (or approximately $41,667/day), the 72-hour deduction represents approximately $125,000 in gross terms (or roughly $15,000-$20,000 in net BI terms). The waiting period must be applied before any BI payment is calculated.
