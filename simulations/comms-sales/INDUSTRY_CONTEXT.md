# Industry Context: Comms & Sales

## What This Industry Does

Communications and Sales encompasses the full revenue generation and market engagement lifecycle for organizations — from identifying prospects and qualifying leads through closing deals, retaining customers, and managing public perception. It spans several distinct but interconnected disciplines: sales development (prospecting, outreach, pipeline management), account management (relationship building, expansion, renewal), communications and PR (media relations, brand reputation, crisis management), content marketing (asset creation, distribution, repurposing), and customer success (onboarding, health monitoring, churn prevention).

The core function is converting market attention into revenue and then retaining that revenue through ongoing relationship management. In B2B contexts, deal cycles range from weeks (SMB self-serve) to 18+ months (enterprise procurement). In B2C, the emphasis shifts to volume, automation, and lifecycle marketing. Across both, the discipline requires balancing personalization at scale with compliance constraints — particularly around unsolicited outreach, data privacy, and truthful representation.

The industry's defining challenge is signal-to-noise ratio. Buyers receive hundreds of outreach messages per week, journalists receive thousands of pitches per month, and customers are constantly evaluating whether to renew or churn. Success depends on relevance, timing, and credibility — not volume. The best practitioners treat every touchpoint as a trust transaction. The worst treat every contact as a target to be sprayed with templated messages until something sticks.

## Key Tools & Platforms

### CRM & Pipeline Management
- **Salesforce** — The dominant CRM platform, used by ~150,000 companies globally. Sales Cloud manages contacts, accounts, opportunities, and pipeline stages. Lightning Experience provides customizable dashboards and reports. Salesforce CPQ (Configure, Price, Quote) handles complex pricing and proposal generation. Einstein AI provides lead scoring and forecasting. The ecosystem includes AppExchange integrations, Flow automation, and SOQL for custom reporting.
- **HubSpot** — All-in-one CRM, marketing, sales, and service platform. Free CRM tier drives adoption. Sales Hub includes sequences (automated email follow-ups), meeting scheduling, deal pipelines, and playbooks. Marketing Hub handles landing pages, forms, email marketing, and attribution reporting. Widely adopted by SMBs and mid-market companies.
- **Pipedrive** — Sales-focused CRM with visual pipeline management. Designed for sales teams who find Salesforce too complex. Strong in deal tracking, activity reminders, and email integration.

### Sales Engagement & Outreach
- **Outreach.io** — Sales engagement platform for creating, managing, and optimizing multi-step outreach sequences across email, phone, and LinkedIn. Provides A/B testing on messaging, task automation, and analytics on open rates, reply rates, and conversion by sequence stage. The industry standard for enterprise SDR teams.
- **Apollo.io** — Combined prospecting database and engagement platform. Provides contact data (emails, phone numbers, job titles) with engagement sequences. Offers intent data signals showing which companies are researching relevant topics. Popular with growth-stage startups for its all-in-one approach.
- **Salesloft** — Sales engagement platform competing with Outreach. Rhythm AI engine prioritizes seller actions based on buyer signals. Cadence management for multi-touch sequences. Strong in enterprise sales team coordination.
- **LinkedIn Sales Navigator** — Premium LinkedIn tool for prospecting. Advanced search filters (company size, industry, seniority, recent job changes). InMail messaging to contacts outside your network. Lead and account recommendations based on activity patterns. TeamLink shows warm introduction paths through colleagues' connections.

### Conversation Intelligence
- **Gong** — Revenue intelligence platform that records, transcribes, and analyzes sales calls and meetings. Identifies talk-to-listen ratios, competitor mentions, pricing discussions, objection patterns, and next steps. Deal intelligence aggregates conversation data to forecast deal outcomes. Coaching features compare top performer patterns against the broader team.
- **Chorus (ZoomInfo)** — Conversation intelligence platform with call recording, transcription, and AI-powered analysis. Tracks key moments (pricing mentioned, competitor discussed, next steps agreed). Now part of ZoomInfo's unified go-to-market platform.
- **Clari** — Revenue operations platform focused on pipeline inspection and forecasting. Uses engagement data, CRM activity, and conversation signals to predict deal outcomes. Executive dashboards show forecast accuracy and pipeline coverage.

### Data & Intelligence
- **ZoomInfo** — B2B contact and company database with 100M+ professional profiles. Provides firmographic data (revenue, employee count, technology stack), intent signals (companies researching specific topics), and organizational charts. Integrates with CRMs and engagement platforms for enrichment and prospecting.
- **6sense** — Account-based orchestration platform using intent data to identify accounts in-market for specific solutions. Revenue AI predicts buying stage (awareness, consideration, decision) and recommends engagement actions. Integrates with advertising, email, and sales engagement platforms.
- **Bombora** — Intent data provider that tracks B2B content consumption across a cooperative of 5,000+ websites. Company Surge scores indicate when an organization is researching topics at above-baseline rates. Used to prioritize outbound efforts toward accounts showing active interest.

### Email Marketing & Automation
- **Mailchimp** — Email marketing platform widely used for newsletters, drip campaigns, and transactional emails. Audience segmentation, A/B testing, automation workflows, and landing pages. Standard for small business email marketing.
- **Klaviyo** — E-commerce email and SMS marketing platform. Deep integrations with Shopify, BigCommerce, and WooCommerce. Behavioral triggers (abandoned cart, post-purchase, browse abandonment). Revenue attribution per campaign and flow.
- **Marketo (Adobe)** — Enterprise marketing automation platform. Lead scoring, nurture campaigns, account-based marketing, and attribution modeling. Integrates deeply with Salesforce. Used by B2B enterprise marketing teams.

### PR & Media
- **Cision** — The largest media database and PR distribution platform. Contains journalist profiles, outlet information, and editorial calendars. Press release distribution via PR Newswire. Media monitoring tracks brand mentions across print, broadcast, and online media.
- **Meltwater** — Media intelligence platform combining media monitoring, social listening, and influencer identification. Tracks brand mentions, sentiment, share of voice, and competitive media coverage. Used by corporate communications and PR agencies.
- **Muck Rack** — Journalist database and media relations management. Tracks journalist beats, recent articles, and social media activity. Pitching tools with open/click tracking. Media monitoring with automated news alerts.
- **Prowly** — PR workflow tool for media database access, press release creation, online newsrooms, and media monitoring. Part of Semrush. Strong for mid-market PR teams.

### Social Media Management
- **Hootsuite** — Social media management platform for scheduling, publishing, and monitoring across multiple networks. Content calendar, team approval workflows, social listening, and analytics dashboards. Supports Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn, X (Twitter), YouTube, TikTok, and Pinterest.
- **Sprout Social** — Social media management with emphasis on engagement and analytics. Smart Inbox aggregates messages from all platforms. Social listening tracks brand mentions and industry conversations. Reporting includes audience growth, engagement rates, and content performance.
- **Buffer** — Simplified social media scheduling and analytics. Popular with small teams and individual creators for its clean interface and straightforward scheduling workflow.

### Customer Success
- **Gainsight** — The leading customer success platform. Health scores aggregate product usage, support ticket volume, NPS responses, and engagement metrics to predict churn risk. Customer journey orchestration automates touchpoints based on lifecycle stage. Playbooks guide CSMs through expansion and renewal workflows.
- **Intercom** — Customer messaging platform combining live chat, chatbots, product tours, and help center. Resolution Bot handles common questions automatically. Inbox manages conversations across email, chat, and social. Used for both support and proactive customer engagement.
- **ChurnZero** — Customer success platform focused on churn prediction and prevention. Real-time usage alerts, automated playbooks, and customer health scoring. Integrates with CRM and billing systems for complete account visibility.
- **Totango** — Customer success platform with SuccessBLOCs (pre-built programs for onboarding, adoption, renewal, and expansion). Health scoring, journey orchestration, and executive dashboards.

## Key Frameworks & Methodologies

### Sales Qualification Frameworks
- **MEDDIC (Metrics, Economic Buyer, Decision Criteria, Decision Process, Identify Pain, Champion)** — The gold standard for enterprise deal qualification. Forces reps to identify who controls the budget (Economic Buyer), who will internally advocate (Champion), what success looks like in measurable terms (Metrics), and how the organization makes purchasing decisions (Decision Process). Deals that lack a Champion or where the Economic Buyer is unidentified have dramatically lower close rates.
- **BANT (Budget, Authority, Need, Timeline)** — Classic qualification framework. Still widely used for initial lead qualification but considered insufficient for complex enterprise deals. Budget may not be allocated yet, Authority may be distributed across a committee, and Timeline often shifts. Best used as a starting filter, not a closing framework.
- **SPICED (Situation, Pain, Impact, Critical Event, Decision)** — Newer framework emphasizing the prospect's current state and the consequences of inaction. The Critical Event is the forcing function — the deadline, contract expiration, or business event that creates urgency. Deals without a Critical Event tend to stall.

### Sales Methodologies
- **Challenger Sale** — Based on CEB (now Gartner) research. The Challenger rep teaches prospects something new about their business, tailors messaging to the stakeholder's priorities, and takes control of the commercial conversation. Emphasizes delivering insight over building rapport. The "Commercial Teaching" pitch reframes the prospect's understanding of their problem.
- **SPIN Selling (Situation, Problem, Implication, Need-Payoff)** — Neil Rackham's research-based methodology for complex sales. Situation questions establish context, Problem questions surface difficulties, Implication questions expand the problem's impact, and Need-Payoff questions guide the prospect to articulate the value of a solution. The sequence is designed to have the buyer discover the urgency rather than being told it.
- **Sandler Selling System** — Reverses the traditional sales dynamic. The rep qualifies out early if the deal isn't a fit. "Up-front contracts" set expectations for every meeting. The buyer must articulate budget and decision process before the rep presents a solution. Pain Funnel questions dig beneath surface-level complaints to identify root causes.
- **Solution Selling** — Focuses on the buyer's pain points rather than product features. Discovery creates a "pain chain" connecting operational problems to business impact. The solution is presented as a custom response to the diagnosed pain, not a product demo. Widely used in enterprise technology sales.
- **SNAP Selling (Simple, iNvaluable, Aligned, Priority)** — Jill Konrath's framework for selling to overwhelmed buyers. Keep it Simple (remove complexity from the buying process), be iNvaluable (provide unique insight), Align with buyer's core priorities, and raise the Priority (connect to urgent business initiatives).

### Account-Based Marketing (ABM)
- **ABM** — A strategic approach where marketing and sales jointly identify target accounts, develop account-specific engagement plans, and coordinate multi-channel outreach. Tiers: 1:1 ABM (custom campaigns for individual strategic accounts), 1:few ABM (tailored campaigns for 5-20 similar accounts), and 1:many ABM (programmatic personalization at scale using intent data and dynamic content). Platforms like 6sense, Demandbase, and Terminus enable ABM execution.

### Customer Success Frameworks
- **Customer Health Scoring** — Composite metric combining product usage frequency, feature adoption breadth, support ticket volume and sentiment, NPS/CSAT scores, billing status, and executive engagement. Scores are typically red/yellow/green or 0-100. A declining health score triggers proactive outreach — the goal is to detect churn risk 60-90 days before renewal.
- **Customer Maturity Model** — Stages: Onboarding (Time to First Value), Adoption (core feature usage), Expansion (cross-sell/upsell), Advocacy (referrals, case studies, speaking engagements). Each stage has different success metrics and CSM playbooks.
- **Outcome-Based Success** — Ties customer success activities to the business outcomes the customer bought to achieve, not product features. The CSM becomes a business consultant who happens to deliver value through the product.

### Communication & Content Frameworks
- **AIDA (Attention, Interest, Desire, Action)** — Classic copywriting framework. The subject line or headline grabs Attention, the opening creates Interest, the body builds Desire through benefits and social proof, and the CTA drives Action. Applies to emails, landing pages, ad copy, and sales presentations.
- **PAS (Problem, Agitation, Solution)** — Copywriting framework that opens by naming the prospect's problem, agitates the pain by describing consequences of inaction, then presents the solution. More aggressive than AIDA. Effective in cold outreach where the prospect hasn't acknowledged a problem yet.
- **BAB (Before, After, Bridge)** — Paints the prospect's current state (Before), describes the desired future state (After), and positions the product as the Bridge between them. Concise and effective for short-form copy and email sequences.
- **StoryBrand (Donald Miller)** — Positions the customer as the hero and the company as the guide. Seven-part framework: Character (customer), Problem (what they face), Guide (your brand), Plan (the steps), Call to Action, Success (what they gain), Failure (what they avoid). Used for website messaging, brand positioning, and sales narratives.

### PR & Media Relations
- **PESO Model (Paid, Earned, Shared, Owned)** — Framework for integrated communications strategy. Paid media (advertising, sponsored content), Earned media (press coverage, analyst reports), Shared media (social platforms, community engagement), Owned media (blog, newsletter, website content). Modern PR strategies coordinate across all four channels.
- **Barcelona Principles 3.0** — AMEC's measurement framework for PR and communications. Key principles: goal setting is fundamental, measurement requires both outputs and outcomes, AVE (Advertising Value Equivalency) is not valid as a measure of earned media, social media can and should be measured, and measurement should be transparent and reproducible.

## Regulations

### Email & Outreach Compliance
- **CAN-SPAM Act (US)** — Federal law governing commercial email. Requirements: accurate From/Reply-To headers, non-deceptive subject lines, clear identification as advertisement, physical mailing address included, functioning unsubscribe mechanism that processes requests within 10 business days, no harvested email addresses. Penalties up to $51,744 per non-compliant email. Does NOT require opt-in (unlike GDPR) — US law is opt-out based.
- **TCPA (Telephone Consumer Protection Act)** — Governs telemarketing calls, texts, and pre-recorded messages. Requires prior express written consent for marketing calls to cell phones using an auto-dialer or pre-recorded voice. Maintains a National Do Not Call Registry. Penalties of $500-$1,500 per violation. Class action lawsuits under TCPA have resulted in settlements exceeding $100M.
- **GDPR (General Data Protection Regulation, EU)** — Impacts sales outreach to EU residents. Requires lawful basis for processing personal data (consent or legitimate interest for B2B). Right to access, rectification, erasure ("right to be forgotten"), and data portability. Data processing agreements required with vendors handling EU personal data. Penalties up to 4% of global annual revenue or EUR 20M.
- **CASL (Canada's Anti-Spam Legislation)** — Requires express or implied consent before sending commercial electronic messages. Implied consent expires 2 years after a business transaction or 6 months after an inquiry. More restrictive than CAN-SPAM. Penalties up to CAD 10M per violation.
- **CCPA/CPRA (California)** — California's data privacy law. Gives consumers right to know what data is collected, right to delete, right to opt out of sale/sharing of personal data. Impacts lead databases and contact enrichment practices.

### PR & Communications Compliance
- **FTC Endorsement Guidelines (16 CFR Part 255)** — Requires clear disclosure of material connections between endorsers and companies. Influencer partnerships must disclose compensation. Testimonials must reflect honest opinions. "Results not typical" disclaimers must include typical results. Updated 2023 guidance extends to social media, affiliate marketing, and employee advocacy programs.
- **SEC Regulation FD (Fair Disclosure)** — Prohibits selective disclosure of material nonpublic information to analysts, institutional investors, or other market participants. Public companies must disclose material information broadly. Impacts how PR teams handle earnings guidance, product announcements, and executive communications. Violations can result in SEC enforcement actions.
- **SEC Rule 10b-5** — Prohibits fraud in connection with securities transactions, including misleading press releases, inflated customer/revenue claims, or omission of material facts in public communications by publicly traded companies.
- **Lanham Act (15 U.S.C. Section 1125)** — Governs false advertising claims. Competitors can sue over misleading claims, including unsubstantiated "industry-first," "#1," or "best-in-class" assertions. Requires substantiation for comparative advertising claims.

### Data & Privacy in Sales
- **Do Not Call Registry (FTC)** — Telemarketers must scrub lists against the registry every 31 days. Established business relationships (within 18 months) and prior express invitation or permission are exceptions.
- **LinkedIn Terms of Service** — Prohibits scraping, automated messaging, and exceeding connection request limits. Violations result in account restriction or permanent ban. Important constraint for SDR operations using LinkedIn Sales Navigator.
- **GDPR Legitimate Interest (Recital 47)** — B2B prospecting may be conducted under legitimate interest without explicit consent if the outreach is relevant, proportionate, and includes an easy opt-out. However, this must be documented in a Legitimate Interest Assessment (LIA), and the data subject's interests must not override the company's commercial interest.

## Common Workflows

### Lead Qualification & Pipeline Progression
1. **Prospect Identification** — SDR uses ZoomInfo, Apollo, or LinkedIn Sales Navigator to build target account lists based on ICP (Ideal Customer Profile) criteria: industry, revenue range, employee count, technology stack, and intent signals.
2. **Outreach Sequence** — Multi-touch cadence (typically 12-15 touches over 3-4 weeks) combining email, phone, LinkedIn, and sometimes direct mail. First touch is personalized to a specific trigger (funding round, new hire, industry event). Subsequent touches provide value (case study, benchmark data, industry report) rather than just "checking in."
3. **Discovery Call** — Qualifying conversation using MEDDIC, BANT, or SPICED. The SDR or AE identifies the pain, maps the decision process, and determines if the opportunity merits progression. Outcome: qualified opportunity created in CRM (SQL) or disqualified with reason code.
4. **Demo/Technical Evaluation** — AE conducts product demonstration tailored to the prospect's specific use case. For complex deals, includes proof of concept, technical workshop, or sandbox access. Sales engineer supports technical requirements.
5. **Proposal & Negotiation** — Formal proposal generated via CPQ tool. Includes scope, pricing, implementation timeline, and terms. Negotiation involves procurement, legal review of MSA/Order Form, and potential discount approval through deal desk.
6. **Close & Handoff** — Contract execution (DocuSign or similar). Account transitions from Sales to Customer Success. Structured handoff includes account history, stakeholder map, success criteria, and implementation plan.

### Content Distribution & Repurposing
1. **Pillar Content Creation** — Long-form asset (webinar, whitepaper, research report, podcast episode) produced by content team.
2. **Atomization** — Pillar content broken into 30-50+ derivative pieces: blog posts, social media posts, email snippets, infographics, short video clips, quote graphics, carousel slides.
3. **Channel Mapping** — Each derivative piece mapped to the appropriate channel (LinkedIn for thought leadership, X/Twitter for quick takes, email for gated content, YouTube for video, Instagram for visual summaries).
4. **Distribution Calendar** — Content scheduled across channels over 2-4 weeks, with platform-specific timing optimization (LinkedIn: Tuesday-Thursday mornings, X/Twitter: throughout the day, email: Tuesday-Wednesday 9-11am).
5. **Performance Tracking** — Engagement metrics (impressions, clicks, shares, comments) tracked per piece and per channel. Top performers recycled or expanded. Underperformers analyzed and adjusted.

### PR & Media Pitching
1. **Story Angle Development** — PR team identifies newsworthy angles: product launches, funding rounds, executive hires, research data, customer milestones, industry trend commentary.
2. **Media List Building** — Target journalists identified via Cision, Muck Rack, or Meltwater based on beat coverage, recent articles, and outlet audience alignment.
3. **Pitch Crafting** — Personalized pitches (not mass emails) referencing the journalist's recent work, explaining why the story matters to their audience, and offering exclusive access (interview, data, early product access).
4. **Follow-Up & Relationship Management** — One follow-up after 3-5 business days. No more than two follow-ups total. Respect "no" and "not interested" responses. Build long-term relationships beyond transactional pitching.
5. **Coverage Amplification** — Earned media shared across owned and social channels. Internal distribution to sales team (press coverage as social proof). Metrics: media mentions, share of voice, sentiment, domain authority of outlets.

### Customer Onboarding & Success
1. **Kickoff Call** — CSM conducts structured onboarding call within 48 hours of contract execution. Reviews success criteria (established during sales), confirms stakeholders, sets implementation timeline, and establishes communication cadence.
2. **Technical Implementation** — Product deployed, integrations configured, data migrated. CSM coordinates between customer's technical team and vendor's implementation engineers.
3. **User Training** — Role-specific training sessions (admin, end user, executive). Documentation provided. In-app guides configured (Intercom product tours, Pendo guides, WalkMe).
4. **Time to First Value (TTFV)** — Critical metric. The customer must achieve their first measurable success within the first 30 days. If TTFV exceeds 45 days, churn risk increases dramatically.
5. **Ongoing Health Monitoring** — Gainsight or ChurnZero tracks usage metrics, support tickets, NPS responses, and engagement signals. Health score declines trigger automated or manual CSM intervention. Quarterly Business Reviews (QBRs) align on ROI achievement and expansion opportunities.

## Terminology Glossary

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **MQL (Marketing Qualified Lead)** | A lead that has engaged with marketing content (downloaded whitepaper, attended webinar, visited pricing page) and meets ICP criteria. Passed from marketing to SDR for qualification. |
| **SQL (Sales Qualified Lead)** | A lead that an SDR has spoken with and confirmed meets qualification criteria (budget, authority, need, timeline or equivalent). Passed to AE for demo/discovery. |
| **SAL (Sales Accepted Lead)** | An MQL that the sales team has accepted and committed to working. Intermediate stage between MQL and SQL in some organizations. |
| **ACV (Annual Contract Value)** | The annualized revenue from a single customer contract. A $36K 3-year contract has an ACV of $12K. |
| **ARR (Annual Recurring Revenue)** | Total annualized recurring revenue from all active subscriptions. The primary growth metric for SaaS companies. |
| **MRR (Monthly Recurring Revenue)** | ARR divided by 12. Used for monthly tracking and forecasting. |
| **TCV (Total Contract Value)** | The full value of a contract over its entire term. A $12K/year 3-year deal has a TCV of $36K. |
| **Pipeline Velocity** | How fast deals move through the pipeline. Calculated as: (Number of Opportunities x Average Deal Size x Win Rate) / Average Sales Cycle Length. Higher velocity = more revenue per unit time. |
| **Win Rate** | Percentage of qualified opportunities that result in closed-won deals. Tracked by stage (demo-to-close, proposal-to-close) and by segment. |
| **Churn Rate** | Percentage of customers (logo churn) or revenue (revenue churn) lost in a given period. Gross churn doesn't account for expansion; net churn includes upsell/cross-sell revenue from existing customers. |
| **NPS (Net Promoter Score)** | Survey metric: "How likely are you to recommend us?" (0-10). Promoters (9-10) minus Detractors (0-6) = NPS. Range: -100 to +100. B2B SaaS benchmark: 30-50. |
| **CSAT (Customer Satisfaction Score)** | Post-interaction survey: "How satisfied were you?" Typically 1-5 scale. Reported as percentage of satisfied responses (4-5). |
| **CES (Customer Effort Score)** | Post-interaction survey: "How easy was it to [accomplish task]?" Lower effort correlates with higher retention. |
| **CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost)** | Total sales and marketing spend divided by number of new customers acquired. CAC payback period = months to recover CAC from customer revenue. |
| **LTV (Lifetime Value)** | Predicted total revenue from a customer over the entire relationship. LTV:CAC ratio of 3:1 or higher is the benchmark for efficient growth. |
| **ICP (Ideal Customer Profile)** | A detailed description of the company type that gets the most value from the product — industry, size, technology stack, organizational structure, buying triggers. Not a buyer persona (which describes the individual). |
| **Buyer Persona** | A semi-fictional representation of an individual buyer: title, responsibilities, goals, challenges, information sources, objections. Used to personalize messaging. |
| **SDR (Sales Development Representative)** | Outbound prospecting role responsible for generating qualified meetings for AEs. Measured on meetings booked, pipeline generated, and SQL conversion. |
| **AE (Account Executive)** — Quota-carrying closer responsible for running demos, managing the sales cycle, negotiating terms, and closing deals. Measured on closed-won revenue, ACV, and win rate. |
| **CSM (Customer Success Manager)** | Post-sale relationship owner responsible for onboarding, adoption, retention, and expansion. Measured on net revenue retention, NPS, and gross churn. |
| **BDR (Business Development Representative)** | In some organizations, synonymous with SDR. In others, BDR handles inbound leads while SDR handles outbound prospecting. |
| **Deal Desk** | Internal function (often within RevOps) that approves non-standard pricing, discounts, payment terms, and contract structures. Ensures margin protection and pricing consistency. |
| **RevOps (Revenue Operations)** | Function that aligns sales, marketing, and customer success operations. Manages CRM, tools, processes, data hygiene, territory assignments, compensation plans, and reporting. |
| **MoM (Month-over-Month)** | Percentage change from one month to the next. Common for tracking growth in MRR, pipeline, and activity metrics. |
| **QoQ (Quarter-over-Quarter)** | Percentage change from one quarter to the next. Used in executive reporting and board presentations. |
| **QBR (Quarterly Business Review)** | Structured meeting between CSM and customer to review ROI achievement, usage trends, upcoming roadmap, and expansion opportunities. |
| **Champion** | An internal advocate at the prospect's organization who actively sells the solution to their colleagues and leadership. Losing a Champion (through role change or departure) is one of the top reasons enterprise deals stall or die. |
| **Economic Buyer** | The person with budget authority to approve the purchase. May not be the day-to-day user. In enterprise sales, correctly identifying and engaging the Economic Buyer is the single highest-leverage activity. |
| **Buying Committee** | The group of stakeholders involved in the purchase decision. Enterprise buying committees average 6-10 people across functional areas. Multi-threading (building relationships with multiple committee members) reduces single-point-of-failure risk. |
| **Multi-threading** | Engaging multiple stakeholders within a prospect account rather than relying on a single contact. Reduces risk if your primary contact changes roles, goes on leave, or deprioritizes the initiative. |
| **TTFV (Time to First Value)** | Days from contract signing to the customer achieving their first measurable success outcome. The single most important predictor of long-term retention. |
| **NRR (Net Revenue Retention)** | Revenue retained from existing customers after accounting for churn, downgrades, and expansion. NRR above 100% means existing customers are growing faster than they're churning. Top SaaS companies target 120%+ NRR. |
| **GRR (Gross Revenue Retention)** | Revenue retained from existing customers excluding expansion. Only accounts for downgrades and churn. Maximum is 100%. GRR above 90% is strong. |
| **SOV (Share of Voice)** | Percentage of total media coverage or social conversation about a brand relative to competitors. Key PR and brand awareness metric. |
| **AVE (Advertising Value Equivalency)** | Deprecated metric that attempted to quantify earned media by calculating what the same space/time would cost as advertising. Rejected by AMEC and serious PR professionals as invalid. |

## 5 Most Common Mistakes

### 1. Spray-and-Pray Outreach
Sending high-volume, low-personalization outreach to massive lists. Response rates crater below 1%, sender reputation degrades (email domains get blacklisted), and prospects associate the brand with spam. The fix: smaller, highly targeted lists with personalized messaging referencing specific triggers. A sequence that converts at 8% on 200 contacts produces more pipeline than one that converts at 0.5% on 5,000.

### 2. Ignoring Buying Signals and Champion Changes
Sales teams obsessively track email opens and link clicks but miss the signals that actually predict deal outcomes: the Champion going quiet, the Economic Buyer canceling a scheduled call, a new VP joining the buying committee, or the prospect's company announcing a hiring freeze. Champion departures are especially lethal — when your internal advocate leaves the organization mid-deal, the deal has a less than 15% chance of closing at the original timeline and terms. CRM notes must be reviewed weekly for relationship changes, not just pipeline stage progression.

### 3. Failing to Segment Communications
Sending the same message to every contact regardless of their role, stage in the buying journey, or relationship history. A prospect who just downloaded a whitepaper needs educational content, not a demo request. An enterprise VP needs business impact framing, not feature lists. An existing customer on a free tier needs different messaging than an Enterprise customer approaching renewal. Segmentation applies to email sequences, social content, PR pitches, and customer success touchpoints.

### 4. Measuring Vanity Metrics Instead of Revenue Impact
Tracking email open rates, social media followers, press release impressions, and website visitors without connecting these to pipeline, revenue, or retention. A LinkedIn post with 50,000 impressions is meaningless if none of them convert to conversations. A press hit in a tier-1 outlet is meaningless if the target buyer audience doesn't read that outlet. Every metric should trace back to the revenue equation: pipeline generated, pipeline velocity, win rate, ACV, NRR, or CAC payback.

### 5. Neglecting the Post-Sale Experience
Pouring resources into acquisition while starving customer success. The typical SaaS company spends 5-7x more on acquiring a customer than retaining one, despite the fact that expanding existing customers is 3-5x more capital-efficient than acquiring new ones. Customer success is not support — it is proactive relationship management tied to business outcomes. Companies that treat post-sale as a cost center instead of a revenue engine suffer from high churn, low NRR, and an increasingly expensive acquisition model.

## What Excellent vs. Dangerous Work Looks Like

### Excellent Work
- **SDR outreach** that references a specific trigger (prospect just raised Series B, hired a new VP of Sales, announced expansion into a new market) and connects it to a relevant customer story — not a generic "I noticed you're in [industry]."
- **Proposals** that quantify ROI using the prospect's own metrics (discussed during discovery), address competitive alternatives head-on, include a realistic implementation timeline, and are tailored to the Economic Buyer's success criteria.
- **PR pitches** that are personalized to the journalist's beat, offer genuine news value or exclusive data, and respect the journalist's time with a concise format. Coverage that results in inbound leads, not just "awareness."
- **Email sequences** with clear segmentation, value-add in every touch (insight, data, resource — not just "circling back"), proper CAN-SPAM compliance, and escalation paths for non-response.
- **Customer success** that proactively identifies expansion opportunities by analyzing usage patterns, delivers QBRs with quantified ROI tied to the customer's stated business objectives, and detects churn signals 60+ days before renewal.
- **Content repurposing** that adapts the core message to each platform's native format and audience expectations — not just posting the same text across LinkedIn, X/Twitter, and email.

### Dangerous Work
- **Outreach** that uses purchased lists of unverified contacts, ignores unsubscribe requests, spoofs sender domains, or makes unsupported product claims. Violates CAN-SPAM, GDPR, or CASL and creates legal liability.
- **Proposals** that undercut margin floors without deal desk approval, promise features on the roadmap as current capabilities, or misrepresent contract terms.
- **PR communications** that make unsubstantiated superlative claims ("industry-first," "the only," "#1") without documented evidence, selectively disclose material information (SEC FD violation for public companies), or misrepresent customer results.
- **Email sequences** that blast the entire database without segmentation, send to EU contacts without GDPR-compliant consent or legitimate interest documentation, or use deceptive subject lines ("Re:" on first contact, fake urgency).
- **Customer success** that ignores declining health scores, rubber-stamps QBRs without data, or surprises customers with renewal price increases without advance discussion.
- **Sales forecasting** that is based on "gut feel" rather than data-driven pipeline analysis, inflates pipeline coverage, or moves close dates without updating deal qualification.
