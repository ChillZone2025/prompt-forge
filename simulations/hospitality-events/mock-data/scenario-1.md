## PLANTED ISSUES (for evaluation — agent should catch these)

1. **OTA commission masking true profitability (Salthaus):** The Salthaus coastal property shows 75% OTA channel share with a blended commission rate of 15.3%. But the financial summary reports gross revenue ($5,306,400 in room revenue). After netting commissions, actual room revenue is approximately $4,494,320 — a difference of $812,080. The property appears profitable in the financial summary but actual net room revenue is 15-20% lower than reported. An effective revenue agent must flag that gross revenue reporting without commission netting creates a false profitability picture and immediately recommend net revenue reporting.

2. **Urban property (The Lark) under-pricing during high demand:** The Lark runs 80.8% average occupancy with peak months at 89-91%, but its RPI is consistently below 90 (averaging 89.6). The comp set achieves $167 RevPAR to The Lark's $149.66. With occupancy at 89% in July, the ADR was only $195 while the comp set implied higher pricing. The hotel is filling rooms but pricing $25-35 below what the market will bear. The rate tiers are set once per season and held — no dynamic adjustment for demand spikes around convention center events or festivals. This is textbook under-pricing: high occupancy + below-comp-set ADR = rate opportunity.

3. **Mountain property (Ridgeline) has no demand forecasting:** Ridgeline's rates are set by the GM based on "feel" and "phone call volume" with no reference to historical demand patterns, comp set data, events calendar, or booking pace. The RPI averages 91.9, meaning it consistently underperforms the comp set by ~8%. The shoulder season RPI drops to 82.6-87.9, suggesting the GM doesn't know how to price low-demand periods. There is no RMS, no formal rate strategy, and no data-driven pricing methodology — purely intuition-based revenue management.

4. **Group attrition not enforced (The Lark):** 76% of group contracts (29 of 38) exceeded the 10% attrition allowance, with an actual portfolio-wide attrition rate of 35%. Zero dollars in attrition penalties were collected despite contractual provisions allowing them. Estimated annual revenue loss: $178,500. This is not a group sales problem — it's a contract enforcement problem. The hotel either isn't tracking attrition against contracts, doesn't have a process for invoicing penalties, or the sales team is waiving penalties to maintain relationships. An effective revenue agent must flag this as an immediate recoverable revenue opportunity.

5. **No length-of-stay restrictions during peak periods:** None of the three properties implement minimum length-of-stay (MLOS) restrictions during peak demand periods. The Salthaus runs 95-96% occupancy in July-August but accepts 1-night bookings, which create housekeeping cost inefficiency and displace potential 2-3 night stays. During Portland convention events (8,000 attendees), The Lark should require 2-night minimums but doesn't. Ridgeline accepts 1-night ski season bookings on Saturdays that prevent selling Friday-Sunday packages. MLOS restrictions during documented peak periods are a standard revenue management lever being completely ignored.

6. **Loyalty and guest retention crisis:** All three properties show repeat guest rates far below industry benchmarks: 6-11% vs. the 25-35% benchmark for boutique hotels. There is no loyalty program across the portfolio, email capture rates are low (28-42%), post-stay survey response is minimal (5-12%), and online review response rates are poor (5-35%). The Ridgeline at 5% review response rate and 3.9 TripAdvisor rating is particularly concerning — unanswered negative reviews are actively driving away future bookings. The portfolio has no guest retention strategy, no CRM, and no mechanism to convert one-time guests into repeat visitors.
