# Industry Context: Education

## What This Industry Does

Education encompasses the design, delivery, assessment, and continuous improvement of learning experiences across the full lifecycle of human development and professional growth. The sector spans K-12 public and private schools, higher education (community colleges, four-year universities, graduate/professional schools), corporate training and workforce development, EdTech platforms, and academic research institutions.

Key functions include curriculum design and instructional development, student assessment and grading, admissions and enrollment management, academic advising and career services, grant writing and research administration, parent/family communication, institutional research and accreditation reporting, and student information management. Supporting all of these are compliance obligations around student privacy (FERPA), accessibility (ADA/Section 504), and equity (Title IX).

The industry's defining characteristic is that it serves a uniquely vulnerable population — students whose futures are shaped by the quality, fairness, and integrity of the education they receive. A flawed assessment, a biased admissions decision, a mismanaged grant, or a breakdown in parent communication can alter individual life trajectories. Every process, system, and recommendation must account for the fact that real people — often minors — are the end users of every output.

## Key Tools & Platforms

### Learning Management Systems (LMS)
- **Canvas LMS (Instructure)** — The dominant LMS in higher education and growing in K-12. Hosts course content, assignments, discussions, quizzes, grade books, and learning analytics. Integrates via LTI (Learning Tools Interoperability) with hundreds of third-party tools. Supports SpeedGrader for rubric-based assessment and outcomes alignment.
- **Blackboard (now Anthology)** — Legacy LMS with deep institutional adoption, particularly in community colleges and large universities. Offers Ultra and Original interfaces. Supports SCORM content packages, Collaborate virtual classroom, and SafeAssign plagiarism detection.
- **Google Classroom** — Free LMS widely used in K-12 via Google Workspace for Education. Simple interface for assignment distribution, submission, and grading. Integrates with Google Docs, Sheets, Slides, and third-party add-ons. Limited analytics compared to Canvas/Blackboard.
- **Moodle** — Open-source LMS used globally, particularly in budget-constrained institutions and international contexts. Highly customizable with plugin architecture. Supports competency-based education, badges, and H5P interactive content. Self-hosted or cloud-hosted via Moodle Cloud.
- **D2L Brightspace** — LMS with strong adaptive learning and analytics capabilities. Used in both K-12 and higher ed. Intelligent Agents feature automates interventions based on student behavior patterns.
- **Schoology (PowerSchool)** — K-12 focused LMS with strong parent communication features and gradebook integration.

### Assessment & Academic Integrity
- **Turnitin** — Plagiarism detection and writing feedback platform. Compares submissions against internet sources, academic databases, and previously submitted student papers. Generates similarity reports with percentage match and source attribution. Also offers Gradescope and Feedback Studio.
- **Gradescope (Turnitin)** — AI-assisted grading platform for STEM and written assignments. Supports rubric-based grading, group grading of similar answers, and handwritten assignment scanning. Reduces grading time by 50-70% for large courses through answer clustering.
- **Proctorio / ExamSoft / Respondus LockDown Browser** — Online proctoring and exam security tools. Proctorio uses AI-based behavior monitoring. ExamSoft (now Anthology) provides item analysis and psychometric data. Respondus locks down the testing environment.
- **Qualtrics** — Survey and experience management platform used for course evaluations, student satisfaction surveys, alumni outcomes tracking, and institutional research.

### Student Information Systems (SIS)
- **PowerSchool** — The largest K-12 SIS in North America. Manages enrollment, attendance, grades, transcripts, and state reporting. Parent/student portal (PowerSchool Mobile) provides real-time access to grades and assignments.
- **Infinite Campus** — K-12 SIS with strong state reporting capabilities. Used in 45+ states. Supports special education (IEP management), 504 plan tracking, and multi-language parent communication.
- **Ellucian Banner** — The dominant ERP/SIS in higher education. Manages student records, financial aid, accounts receivable, HR, and finance. Banner 9 provides modern web interface. Integrates with DegreeWorks (degree audit) and CRM Advise (student success).
- **Ellucian Colleague** — Alternative higher ed ERP, particularly in community colleges. Self-service portal for registration, financial aid, and academic planning.
- **PeopleSoft Campus Solutions (Oracle)** — Higher ed SIS used at large research universities. Manages admissions, student records, financial aid, and academic advising.

### Admissions & Enrollment
- **Slate (Technolutions)** — The dominant CRM and admissions platform in higher education. Manages the full admissions funnel: inquiry, application, review, decision, yield. Supports application reading with rubrics, committee review workflows, territory management, and event planning. Highly configurable with Slate queries, reports, and portals.
- **Common Application / Coalition Application** — Centralized application platforms for undergraduate admissions. Common App serves 1,000+ member institutions. Coalition (now part of Scoir) focuses on access and affordability.
- **SchoolAdmin / Ravenna** — Private K-12 admissions management platforms.

### Grant Writing & Research Administration
- **Grants.gov** — The federal government's central portal for finding and applying to all federal grant opportunities. Supports Workspace for multi-user application development. Requires SAM.gov registration and UEI (Unique Entity Identifier).
- **Research.gov (NSF)** — NSF's portal for proposal submission, award management, and project reporting. Houses FastLane (legacy) and Research.gov proposal submission system. Manages Current & Pending disclosures, bio sketches in NSF format, and project outcomes reporting.
- **eRA Commons (NIH)** — NIH's electronic research administration system. Manages grant applications (via ASSIST or Grants.gov), Just-In-Time information, progress reports (RPPR), and compliance reporting.
- **Cayuse / Kuali Research** — Institutional research administration platforms for managing proposals, IRB protocols, conflicts of interest, and compliance.
- **Pivot-RP (ProQuest)** — Funding opportunity search engine used by researchers and research offices to identify relevant grants.

### Reference Management & Research Tools
- **Mendeley (Elsevier)** — Reference manager and academic social network. Stores PDFs, generates citations in 9,000+ styles, and supports collaborative annotation.
- **Zotero** — Free, open-source reference manager. Browser connector captures citations directly from web pages. Supports group libraries for collaborative research.
- **Overleaf** — Collaborative LaTeX editor used for academic paper writing, particularly in STEM fields. Integrates with journal submission systems.

### Communication & Family Engagement
- **ParentSquare** — K-12 family engagement platform with multi-language translation, unified messaging, and consent/permission slip management.
- **Remind** — Messaging platform for teacher-parent-student communication. Supports scheduled messages and translation.
- **ClassDojo** — Behavior tracking and family communication for elementary schools.
- **Seesaw** — Student-driven digital portfolio and family engagement platform for K-8.

## Key Frameworks & Methodologies

### Instructional Design
- **Bloom's Taxonomy (Revised, Anderson & Krathwohl 2001)** — Hierarchical classification of cognitive learning objectives: Remember, Understand, Apply, Analyze, Evaluate, Create. The foundation of learning objective writing. Verbs must match the target cognitive level — "list" (Remember) is fundamentally different from "evaluate" (Evaluate). Curriculum alignment requires that assessments measure the cognitive level stated in the objective.
- **Universal Design for Learning (UDL, CAST)** — Framework for designing flexible learning experiences that accommodate variability in how students engage (the "why" of learning), represent information (the "what"), and express knowledge (the "how"). Three principles: Multiple Means of Engagement, Multiple Means of Representation, Multiple Means of Action & Expression. Legally grounded in ADA/Section 504 compliance for higher ed.
- **Backward Design (Wiggins & McTighe, Understanding by Design)** — Three-stage curriculum design: (1) Identify desired results (learning goals), (2) Determine acceptable evidence (assessments), (3) Plan learning experiences. Prevents the common mistake of planning activities before defining what students should actually learn and how you will know they learned it.
- **ADDIE Model** — Systematic instructional design process: Analysis, Design, Development, Implementation, Evaluation. Widely used in corporate training and higher ed course development. The Evaluation phase loops back to Analysis for continuous improvement.
- **SAMR Model (Puentedura)** — Framework for integrating technology into instruction: Substitution (tech replaces tool with no functional change), Augmentation (tech replaces with functional improvement), Modification (tech allows significant redesign), Redefinition (tech allows creation of previously inconceivable tasks). Useful for evaluating whether EdTech adds pedagogical value or is technology for its own sake.

### Assessment & Evaluation
- **Kirkpatrick's Four Levels of Evaluation** — Framework for evaluating training effectiveness: Level 1 (Reaction — did they like it?), Level 2 (Learning — did they learn it?), Level 3 (Behavior — did they apply it?), Level 4 (Results — did it impact outcomes?). Most training evaluation stops at Level 1; rigorous evaluation requires all four levels.
- **Webb's Depth of Knowledge (DOK)** — Four-level framework for cognitive complexity of assessment items: Level 1 (Recall), Level 2 (Skill/Concept), Level 3 (Strategic Thinking), Level 4 (Extended Thinking). Used in K-12 to align state standards and assessments. Different from Bloom's in that it classifies complexity of the task, not the cognitive process.
- **Danielson Framework for Teaching** — Evaluation framework for K-12 teacher performance across four domains: Planning and Preparation, Classroom Environment, Instruction, Professional Responsibilities. Used in teacher evaluation systems in 20+ states. Four performance levels: Unsatisfactory, Basic, Proficient, Distinguished.

### Accreditation & Quality Assurance
- **SACSCOC (Southern Association of Colleges and Schools Commission on Colleges)** — Regional accreditor for institutions in 11 Southern states. Compliance Certification and Quality Enhancement Plan (QEP) required for reaffirmation every 10 years. Standards cover institutional mission, governance, faculty credentials, student learning outcomes, and financial stability.
- **HLC (Higher Learning Commission)** — Regional accreditor for institutions in 19 North-Central states. Open Pathway, Standard Pathway, or AQIP pathway for accreditation maintenance. Criteria for Accreditation cover mission, integrity, teaching/learning, resources, and planning.
- **ABET (Accreditation Board for Engineering and Technology)** — Program-level accreditor for engineering, computing, applied science, and technology programs. Student Outcomes (1-7) must be systematically assessed with continuous improvement evidence. Critical for engineering licensure (PE exam eligibility).
- **AACSB (Association to Advance Collegiate Schools of Business)** — Business school accreditor. Assurance of Learning (AoL) standards require direct and indirect assessment of student learning. Only ~6% of business schools worldwide hold AACSB accreditation.
- **CAEP (Council for the Accreditation of Educator Preparation)** — Accreditor for teacher preparation programs. Requires evidence of completer impact on P-12 student learning.

### Research Compliance
- **IRB (Institutional Review Board)** — Federal requirement (45 CFR 46, the "Common Rule") for review and approval of human subjects research. Three review levels: Exempt, Expedited, Full Board. Requires informed consent, risk-benefit assessment, equitable subject selection, and privacy/confidentiality protections. IRB approval must be obtained BEFORE data collection begins — retroactive approval is not possible.
- **Responsible Conduct of Research (RCR)** — NSF and NIH require RCR training for all grant-supported trainees. Covers data management, authorship, peer review, mentoring, conflicts of interest, and research misconduct (fabrication, falsification, plagiarism).

## Regulations

### Student Privacy
- **FERPA (Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act, 20 U.S.C. § 1232g)** — The foundational student privacy law. Protects the privacy of student education records. Institutions that receive federal funding must: (1) allow eligible students/parents to inspect records, (2) allow amendment requests, (3) limit disclosure without consent (with specific exceptions: directory information, school officials with legitimate educational interest, health/safety emergencies). Violations can result in loss of all federal funding. FERPA applies to all education records, including grades, transcripts, disciplinary records, financial aid records, and any personally identifiable information derived from education records.
- **COPPA (Children's Online Privacy Protection Act)** — Applies to online services collecting data from children under 13. K-12 schools using EdTech tools must ensure COPPA compliance — schools can consent on behalf of parents for school-authorized educational purposes, but the EdTech vendor must meet COPPA requirements (verifiable parental consent, data minimization, security, deletion upon request).
- **State Student Privacy Laws** — California (SOPIPA), New York (Education Law 2-d), Colorado, Connecticut, and 40+ other states have student data privacy laws that often exceed FERPA requirements. Many require data governance plans, vendor agreements, and breach notification.

### Civil Rights & Equity
- **Title IX of the Education Amendments of 1972** — Prohibits sex-based discrimination in education programs receiving federal funding. Covers admissions, athletics, sexual harassment/assault, pregnancy discrimination, and LGBTQ+ protections (scope varies by administration and court rulings). Institutions must designate a Title IX coordinator and maintain grievance procedures.
- **IDEA (Individuals with Disabilities Education Act)** — Guarantees free appropriate public education (FAPE) for children with disabilities ages 3-21. Requires Individualized Education Programs (IEPs), least restrictive environment (LRE), procedural safeguards for parents, and Child Find obligations. IEP process involves evaluation, eligibility determination, IEP development, placement, and annual review.
- **Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act** — Prohibits disability discrimination in programs receiving federal funding. Broader definition of disability than IDEA. 504 Plans provide accommodations (extended time, preferential seating, assistive technology) without the full IEP process. Applies from K-12 through higher education.
- **ADA (Americans with Disabilities Act)** — Applies to all public institutions and private institutions of a certain size. Title II covers public universities; Title III covers private institutions. Requires accessible facilities, technology, and communications. In higher ed, requires accessible course materials, captioned videos, and screen-reader-compatible content.
- **Clery Act (Jeanne Clery Disclosure of Campus Security Policy and Campus Crime Statistics Act)** — Requires colleges and universities to disclose campus crime statistics and security policies. Annual Security Report (ASR) must be published by October 1 each year. Timely warning requirements for Clery-reportable crimes. Violations can result in fines up to $69,733 per violation.

### Accreditation & Institutional Compliance
- **Gainful Employment (34 CFR Part 668)** — Federal regulation requiring career education programs to demonstrate adequate graduate earnings relative to debt. Programs failing debt-to-earnings ratios face disclosure, warning, and potential loss of Title IV eligibility.
- **Title IV (Higher Education Act)** — Federal student financial aid programs (Pell Grants, Direct Loans, PLUS Loans, Federal Work-Study). Participating institutions must meet administrative capability, financial responsibility, and program integrity requirements. Audited by the Department of Education's Federal Student Aid office.
- **SACSCOC/HLC/regional accreditation standards** — Institutions must maintain regional accreditation to participate in Title IV. Substantive changes (new programs, locations, distance education) require accreditor notification or approval.

### Research Compliance
- **45 CFR 46 (The Common Rule)** — Federal policy for protection of human subjects in research. Applies to all federally funded human subjects research. Revised 2018 with changes to exempt categories, informed consent requirements, and single-IRB mandate for multi-site research.
- **NSF Proposal & Award Policies & Procedures Guide (PAPPG)** — Governs all NSF proposal submissions. Specific requirements for biographical sketches (no publications "in preparation"), current & pending support disclosure (all sources, including foreign), data management plans, and mentoring plans for postdoctoral researchers. Updated annually — agents must reference the current version.
- **NIH Grants Policy Statement** — Governs all NIH-funded research. Includes salary cap ($221,900 for FY2025), NRSA stipend levels, modular budget thresholds ($250K direct costs), and biosketch format requirements.

## Common Terminology

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Rubric** | Scoring guide that defines criteria and performance levels for an assignment or assessment. Analytic rubrics score each criterion separately; holistic rubrics assign a single score based on overall quality. |
| **Formative Assessment** | Assessment conducted during instruction to monitor learning and provide feedback. Low-stakes, intended to guide teaching adjustments. Examples: exit tickets, think-pair-share, quizzes, draft feedback. |
| **Summative Assessment** | Assessment conducted after instruction to evaluate learning against standards. High-stakes, contributes to grades. Examples: final exams, capstone projects, standardized tests. |
| **IEP (Individualized Education Program)** | Legal document under IDEA specifying a student's present levels, annual goals, accommodations, modifications, services, and placement. Developed by the IEP team (parents, teachers, special education staff, administrators). Must be reviewed and updated annually. |
| **504 Plan** | Accommodation plan under Section 504 for students with disabilities who do not qualify for an IEP but need accommodations to access the general education curriculum. Less formal than an IEP, no specialized instruction requirement. |
| **SLO (Student Learning Outcome)** | Measurable statement of what a student should know, be able to do, or value upon completing a course or program. Must use observable, measurable verbs aligned to Bloom's Taxonomy. |
| **CLO (Course Learning Outcome)** | Learning outcome specific to a single course. Must align upward to program-level outcomes (PLOs) and institutional learning outcomes (ILOs). |
| **PLO (Program Learning Outcome)** | Learning outcome at the degree/certificate program level. Assessed through curriculum mapping and capstone/signature assignments. |
| **Retention Rate** | Percentage of first-time, full-time students who return for their second year. IPEDS definition. A key metric for institutional effectiveness and accreditation. National average ~75% at four-year institutions. |
| **Yield Rate** | Percentage of admitted students who enroll. A critical admissions metric. Higher yield = less over-admission needed. Influenced by financial aid packaging, campus visits, and peer competition. |
| **Discount Rate** | Percentage of gross tuition revenue given back as institutional financial aid. Average ~55% at private four-year institutions. A discount rate that is too high indicates unsustainable pricing strategy. |
| **F&A Costs (Facilities and Administrative)** | Indirect costs on sponsored research (also called "overhead"). Negotiated rate between institution and federal government (typically 40-65% of modified total direct costs). Covers building maintenance, utilities, administrative support, libraries. |
| **PI (Principal Investigator)** | Lead researcher responsible for the intellectual direction of a grant-funded project. Responsible for technical reporting, compliance, and budget management. Must hold appropriate faculty appointment at the applying institution. |
| **Co-PI (Co-Principal Investigator)** | Shares responsibility for grant direction with the PI. NSF allows multiple Co-PIs; NIH uses "Multi-PI" leadership plan for projects with more than one PI. |
| **IRB (Institutional Review Board)** | Committee that reviews human subjects research for ethical compliance. Three review levels: Exempt (minimal risk, fits defined categories), Expedited (minimal risk, fits expedited categories), Full Board (greater than minimal risk). |
| **IACUC (Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee)** | Committee that reviews and approves animal research protocols. Required by PHS Policy and USDA Animal Welfare Act. |
| **IPEDS (Integrated Postsecondary Education Data System)** | Federal data collection system operated by NCES. All Title IV institutions must report annually on enrollment, graduation rates, finances, faculty, and student financial aid. Data is publicly available via College Scorecard and College Navigator. |
| **CIP Code (Classification of Instructional Programs)** | Six-digit code classifying academic programs. Used in IPEDS reporting, state authorization, and accreditation. Example: 11.0701 = Computer Science. |
| **Credit Hour** | Unit of academic credit. Federal definition (34 CFR 600.2): one hour of classroom instruction plus two hours of out-of-class work per week for 15 weeks (or equivalent). Audited by accreditors and state agencies. |
| **Scaffolding** | Instructional technique of providing temporary supports that are gradually removed as students gain competence. Examples: graphic organizers, sentence starters, worked examples, think-alouds. |
| **Differentiated Instruction** | Adapting content, process, product, or learning environment based on student readiness, interest, or learning profile. Often confused with individualized instruction (1:1 modification) but is designed for groups. |
| **FAFSA (Free Application for Federal Student Aid)** | Federal application required for all Title IV financial aid. Simplified in 2024 (FAFSA Simplification Act). Determines Expected Family Contribution (now Student Aid Index, SAI). |
| **SAI (Student Aid Index)** | Replaced EFC (Expected Family Contribution) in 2024. Calculated from FAFSA data. Can be negative (down to -$1,500), indicating maximum Pell Grant eligibility. |
| **QEP (Quality Enhancement Plan)** | SACSCOC-specific requirement for reaffirmation of accreditation. A focused plan for institutional improvement in student learning, chosen by the institution, implemented over 5+ years, with assessment evidence. |
| **Enrollment Funnel** | Admissions pipeline stages: Prospect -> Inquiry -> Applicant -> Admit -> Deposited -> Enrolled. Conversion rates at each stage are key admissions metrics. |
| **Melt** | Loss of enrolled/deposited students between deposit and census date. Summer melt is a significant challenge, particularly for first-generation and low-income students. |
| **DFW Rate** | Percentage of students earning D, F, or Withdrawal in a course. A key indicator of course-level barriers. Courses with DFW rates above 25% warrant curricular review. |
| **OER (Open Educational Resources)** | Free, openly licensed educational materials. Includes textbooks, course modules, videos, and assessments. Licensed under Creative Commons (typically CC BY or CC BY-SA). |
| **FERPA Directory Information** | Student information that may be disclosed without consent (name, address, enrollment dates, degree, honors). Institution must define directory information in its FERPA policy and allow students to opt out. |
| **Substantive Change** | A significant modification to an institution's mission, programs, or operations that requires accreditor notification or approval. Examples: adding distance education, opening a new location, starting a doctoral program. |

## 5 Most Common Mistakes in This Field

1. **FERPA violations through casual data sharing** — Faculty emailing grade lists with student names, posting grades by Social Security number (still happens), discussing student performance with parents of adult students without a signed FERPA waiver, or using non-institutional email/tools to transmit student records. "I was just trying to be helpful" does not constitute a FERPA exception. Directory information exceptions are narrow and must be defined in institutional policy. A single systemic FERPA violation can trigger a Department of Education investigation and threaten all federal funding.

2. **Assessment-objective misalignment** — Writing learning outcomes at the "Analyze" or "Evaluate" level of Bloom's Taxonomy but assessing with multiple-choice recall questions. The result is that the institution claims students can analyze and evaluate but has no evidence to support it. This is the single most common accreditation finding across all regional accreditors. Fix: backward design — write the assessment first, then verify it measures the stated outcome at the stated cognitive level.

3. **Survey data presented as statistically valid without adequate response rates** — Course evaluations, student satisfaction surveys, and program review surveys routinely have 15-25% response rates. These results are presented in accreditation reports, program reviews, and strategic plans as if they represent the student population. A 15% response rate introduces severe non-response bias — the students who respond are systematically different from those who don't. Minimum threshold for actionable data: 30% response rate with demographic representativeness analysis.

4. **Grant budgets that ignore institutional requirements** — New faculty submitting grant proposals without consulting the Office of Sponsored Programs. Common errors: using the wrong F&A rate, not including fringe benefit rates, budgeting for equipment without institutional approval, failing to include cost-sharing commitments in the budget justification, and not accounting for effort certification requirements. At NSF, a budget error can result in the proposal being returned without review.

5. **Admissions yield models built on aspirational rather than actual peer data** — Institutions comparing themselves to schools they aspire to resemble rather than schools that actually share their characteristics (size, selectivity, geography, mission). This produces unrealistic yield predictions, leads to over- or under-admission, and creates enrollment cliffs or housing crises. Peer institutions must be selected based on IPEDS data and validated by enrollment trends, not by institutional ambition.

## Excellent vs. Dangerous Work

### Excellent Work Looks Like
- Learning outcomes written with measurable verbs, mapped to program outcomes, and assessed with aligned instruments at the appropriate cognitive level
- Assessment rubrics that are reliable (different graders produce the same score), valid (they measure what they claim), and transparent (students see them before the assignment)
- Grant proposals where every budget line is justified by the research plan, F&A rates match the institution's negotiated agreement, and current & pending support is fully disclosed
- Admissions models built on actual peer data with yield predictions validated against 3+ years of historical enrollment
- Parent communication that respects FERPA boundaries while maintaining transparency appropriate to the student's age and enrollment status
- Program reviews that use multiple data sources (direct assessment, indirect assessment, external benchmarks) and produce specific, actionable improvement plans
- IEP goals that are specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, and time-bound (SMART) with progress monitoring data driving instructional adjustments

### Dangerous Work Looks Like
- Reporting graduation rates using a different cohort definition than IPEDS requires and presenting the inflated number in marketing materials
- Counting students as "employed" in placement data when they have part-time, unrelated, or freelance work
- Submitting grant proposals with undisclosed conflicts of interest or failing to report foreign funding sources
- Using survey data with a 10% response rate to make curricular decisions affecting hundreds of students
- Sharing student grades, disciplinary records, or disability status with unauthorized individuals
- Faculty evaluations that are punitive rather than developmental, based on a single observation
- Admissions decisions that use unvalidated predictive models with demographic proxies that function as discriminatory screens
- IEPs written with goals so vague ("student will improve reading") that progress cannot be measured
