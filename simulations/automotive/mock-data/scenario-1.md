## PLANTED ISSUES (for evaluation — agent should catch these)

1. **Used vehicle inventory aging — 35 units over 60 days with no markdown strategy:** 35 of 125 used vehicles (28%) are past the 60-day benchmark, costing $33,876/month ($406K/year) in floor plan interest alone. The additional losses from depreciation, lot space consumption, and eventual wholesale loss are not captured in this number. The used car manager's stated philosophy — "we don't chase the market down" — is directly contradicted by the data: vehicles over 60 days have average front-end gross of $680 (61-75 days) and $220 (76-90 days), meaning the longer they sit, the less they make. The 90+ day units are selling at a $350 loss on average. There is no documented markdown escalation policy. A proper aged inventory protocol: price-to-market at day 30, aggressive markdown at day 45, wholesale decision at day 60. The 6 detailed units are all priced 8-12% above vAuto market data — they will continue aging until priced competitively. This single issue is likely costing the dealership $500K+/year in combined floor plan interest, depreciation, and eventual wholesale losses.

2. **Service absorption rate of 67.9% vs. 80% benchmark — service department not covering fixed overhead:** The $160,040/month gap between fixed ops gross and fixed overhead means the dealership needs to make $160K+ in vehicle sales gross profit just to break even. The root causes are identifiable: (a) effective labor rate is $148 vs. $155-$170 benchmark (pricing too low), (b) hours per RO is 1.42 vs. 1.6-2.0 benchmark (service advisors not recommending work found on inspections), (c) service advisor upsell is $62/RO vs. $80-$120 benchmark (advisors rushing through presentations), (d) the Hunter Quick Check system generates objective data that advisors are not using. Fixing service absorption requires a combination of rate adjustment, advisor training on multi-point inspection presentation, and accountability for recommended-to-sold metrics.

3. **Used vehicle F&I PVR of $900 vs. $1,850 new — massive missed opportunity:** The $950/unit gap on 82 units/month is $77,900/month in unrealized gross. The F&I manager's explanation ("used car customers are more price-sensitive... we present the menu but don't push it") reveals a fundamental misunderstanding: used vehicle buyers have greater F&I product needs (no factory warranty = higher VSC need; higher LTV ratios = higher GAP need). VSC penetration at 28% vs. 45% benchmark alone represents approximately $27,550/month in lost gross. The F&I manager is applying new-vehicle assumptions to used-vehicle customers. A targeted used-vehicle F&I process — emphasizing that these vehicles have no factory warranty and that the VSC is their protection — would materially improve PVR.

4. **BDC appointment-show rate of 44.9% vs. 65% benchmark — broken confirmation process:** The confirmation workflow has a critical gap: no personal contact between initial booking and the day-of automated text. Customers who book appointments online or by phone may book days in advance. Without a personal confirmation call at 24 hours and a day-of personal reminder, there is no human accountability for the appointment. The 83 missed appointments per month (at the 20% gap) represent approximately 33 lost vehicle sales at the current shown-to-sold rate — worth an estimated $99,000-$132,000/month in total GPU. The fix is straightforward and low-cost: implement a 3-touch confirmation process with personal phone/text contact at 24 hours and 2 hours before appointment.

5. **$180K in obsolete parts with no OEM return program utilization:** Parts obsolescence at 29% of total inventory ($180K) is catastrophic — benchmark is under 10%. The parts manager's rationale ("we still get the occasional repair") does not withstand financial analysis: the carrying cost of $180K at 7.8% is $14,040/year in interest alone, plus bin space that could hold fast-moving inventory. Honda's parts return program allows quarterly returns of up to 5% of annual purchases. At Riverside Honda's purchase level, that is approximately $52K/quarter or $208K/year — more than enough to systematically eliminate the obsolete inventory over 4 quarters. The parts turn rate of 5.8 vs. benchmark 8-12 is a direct consequence of carrying $180K in dead stock. Removing obsolete parts would immediately improve turn rate to approximately 9.5 turns.

6. **CSI at 870 vs. 900 threshold — $400K/year allocation bonus at risk:** The CSI shortfall has a direct, quantifiable financial consequence: $400,000/year in OEM allocation bonus. The dealership has been below threshold for Q1 2026 and a second consecutive quarter below will trigger loss of the bonus. The root causes are operational, not cultural — delivery process bottleneck (2.5 hours vs. 1-1.5 hour benchmark, driven by only 2 F&I managers for 230 units/month), service timeliness (quoting 2 hours for 45-minute services, driven by technician productivity and RO stacking), and service communication (advisors too busy to update customers). These are solvable problems, but they require immediate action: (a) add a third F&I manager or restructure the delivery process, (b) implement service lane scheduling to reduce peak-hour congestion, (c) implement automated service status text updates. The $400K bonus is at risk and the fixes are straightforward — this should be the #1 priority.
