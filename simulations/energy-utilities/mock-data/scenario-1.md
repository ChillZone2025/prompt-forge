### Lighting Retrofit Analysis (from auditor report)

| Measure | Annual kWh Savings | Annual Cost Savings | Installed Cost | Simple Payback |
|---------|-------------------|--------------------| --------------|----------------|
| LED troffer retrofit (offices) | 98,400 | $7,077 | $42,500 | 6.0 years |

| **Total Lighting Package** | **173,500** | **$11,911** | **$77,900** | **6.5 years** |

**Note:** The above cost savings were calculated using a blended rate of $0.07/kWh. **No utility rebates were included in the analysis.** Duke Energy Carolinas offers a Smart $aver Custom Incentive program that provides $0.06/kWh for verified first-year kWh savings on lighting retrofits. This would provide an estimated rebate of **$10,410** (173,500 kWh x $0.06), reducing the net installed cost to **$67,490** and the simple payback to **4.1 years** (a 37% improvement in payback).

## Planted Issues (Evaluator Reference — Not Shown to Agent)

| # | Issue | What's Wrong | What Good Agent Should Do |
|---|-------|-------------|--------------------------|
| 1 | **HVAC running 24/7 including weekends** | BAS has no occupancy schedule configured. AHUs running at full capacity Saturday during site visit with 3 occupants. Estimated 25-35% HVAC energy waste. | Identify this as the single highest-impact, lowest-cost finding. Calculate approximate savings from implementing occupied/unoccupied schedules with temperature setbacks. Flag that this should have been #1 priority in the audit recommendations (auditor buried it at #2). |
| 2 | **Lighting retrofit ROI calculated without utility rebate** | Auditor used blended $0.07/kWh and did not include Duke Energy Smart $aver Custom Incentive ($0.06/kWh). The rebate would reduce payback from 6.5 years to 4.1 years — potentially changing the project decision. | Catch that the lighting retrofit analysis excludes available utility rebates. Recalculate or flag the need to recalculate with rebate included. Note that failing to include rebates understates savings and could kill a financially viable project. |
| 3 | **EUI 30% above ENERGY STAR median but report says "average performance"** | The building's EUI of 95.8 kBtu/sq ft/yr is 30% above the ENERGY STAR median for offices (73.7). The ENERGY STAR score is 28 (bottom quartile). Describing this as "average" is misleading. | Challenge the characterization. An ENERGY STAR score of 28 means the building performs worse than 72% of similar buildings nationally. This is poor performance, not average. The report should state clearly that the building is a below-average performer with significant improvement potential. |
| 4 | **Demand charges are 40% of electric bill but no demand management strategy** | Demand charges total $72,612/year (32.5% of electric cost). At $12.00/kW with a peak of 690 kW, demand cost is substantial. No demand management, peak shaving, or load shifting strategy is proposed. | Identify the demand charge gap. Recommend demand management strategies: battery peak shaving, HVAC pre-cooling, load staggering, demand limiting through BAS. Note that reducing peak demand by even 10% (69 kW) saves $828/month ($9,936/year) at zero energy reduction. |
| 5 | **Chiller is 18 years old at 0.85 kW/ton vs. modern 0.55 kW/ton** | The primary chiller (400 tons) operates at 0.85 kW/ton full load. Modern centrifugal chillers achieve 0.55 kW/ton full load and 0.35 kW/ton IPLV. The chiller replacement ROI is not calculated anywhere in the report. | Calculate the approximate savings from chiller replacement. At ~752,000 kWh cooling energy, a 35% efficiency improvement translates to substantial savings. Flag that an 18-year-old chiller is approaching end of useful life (typical: 20-25 years) and replacement planning should begin now, with energy savings factored into the ROI. |
| 6 | **200kW solar-ready roof but no renewable assessment** | The roof has been structurally assessed, has excellent solar access, and could support ~200 kW. Estimated production of 268,000 kWh/year worth ~$19,300/year, with $105,000 ITC available. The auditor explicitly noted "No solar feasibility assessment was included in this audit scope." | Flag the missing renewable assessment as a significant gap. A 200 kW system could offset 12.4% of the building's electricity consumption. With ITC and accelerated depreciation, the financial case is likely compelling. Recommend a detailed solar feasibility study with PVsyst modeling, structural confirmation, and financial analysis including ITC, MACRS, and utility program options. |
