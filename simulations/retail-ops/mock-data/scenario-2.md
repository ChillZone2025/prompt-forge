## PLANTED ISSUES (for evaluation — agent should catch these)

1. **Volume has exceeded the top pricing tier for 18 months but the discount was never triggered:** Summit Trail ordered 78,200 units in FY2024 and 84,500 units in FY2025, both exceeding the 75,001-unit threshold for Tier 4 pricing ($36.96/unit, 12% off list). However, all invoices were processed at the Tier 3 rate ($38.64/unit). The overpayment is $1.68/unit for 162,700 units = $273,336 total. A vendor negotiator should immediately flag this as a contract compliance issue and demand a retroactive rebate. The contract clearly defines the tier thresholds — this is not a negotiation point, it is money owed under existing terms. Whether the failure was AlpineForge's error or Summit Trail's failure to monitor invoices, the contractual obligation exists. This should be the opening item in any renegotiation — it establishes leverage and signals that Summit Trail is now paying close attention to contract compliance.

2. **Contract has an auto-renewal clause that triggers in 45 days — missing this deadline means being locked in for another year:** The MSA auto-renews on May 15, 2026 unless written notice is delivered 45 days prior (by March 31, 2026). The current date is late March 2026 — the deadline is imminent. If Summit Trail wants to renegotiate terms (and given the quality issues, pricing discrepancy, and raw material cost reductions, there are strong reasons to renegotiate), they must deliver written notice before March 31. Missing this deadline locks Summit Trail into the current terms for another year — including the Tier 3 pricing, the inadequate quality remedies, and the missing contractual provisions. A vendor negotiator should treat this as the highest-urgency action item in the entire package.

3. **Defect rate spiked to 4.2% last quarter but the SLA penalty clause requires written notification within 30 days (window may have passed):** The contract (Section 8.3) allows Summit Trail to assess a 1% quality penalty when defect rates exceed 2.0% in any rolling 6-month period, but requires written notice within 30 days of the measurement period's end. The Q3 FY2025 period ended September 30, 2025 — the notice window closed October 30, 2025. The Q4 period ended December 31, 2025 — the notice window closed January 30, 2026. It is now late March 2026. Both notification windows have expired. The $23,100 in potential quality penalties is likely no longer collectible. This is a process failure — Summit Trail has quality data showing SLA breaches but failed to invoke the contractual remedies within the required timeframe. A negotiator should (a) acknowledge this internally as a lesson learned, (b) implement a process for automated SLA breach notification, and (c) use the documented quality trend as leverage in renegotiation even though the formal penalty window has passed.

4. **Competitive quotes are for a different spec — not an apples-to-apples comparison:** Two of the three competitive quotes (PeakStitch and TrekWear) are for materially different product specifications. PeakStitch quoted on 600-fill duck down (vs. AlpineForge's 650-fill goose down) — goose down at higher fill power is a meaningfully superior insulator with better warmth-to-weight ratio. TrekWear quoted on 550-fill recycled polyester insulation — a completely different insulation category. Only SummitCraft (Quote 3) matches AlpineForge's actual spec. Presenting the PeakStitch ($34.50) or TrekWear ($31.80) prices as competitive alternatives would be misleading and would damage credibility if AlpineForge asks for spec sheets. A negotiator must either (a) obtain re-quotes from PeakStitch and TrekWear on the exact 650-fill goose down spec, or (b) only use the SummitCraft quote ($37.20) as a direct comparable. The SummitCraft price plus 25% Section 301 tariff ($46.50 landed) is actually higher than AlpineForge at the correct Tier 4 rate ($46.20 landed), which weakens the competitive pricing argument.

5. **The vendor's raw material costs have dropped 12% per market data but pricing hasn't been adjusted:** Goose down (35% of garment cost) has dropped 12.7% over 2 years. Nylon fabric (20% of cost) has dropped 12.2%. Zippers (8% of cost) have dropped 7.7%. The estimated total input cost reduction is approximately 7.5% of garment manufacturing cost. AlpineForge has not passed through any of this reduction — pricing has remained flat at $38.64/unit (or $36.96 at the Tier 4 rate that should have been applied). The contract does not include a raw material cost pass-through or adjustment clause, so there is no contractual obligation to reduce pricing. However, the data provides strong negotiating leverage: AlpineForge's margins have expanded by ~7.5% over 2 years purely from input cost deflation, while Summit Trail is paying the same price. In renegotiation, this data supports demanding a 5-8% price reduction on the base rate, or at minimum, adding a raw material index clause to the new contract.

6. **Contract doesn't include a force majeure clause or supply disruption contingency:** The current MSA has no force majeure clause and no supply disruption contingency plan. AlpineForge is located in Guangdong Province, China — a region subject to typhoon disruptions, potential trade restrictions, COVID-era lockdown precedent, and geopolitical risk. Summit Trail has 100% of its private-label insulated outerwear sourced from this single facility. If AlpineForge's factory experiences a fire, extended power outage, government-ordered closure, or port disruption, Summit Trail has no qualified backup supplier, no contractual protection, and would lose its highest-margin product category for potentially an entire selling season. The contract should include: (a) force majeure definition and notification requirements, (b) supply disruption contingency (e.g., AlpineForge must maintain a 30-day safety stock of finished goods or provide alternative production facility within 45 days), (c) business continuity requirements, and (d) the right to dual-source without violating exclusivity. The missing IP assignment clause compounds this risk — if the relationship ends, AlpineForge retains the patterns and construction specs for 8 proprietary Summit Trail styles.
