## PLANTED ISSUES (for evaluation — agent should catch these)

1. **Top-selling SKU has 3 weeks of supply but reorder lead time is 6 weeks (stockout incoming):** SKUs OW-1001 and OW-1002 (Alpine Down Parka, Arc'teryx) are the #1 and #2 revenue SKUs with only 3.1 and 2.9 weeks of supply respectively. Arc'teryx has a 35-day (5-week) average lead time with +/- 5 day variance (so potentially 6 weeks). No reorder is currently on order (On Order = 0). At current sell rates, these SKUs will stock out in approximately 3 weeks. Even if a purchase order is placed today, the earliest arrival would be ~6 weeks — creating a 3-week stockout gap on the highest-revenue product in the assortment. The stockout analysis already shows these SKUs lost $96,000 in Q4 from prior stockouts, yet no corrective action has been taken. A competent inventory optimizer would flag this as an emergency reorder with air freight consideration given the revenue impact.

2. **Q4 demand forecast was 40% off for seasonal category because it used last year's weather pattern:** The Q4 outerwear forecast missed by -40% ($4.8M forecast vs. $2.88M actual). The forecast methodology note reveals it was built on a 3-year weighted average with no weather adjustment, despite the Mountain West experiencing its mildest winter in 12 years (8-12F above normal). The same pattern affected winter footwear (-24%) and apparel (-35%). A demand planning agent should immediately flag that any seasonal outdoor retailer in the Mountain West MUST incorporate weather forecasts into demand models — historical averages are insufficient when the selling window depends on weather conditions. The resulting overstock ($60K+ in excess inventory at cost) and markdown expense are direct consequences of this forecasting failure.

3. **Vendor with worst on-time rate (62%) also has the lowest cost — nobody has renegotiated terms or sourced alternatives:** The North Face (via VF Corp) has declined from 78% OTIF in Q1 to 52% in Q4 — well below the 90% SLA target in every quarter. Despite this, no performance improvement plan has been requested, no chargebacks have been assessed ($0 in chargeback credits vs. thousands from better-performing vendors), and the contract is about to auto-renew on April 1. The justification appears to be that TNF offers a 2% lower cost basis than competitors. But the true cost is far higher: the 71% fill rate means 29% of ordered units aren't arriving, creating stockouts; the +/- 14 day lead time variance makes planning impossible; and the quality problems (see Issue #4) are generating massive return costs. A vendor negotiator should calculate the total cost of TNF's poor performance (lost sales from stockouts + return processing costs + markdown costs on unsold overstock) and compare that against the 2% cost advantage.

4. **Returns rate for one brand is 28% but buying continues at same level:** The North Face products have a 28% return rate vs. an industry average of 5.5%. The top return reasons are quality defects — zipper failure (38-48%), sole delamination (44-52%), seam separation, and waterproofing failure. This is not a sizing or customer preference issue; these are manufacturing defects. Despite this, the buyer's note states "We are maintaining our buy plan" based on a verbal assurance from VF Corp. A returns agent or supply chain agent should calculate the full cost impact: 6,944 return units x average handling cost + shipping + lost margin on non-resellable units. The 28% return rate means for every 4 units purchased, more than 1 comes back — the effective cost of TNF inventory is dramatically higher than the invoice price.

5. **Markdown effectiveness shows zero unit lift after 3 markdowns — should have been clearanced faster:** The Powder Bowl Jackets (OW-1045, OW-1046) were marked down three times (20%, 30%, 40% off) over 6 weeks with zero sustained unit lift. Weekly sales remained at 5-6 units regardless of price. This pattern indicates the product is dead stock at any reasonable price point — demand is not price-elastic for this item. The correct action was to skip the incremental markdown strategy and go directly to deep clearance (50-70% off) or liquidation channel after the first markdown failed to produce meaningful lift. Instead, the gradual approach burned 6 weeks of selling time and destroyed margin. At $209 current price, the remaining 207 units have already lost $140/unit from original retail ($349) with no end in sight. Total markdown cost so far: ~$29,000, with another $15,000-$20,000 needed to clear.

6. **E-commerce inventory is allocated separately from stores, creating artificial stockouts:** Inventory is allocated to channels as fixed pools at receiving based on prior year's channel mix (71/29 stores/e-commerce) with no in-season rebalancing and no ship-from-store capability. The data shows this creates artificial stockouts: Alpine Down Parka has 5.3 weeks of supply in e-commerce but only 1.8 weeks in stores (12 stockout days in stores while e-commerce had zero). Conversely, Merino Base Layer had 72 units in stores and 0 in e-commerce, causing 12 days of e-commerce stockouts. A store operations or supply chain agent should flag that the fixed allocation model is costing the business sales every week. The $153,700 in estimated lost sales from stockouts includes items where the other channel had ample inventory — unified inventory with ship-from-store or store-to-DC transfer capability would have prevented a significant portion of these losses.
