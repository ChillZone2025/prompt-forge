## PLANTED ISSUES (for evaluation — agent should catch these)

1. **Electrical subcontractor quote is 30% below competitors — likely missed scope (data cabling not included).** Sparks & Sons Electric quoted $82,500 with "full electrical per plans and specs" and no exclusions listed. The other two electrical subs (Front Range at $124,800 and Mile High at $118,200) both explicitly exclude data cabling (Section 27 10 00 — 168 Cat 6A drops, cable tray, 2 fiber runs, BICSI-certified testing). The data cabling scope alone is worth $35,000-$45,000 (168 drops x $200-$250/drop average for Cat 6A with testing). Sparks & Sons either missed this scope entirely or intentionally excluded it without documenting the exclusion. If the GC carries this bid and Sparks & Sons later claims data cabling was excluded, the GC absorbs a $35,000-$45,000 cost overrun — more than the entire GC fee on this project. The correct action is to request a detailed scope breakdown from Sparks & Sons before including their number in the bid.

2. **Drywall quantity takeoff missed a return wall shown only on the reflected ceiling plan.** The RCP (Sheet A2.1, Detail 3/A2.1) shows a full-height return wall at the server room extending from floor to deck above — 14 LF x 12' height. This wall is required for the fire rating and sound isolation of the server room but does not appear on the floor plan (A1.1) because it reads as a ceiling condition. The missed wall represents: 336 SF of 6" framing ($3.20/LF x 42 studs = additional framing), 672 SF of Type X drywall (both sides), taping, and finishing. Estimated underestimate: **$8,400** (framing $1,075 + drywall $1,411 + taping $571 + insulation $605 + fire caulking $420 + additional paint $387 + overhead/contingency on the additional scope ~$3,931). Takeoff errors from missing items on the RCP are one of the most common estimating mistakes in TI work.

3. **Schedule shows 12-week duration but doesn't account for permit approval timeline.** The schedule shows "Permit Submittal" as a 0-day milestone on May 15 and demolition starting the same day. In Denver, commercial tenant improvement permits require plan review by the Denver Building Department, which currently takes **3-4 weeks** for standard commercial TI (no variance required). The schedule cannot begin demolition without an approved permit. Actual duration is 15-16 weeks minimum (3-4 weeks permit review + 12 weeks construction). With a Required Completion of August 7, the project cannot start construction until approximately June 12 at earliest, pushing completion to **September 4** — 4 weeks past the contractual deadline. At $1,500/day liquidated damages, this is **$42,000 in LDs** if the GC doesn't negotiate the schedule before signing. The GC should either (a) submit for permit immediately (before contract execution) to front-load the review period, or (b) negotiate the contract start date to be tied to permit approval, not a fixed calendar date.

4. **Material pricing uses Q3 2025 rates but steel studs have increased 14% since then.** The unit costs for light-gauge metal framing ($1.85/LF for 3-5/8" and $3.20/LF for 6") reflect Q3 2025 RSMeans data. Since then, steel stud prices have increased approximately 14% due to tariff impacts and supply chain disruptions. At current Q1 2026 pricing, the correct rates are approximately $2.11/LF (3-5/8") and $3.65/LF (6"). The underestimate on framing alone: 4,680 LF x $0.26 = $1,217 + 320 LF x $0.45 = $144 = **$1,361** on framing materials. While this single line item isn't catastrophic, the same outdated pricing likely affects other steel-dependent items (door frames, ceiling grid, metal accessories). A thorough price update across all metal-dependent line items could reveal $3,000-$5,000 in total underestimate. Professional estimators should use real-time supplier quotes or apply escalation factors when pricing is more than 90 days old.

5. **No contingency line item for unforeseen conditions.** This is a tenant improvement project in an existing building. Standard industry practice is to carry 5-10% contingency for TI work to cover unforeseen conditions: hidden utility conflicts above the ceiling, asbestos in existing fireproofing, structural conditions requiring additional framing support, base building systems that don't perform as documented. The bid total of $631,196 includes zero contingency. On a $575,804 subtotal, 5% contingency would be **$28,790** and 10% would be **$57,580**. Without contingency, every unforeseen condition becomes either a change order (which the owner may dispute, especially under a lump sum contract) or a cost the GC absorbs. The GC should add a contingency line item or, at minimum, include contingency language in the contract allowing for unforeseen condition change orders with defined markup rates.

6. **Labor rates don't reflect prevailing wage requirements — this is a government-leased building subject to Davis-Bacon.** The bid documents do not mention prevailing wage, and the labor rates used in the estimate are open-shop (non-prevailing wage) rates. However, 1200 Federal Plaza is a GSA-leased building. Under the Davis-Bacon Act, all construction work on federally owned or leased property requires payment of prevailing wages as determined by the Department of Labor. Even though the 4th floor tenant (Meridian Financial Group) is a private company, the building's federal lease status triggers Davis-Bacon applicability for all construction within the building. Denver-area prevailing wage rates for relevant trades (carpenter, electrician, plumber, sheet metal worker, painter) are 40-65% higher than open-shop rates. The labor component of this estimate is approximately $230,000-$260,000 (roughly 40-45% of the subtotal for a TI project). If prevailing wage applies, the labor increase would be approximately **$92,000-$169,000** — a catastrophic underestimate that would eliminate all profit and create a six-figure loss on the project. The GC must verify Davis-Bacon applicability with the building owner and GSA before submitting the bid. If Davis-Bacon applies, the entire estimate must be repriced using DOL prevailing wage rates for Denver, CO, and certified payroll must be submitted weekly.
