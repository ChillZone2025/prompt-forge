## PLANTED ISSUES (for evaluation — agent should catch these)

1. **Fall protection violation — 3 workers on scaffold at 14 feet without harnesses or guardrails.** Three drywall subcontractor workers were observed on a tubular frame scaffold at the elevator shaft opening at 14 feet above the floor 2 slab. One worker was wearing a harness but was not connected to any anchor point. Two workers had no harness at all, and one also had no hard hat. The scaffold had no guardrails (top rail, mid rail, or toe board), no inspection tag, and was missing cross-bracing on one bay. This violates OSHA 29 CFR 1926.451(g)(1) (scaffold fall protection above 10 feet), 1926.501(b)(1) (general fall protection above 6 feet), and the site's own Fall Protection Plan requiring 100% tie-off. This is a **serious violation** under OSHA's severity classification — workers were exposed to a hazard likely to cause death or serious physical harm. Penalties for serious violations: up to $16,131 per instance (2026 rates). With 3 workers, this could be cited as 3 instances or as a single violation depending on the Area Director's assessment. The missing scaffold tag and absent cross-bracing also indicate the scaffold was not inspected by a competent person before use, violating 1926.451(f)(7). If OSHA determines the GC had knowledge and failed to act, this could escalate from serious to willful ($156,259 maximum per violation).

2. **Excavation at 7.5 feet with no protective system — potential willful violation.** A utility trench at 7.5 feet deep has vertical walls with no sloping, shoring, or trench box. Two workers were observed inside the trench. Under 29 CFR 1926.652(a), excavations 5 feet or deeper require a protective system unless the excavation is in stable rock (this site has clay soil). The violation is compounded by: (a) no documented soil classification by the competent person, (b) last daily inspection was 7 days ago (1926.651(k)(1) requires daily inspection), (c) no post-rain inspection after 1.2 inches of rainfall on March 23 — rain dramatically increases cave-in risk in clay soils, (d) inadequate egress — only one ladder at the north end, 75 feet from the south end workers (1926.651(c)(2) requires a ladder within 25 feet of lateral travel). Cave-in is one of the most lethal construction hazards — soil weighing 100+ pounds per cubic foot can crush a worker in seconds. This trench has multiple simultaneous violations: no protective system, no inspection, no post-rain inspection, no soil classification, inadequate egress. If OSHA determines the employer knew about the excavation standard and failed to comply (the site has a written Excavation Safety Plan and the competent person is the superintendent), this could be classified as a **willful violation** ($156,259 per violation). A fatality in this trench would result in criminal prosecution.

3. **GFCIs not tested monthly as required — last test 4 months ago.** The site's Assured Equipment Grounding Conductor Program (required by 29 CFR 1926.404(b)(1)(iii)) specifies monthly GFCI testing. All temporary power GFCIs were last tested on November 15, 2025 — 4 months before the audit date. OSHA 1926.404(b)(1)(ii) requires the employer to designate a competent person to implement the testing program. Untested GFCIs may not trip on a ground fault, exposing workers to electrocution — the fourth-leading cause of construction fatalities (OSHA "Focus Four"). The floor 3 spider box has never been tested. This is an other-than-serious or serious violation depending on OSHA's assessment of the probability of harm. The practical risk: if a worker using a defective tool on a wet floor gets electrocuted and the GFCI didn't trip because it was never tested, the employer faces both OSHA citation and potential wrongful death litigation. The fix is simple and inexpensive — a GFCI tester costs $15 and testing takes minutes. The fact that it hasn't been done suggests the safety program is documentation-focused rather than action-focused.

4. **Crane annual inspection certificate expired 17 days ago — crane in daily use.** The Liebherr LTM 1100-5.2 crane's annual inspection certificate expired on March 8, 2026. The crane has been in daily use for steel erection for 17 days on an expired certificate. Under 29 CFR 1926.1412(a), cranes must be inspected by a qualified person at least every 12 months. While the monthly and daily inspections are current, the annual inspection is a comprehensive examination of structural components, wire rope, hydraulic systems, safety devices, and load-limiting systems that cannot be replicated by daily visual checks. The crane rental company acknowledged the issue on March 10 but has not provided a specific inspection date. The site safety officer and superintendent are aware (the monthly inspection log notes the annual is due), yet the crane continues to operate. Operating a crane with an expired annual inspection certificate is a regulatory violation (1926.1412(a)) and a serious liability exposure. If a crane failure occurs during this period — a load drop, a boom failure, an outrigger failure — the expired inspection becomes the central fact in the OSHA investigation and any wrongful death/injury litigation. The crane should be taken out of service until the annual inspection is completed. Steel erection will be delayed, but the schedule impact is preferable to the consequences of a crane incident during an uninspected period.

5. **Toolbox talk attendance shows same 8 workers every week but site has 24 workers — 67% non-participation.** Every toolbox talk in March had exactly the same 8 attendees — all Ironclad (GC) employees. Zero subcontractor workers have attended any toolbox talk. The site averages 24 workers daily, meaning 67% of the workforce receives no weekly safety training. This is particularly concerning because: (a) the subcontractors are performing the highest-risk work (scaffold, steel erection, excavation), (b) the 3 workers on the scaffold without fall protection are drywall subcontractor employees who never received the Fall Protection toolbox talk, (c) the workers in the unprotected trench may not have attended the Excavation Safety toolbox talk, (d) no subcontractor safety orientation sign-in sheets are on file for workers starting after January 2026. Under the multi-employer worksite doctrine (OSHA Instruction CPL 02-00-124), the GC as the controlling employer has responsibility for ensuring all workers on site receive adequate safety training, regardless of who employs them. The GC cannot defend against OSHA citations for subcontractor violations by claiming "they're not our employees" if the GC failed to enforce its own safety program. The toolbox talk program is functioning as a documentation exercise for GC employees only, not as an actual safety training system for the entire site.

6. **Fire extinguishers in the job trailer and Floor 2 west corridor have expired inspection tags — 3 months overdue.** Three of eight fire extinguishers (job trailer main, job trailer plan room, and Floor 2 west corridor) have inspection tags dated December 2025 — 3 months overdue for monthly visual inspection per NFPA 10 and OSHA 1926.150(a). Additionally, Floor 3 (where hot work potential exists from steel erection and welding) has no fire extinguisher at all. First aid kits were last inspected in December 2025 and Floor 2 (with 8+ MEP workers) has no first aid kit. The last emergency evacuation drill was October 2025 — 5 months ago against a quarterly requirement. These are individually minor citations, but collectively they paint a picture of a safety program that was set up at project start and has not been maintained. The expired extinguishers, missing Floor 3 coverage, and lapsed drill schedule suggest the safety officer's attention is focused elsewhere — or that safety program maintenance has fallen off as the project has progressed and pressure to maintain schedule has increased.
