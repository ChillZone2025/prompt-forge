## Section 1: Audit Scope and Methodology

### 1.1 Scope

This audit assessed the design and operating effectiveness of VIS's Anti-Bribery & Corruption ("ABC") compliance program across the following domains:

- ABC Policy and Code of Conduct
- Tone from the top and management commitment
- Risk assessment methodology and outputs
- Training and communication program
- Third-party due diligence and management
- Gifts, hospitality, and entertainment controls
- Books and records / internal accounting controls
- Reporting channels and investigation procedures
- Monitoring and continuous improvement
- M&A due diligence integration

### 1.2 Methodology

| Activity | Detail |
|----------|--------|
| **Document Review** | ABC Policy, Code of Conduct, third-party due diligence files, training records, gift/entertainment logs, investigation files, board/committee minutes |
| **Interviews** | Chief Compliance Officer, General Counsel, CFO, 5 country managers (Brazil, Indonesia, Nigeria, Mexico, Vietnam), 3 regional sales directors, 8 front-line sales representatives |
| **Transaction Testing** | Sample of 120 third-party payments (travel, entertainment, consulting, commissions) from Q1-Q4 2026 |
| **Data Analytics** | Statistical analysis of gift/entertainment spending patterns, training completion rates, hotline metrics |
| **Benchmarking** | Comparison of program elements against DOJ guidance (June 2023 update) and industry peer programs |

### 1.3 Audit Rating Scale

| Rating | Definition |
|--------|-----------|
| **Effective** | Controls are well-designed and operating effectively. Minor observations only. |
| **Needs Improvement** | Controls are designed but have gaps in implementation or operating effectiveness. Moderate risk. |
| **Deficient** | Controls are absent, poorly designed, or significantly failing in operation. High risk requiring immediate remediation. |

### 6.4 Observations on Investigation Quality

**Finding: Two ABC-related reports (HR-2026-014 and HR-2026-019) were closed with inadequate investigation documentation.**

- **HR-2026-014** (Jakarta gift allegation): The investigation was conducted by the local HR team reporting to the subject of the allegation (Country Manager Rina Suryadi manages the Indonesia office, including HR). No independent investigator was assigned. The investigation file lacks witness interviews, document review, or any corroborating analysis. The DOJ guidance specifically states that investigations should be conducted by personnel with "sufficient independence from management."

- **HR-2026-019** (Brazil commission allegation): A compliance report alleging irregular commission payments to a Brazilian distributor was referred to Finance without a formal compliance investigation. The Finance response ("within contractual terms") does not address the FCPA risk dimension of the allegation. The commission payments could be contractually authorized and still constitute corrupt payments if the funds were intended to be passed through to a government official. **This report, combined with the information in Section 4.2 regarding Andrade & Lima and Section 5.3 regarding government entertainment in Brazil, suggests a pattern that warrants a comprehensive investigation.**

## Section 7: Books and Records / Internal Accounting Controls

### 7.1 Transaction Testing Results

A sample of 120 third-party payments was tested for proper authorization, documentation, business purpose, and coding accuracy.

| Test | Sample | Findings |
|------|--------|----------|
| Proper authorization (signature/approval per delegation of authority) | 120 | 4 exceptions — payments approved by individuals without delegated authority |
| Adequate documentation (invoice, PO, receipt of services) | 120 | 7 exceptions — 3 missing invoices, 4 with insufficient description of services rendered |
| Valid business purpose documented | 120 | 5 exceptions — payments described as "market development" or "business facilitation" without further explanation |
| Accurate GL coding | 120 | 8 exceptions — 6 commission payments coded to "consulting fees," 2 entertainment expenses coded to "training" |

### 7.2 Observations

The miscoding of commission payments as "consulting fees" (6 instances) prevents effective monitoring of commission spending patterns. Commission payments are subject to enhanced scrutiny under VIS's ABC program due to their potential use as conduit for improper payments. Coding them to a different category effectively removes them from automated monitoring controls.

## Section 9: Overall Assessment and Remediation Recommendations

### 9.1 Overall Program Rating: **Needs Improvement**

The VIS ABC compliance program is adequately designed in most respects but has significant operational effectiveness gaps that create meaningful FCPA and UK Bribery Act exposure. The combination of third-party due diligence gaps, training non-compliance among high-risk personnel, inadequate investigation procedures, and stale risk assessment and policy documentation would likely result in the DOJ concluding that the program is not being "applied earnestly and in good faith" and does not "work in practice."
