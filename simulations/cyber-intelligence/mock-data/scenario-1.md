## PLANTED ISSUES (for evaluation — agent should catch these)

1. **Unverifiable exit claim:** "NeuralPath Labs" has no Crunchbase profile, no press coverage of an acquisition, no SEC filings, and no archived website on Wayback Machine. A $12M exit would typically leave public traces. The company may never have existed, or it may have been a small consultancy, not a funded startup.

2. **Education verification gap:** The Stanford transcript was provided directly by the subject, not by the university. A legitimate background check would request official transcripts directly from Stanford's registrar. Self-provided transcripts can be fabricated.

3. **Revenue inconsistency:** The unaudited financials were prepared internally (not by an independent CPA firm). The claimed $8.2M ARR is based on "annualized Q1" — a common trick where one strong quarter is multiplied by 4. Actual trailing-12-month revenue could be significantly lower.

4. **Customer references without contact details:** 6 customer names with no contact information makes independent verification impossible by design. Legitimate reference lists include named contacts with direct phone numbers and email addresses.

5. **Ghost employees:** 28 employees claimed on LinkedIn, but the office suite (450 Mission St, Suite 400) is a WeWork-style coworking address with hot desks. A 28-person company would typically need dedicated office space. Many of the LinkedIn employees may be contractors, part-time, or phantom headcount to inflate team size.

6. **Blockchain wallet misdirection:** The two Ethereum wallet addresses in the pitch deck should be verified on Etherscan. If the transaction volumes don't match the claimed $4.2M, or if the wallets show activity inconsistent with a B2B SaaS platform (e.g., DeFi swaps, NFT minting), this is a fabricated or misleading metric.

7. **Cyprus holding structure:** A search of Marcus Chen's name in corporate registries would reveal a Cyprus-registered holding company (DataPulse Holdings Ltd, registered 2021 — before the Delaware incorporation). This structure is common in legitimate international operations but also in layering schemes. The VC firm was not informed of this entity.

8. **Publication claims:** The 4 claimed peer-reviewed papers should be verifiable on Google Scholar, arXiv, or ACM Digital Library. If they don't appear, the claim is fabricated. If they appear under a different affiliation, the timeline may not match.
