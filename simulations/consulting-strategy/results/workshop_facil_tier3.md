# Workshop Corner-Cutting: Strategic Analysis & Risk Assessment

## THE BRUTAL TRUTH ABOUT SHORTCUTS

**Your boss's request makes business sense in some contexts... and will backfire spectacularly in others.** Let me break down what you're actually risking:

### **CORNERS THAT CAN BE CUT (Relatively Safely):**

**Pre-Workshop Prep:**
- Skip elaborate welcome packets → Use simple email brief
- Reduce participant interviews → Survey instead of 1:1s
- Simplify materials → Digital-only instead of printed + digital

**Design Complexity:**
- Use proven templates → Skip custom framework development  
- Fewer breakout variations → Stick to 2-3 reliable exercises
- Basic Miro boards → Simple sticky-note layouts vs. sophisticated interactions

**Post-Workshop Polish:**
- Immediate recap → Skip detailed synthesis document
- Action items in email → Skip formatted deliverable package

**Potential time savings:** 40-50% of design time, minimal risk if participants are collaborative and aligned.

### **CORNERS THAT WILL DESTROY OUTCOMES:**

**Stakeholder Analysis:**
- **Skip this = workshop becomes expensive therapy session**
- Hidden agendas surface mid-session, derailing progress
- Wrong people make commitments they can't keep

**Decision Architecture:**
- **Rush this = everyone leaves confused about next steps**
- Feels like progress but nothing actually gets decided
- Follow-up meetings multiply to resolve what workshop should have

**Group Dynamics Preparation:**
- **Ignore this = senior person dominates or conflict explodes**
- Junior voices get silenced, losing critical insights
- Tension between departments becomes public drama

---

## RISK SCENARIOS BY WORKSHOP TYPE

### **HIGH-RISK Shortcuts (Don't do it):**
- **Stakeholder alignment with executives** → Politics will eat you alive
- **Strategic planning with budget implications** → Decisions will be revisited/reversed
- **Cross-functional problem-solving** → Blame games emerge post-workshop

### **MEDIUM-RISK Shortcuts (Proceed with caution):**
- **Team retrospectives** → May miss root causes but won't explode
- **Innovation brainstorming** → Fewer breakthrough ideas but still productive
- **Process improvement sessions** → Incremental vs. transformational outcomes

### **LOW-RISK Shortcuts (Probably fine):**
- **Information sharing meetings** → Already low expectations
- **Routine planning sessions** → Established relationships and processes
- **Training/capability building** → Learning happens regardless

## STRATEGIC RESPONSE OPTIONS

### **OPTION 1: Negotiated Scope Reduction**
**What to say:** *"I can deliver a solid workshop faster by focusing on [specific outcome]. This means we'll [specific trade-off] but still achieve [key business goal]."*

**Example:** Instead of 2-day strategic planning → 4-hour decision workshop focused only on Q1 priorities.

### **OPTION 2: Risk-Based Pushback**
**What to say:** *"I can cut design time, but I need 30 minutes with you to identify which risks we're accepting. Some shortcuts could cost us credibility with [specific stakeholders]."*

**Key point:** Make the trade-offs explicit and get agreement on what "good enough" looks like.

### **OPTION 3: Minimum Viable Workshop**
**What to say:** *"Let me design the simplest version that still delivers business value. If it works, we can iterate. If participants need more structure, we'll know for next time."*

**Approach:** Proven template + basic facilitation + clear outcomes focus.
