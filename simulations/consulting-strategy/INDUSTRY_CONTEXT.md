# Industry Context: Consulting & Strategy

## What This Industry Does

Management consulting and strategy advisory is the practice of helping organizations solve complex business problems, make high-stakes decisions, and execute transformational change. The field spans strategy consulting (market entry, M&A, corporate strategy), management consulting (operations, organization design, digital transformation), and financial advisory (due diligence, valuation, restructuring).

The top firms — McKinsey, BCG, Bain (MBB), Deloitte, Accenture, the Big Four advisory arms — set the standard, but the industry also includes boutique strategy firms, independent consultants, and in-house strategy teams at large corporations. Clients range from Fortune 500 CEOs evaluating billion-dollar acquisitions to PE firms conducting due diligence on portfolio targets to government agencies designing policy frameworks.

The core deliverable is structured analysis that drives decisions. Not opinions — analysis. The difference matters.

## Key Tools & Frameworks

### Strategic Analysis Frameworks
- **Porter's Five Forces** — Evaluates industry attractiveness by analyzing competitive rivalry, supplier power, buyer power, threat of substitutes, and threat of new entrants. Used to determine whether an industry is structurally profitable.
- **McKinsey 7S Model** — Internal alignment framework: Strategy, Structure, Systems, Shared Values, Skills, Style, Staff. Used for organizational assessments and post-merger integration planning.
- **BCG Growth-Share Matrix** — Plots business units or products on market growth rate vs. relative market share (Stars, Cash Cows, Question Marks, Dogs). Used for portfolio strategy and resource allocation.
- **SWOT Analysis** — Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, Threats. The most overused and most misused framework in consulting — useful only when each quadrant is backed by data, not brainstorming.
- **PESTEL Analysis** — Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Environmental, Legal factors. Macro-environment scanning for market entry and strategic planning.
- **Value Chain Analysis (Porter)** — Maps primary activities (inbound logistics, operations, outbound logistics, marketing/sales, service) and support activities (procurement, technology, HR, infrastructure) to identify competitive advantage sources.
- **Ansoff Matrix** — Growth strategy framework: Market Penetration, Market Development, Product Development, Diversification. Used for evaluating organic growth paths.
- **Blue Ocean Strategy** — Framework for creating uncontested market space rather than competing in existing markets. Value innovation through simultaneous differentiation and cost reduction.

### Market Sizing & Financial Analysis
- **TAM/SAM/SOM** — Total Addressable Market, Serviceable Addressable Market, Serviceable Obtainable Market. The foundational hierarchy for market sizing. TAM without SAM/SOM is meaningless.
- **Top-Down vs. Bottom-Up Sizing** — Top-down starts from macro data and narrows; bottom-up starts from unit economics and scales. Credible market sizing requires both approaches and triangulation.
- **Discounted Cash Flow (DCF)** — Intrinsic valuation method projecting future free cash flows and discounting to present value. Standard for M&A valuation.
- **Comparable Company Analysis (Comps)** — Relative valuation using trading multiples (EV/Revenue, EV/EBITDA, P/E) from peer companies.
- **Precedent Transaction Analysis** — Valuation based on multiples paid in prior acquisitions of similar companies.

### Problem-Solving Methodologies
- **MECE (Mutually Exclusive, Collectively Exhaustive)** — The structuring principle. Every analysis must break a problem into non-overlapping categories that cover all possibilities. If your structure isn't MECE, your analysis has gaps or double-counts.
- **Issue Trees** — Hierarchical decomposition of a core question into sub-questions. The backbone of hypothesis-driven consulting.
- **Hypothesis-Driven Problem Solving** — Start with an answer hypothesis, then test it against data. The opposite of "boiling the ocean" (collecting all data before forming a view).
- **Pyramid Principle (Barbara Minto)** — Communication structure: lead with the answer, then group supporting arguments, then provide evidence. Top-down, not bottom-up.
- **80/20 Rule (Pareto Principle)** — Focus analysis on the 20% of factors that drive 80% of impact. Essential for managing scope.

### Due Diligence Frameworks
- **Commercial Due Diligence** — Market assessment, competitive positioning, customer validation, revenue quality. Answers: "Is the business sustainable?"
- **Financial Due Diligence** — Quality of earnings, working capital normalization, debt-like items, pro forma adjustments. Answers: "Are the numbers real?"
- **Operational Due Diligence** — Processes, technology, supply chain, key person dependencies. Answers: "Can the business execute?"
- **Legal Due Diligence** — Contracts, litigation, IP, regulatory compliance. Answers: "What liabilities exist?"
- **Integration Planning** — Day 1 readiness, synergy capture, cultural integration, technology migration. Answers: "Can we merge without destroying value?"

## Real Platforms & Data Sources

### Market Intelligence
- **Gartner** — Technology research and advisory. Magic Quadrant reports are the standard for vendor landscape analysis in tech-adjacent strategy work.
- **CB Insights** — Startup and venture capital data. Used for competitive landscape mapping in emerging markets.
- **PitchBook** — Private market data: VC/PE deals, valuations, fund performance. Essential for M&A advisory and market sizing.
- **Capital IQ (S&P)** — Financial data, screening, and analytics. Used by investment banks and PE firms for comps and precedent transactions.
- **Bloomberg Terminal** — Real-time financial data, news, and analytics. The gold standard for capital markets intelligence.
- **Statista** — Market data aggregator. Useful for quick sizing but should always be triangulated with primary sources.
- **IBISWorld** — Industry research reports with revenue, growth, and competitive landscape data by NAICS code.
- **Euromonitor (Passport)** — Consumer market data, particularly strong in FMCG, retail, and emerging markets.

### Consulting-Specific Resources
- **McKinsey Global Institute (MGI)** — Macroeconomic and cross-sector research. Sets the agenda on topics like automation, productivity, and global trade.
- **Bain Insights** — Proprietary research and frameworks (e.g., Net Promoter System, Repeatable Models).
- **BCG Henderson Institute** — Strategy research, particularly on adaptive strategy and AI.
- **Harvard Business Review** — Peer-reviewed management research. Reference for frameworks and case studies.
- **McKinsey Solutions** — Proprietary analytics platforms (Periscope for pricing, OrgSolutions for organizational design).

### Regulatory & Compliance
- **SEC EDGAR** — US public company filings (10-K, 10-Q, proxy statements). Primary source for financial due diligence on public targets.
- **OECD Reports** — Cross-border regulatory and economic data for international market entry strategy.
- **World Bank Doing Business / B-Ready** — Country-level regulatory environment data for market entry assessments.

## Regulations & Professional Standards

- **SOX (Sarbanes-Oxley Act)** — Governs financial reporting and internal controls for public companies. Critical context for financial due diligence — SOX compliance status affects risk assessment.
- **Antitrust Review Requirements** — Hart-Scott-Rodino (HSR) Act requires FTC/DOJ notification for acquisitions above threshold ($119.5M in 2026). EU equivalent: EUMR (European Union Merger Regulation). Consultants must flag antitrust risk in M&A engagements.
- **Confidentiality / NDA Standards** — Consulting engagements operate under strict NDA. Client data, findings, and even the existence of the engagement may be confidential. Data rooms use platforms like Intralinks or Datasite.
- **Conflict of Interest Policies** — Firms cannot advise both sides of a transaction. Must disclose prior or concurrent engagements with competitors of the client.
- **Management Consultancies Association (MCA)** — UK industry body with ethical standards and certification for consulting firms.
- **Institute of Management Consultants (IMC)** — US professional body offering the CMC (Certified Management Consultant) designation.
- **FCPA (Foreign Corrupt Practices Act)** — Relevant for international market entry advisory. Consultants must flag bribery and corruption risk in target markets.
- **GDPR** — Data protection implications for due diligence involving European targets or customers. Customer data review in DD must comply.

## Common Terminology

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Engagement letter** | Formal agreement defining scope, fees, timeline, and deliverables for a consulting project |
| **SOW (Statement of Work)** | Detailed scope document specifying deliverables, milestones, and responsibilities |
| **Deliverable** | Tangible output of a workstream — a report, presentation, model, or recommendation set |
| **Workstream** | A distinct track of work within a larger engagement (e.g., "commercial DD workstream") |
| **Swim lane** | Visual representation of parallel workstreams showing ownership and timing |
| **MECE** | Mutually Exclusive, Collectively Exhaustive — the structuring standard |
| **Issue tree** | Hierarchical breakdown of a problem into testable sub-questions |
| **Hypothesis** | A testable answer to a business question, stated before analysis begins |
| **Straw man** | An initial draft or proposal designed to be challenged and refined, not defended |
| **Tiger team** | A small, high-intensity task force assembled for a specific urgent problem |
| **War room** | Dedicated physical or virtual space for intensive collaborative work on a critical project |
| **Red team** | A group assigned to challenge assumptions, find flaws, and stress-test a strategy |
| **Blue sky** | Unconstrained brainstorming without regard to feasibility — used early in ideation |
| **Boil the ocean** | Attempting to analyze everything instead of focusing on the highest-impact areas — a cardinal sin |
| **Deck** | A PowerPoint/Keynote presentation — the primary deliverable format in consulting |
| **One-pager** | A single-page executive summary distilling a complex topic into key takeaways |
| **Tearsheet** | A one-page company or asset profile used for quick reference in deal contexts |
| **Comp set** | Comparable companies or transactions used as benchmarks for valuation or positioning |
| **Run rate** | Annualized revenue or expense figure based on a recent period (often misleading if that period is atypical) |
| **Synergies** | Cost savings or revenue gains expected from combining two businesses post-merger |
| **White space** | Unserved or underserved market segments representing growth opportunity |
| **Adjacency** | A market, product, or capability that is near the company's current core and represents a logical expansion |
| **Quality of earnings (QoE)** | Analysis separating sustainable, recurring earnings from one-time items, adjustments, and accounting choices |
| **Pro forma** | Financial statements adjusted to reflect a hypothetical scenario (e.g., post-acquisition combined entity) |
| **Haircut** | A reduction applied to a projected figure to account for risk or optimism bias |

## Common Mistakes in This Field

1. **Scope creep without re-scoping** — Allowing the engagement to expand beyond the original SOW without renegotiating timeline, fees, or resources. The client keeps asking "one more thing" and the team keeps saying yes until the project is undeliverable.

2. **Boiling the ocean** — Collecting exhaustive data before forming a hypothesis. Hypothesis-driven consulting requires forming a view early and testing it, not waiting until all data is in. Teams that boil the ocean run out of time before reaching actionable insights.

3. **Confirmation bias in market sizing** — Starting with a desired market size (often the client's internal estimate) and working backward to justify it. Top-down and bottom-up estimates that magically converge on the same number should be treated with suspicion.

4. **Presenting data without insight** — Slides full of charts and tables that describe what happened but never say what it means or what to do about it. Data is not analysis. Analysis is not insight. Insight is not recommendation.

5. **Failing to pressure-test assumptions** — Accepting client-provided data, management projections, or industry reports without stress-testing key assumptions. What happens if growth is 15% instead of 25%? What if the largest customer churns? Sensitivity analysis is not optional.

6. **Ignoring implementation feasibility** — Recommending a strategy that is theoretically optimal but operationally impossible given the client's resources, culture, or timeline. The best strategy the client can't execute is worse than a good strategy they can.

7. **Misaligning deliverables with client decision-making timeline** — Delivering a perfect analysis two weeks after the board vote it was supposed to inform. Consulting output must be timed to decision points, not to when the analysis feels "complete."

8. **Overcomplicating frameworks** — Using a 5-layer framework when a 2x2 matrix would communicate the same insight more clearly. Frameworks are tools for thinking, not demonstrations of intellectual sophistication.

9. **Using jargon with non-consulting clients** — Talking about "MECE issue trees" and "hypothesis-driven approaches" to a manufacturing CEO who just wants to know whether to build a new plant. Match the language to the audience.

10. **Neglecting change management** — Delivering a brilliant strategy with no plan for how the organization will actually adopt it. Most strategies fail not because the analysis was wrong but because the implementation was never designed for real humans in a real organization.

## Excellent vs. Dangerous Work

### Excellent Consulting Work
- **Structures the problem before solving it.** MECE issue tree, clear hypotheses, defined analyses per branch. The client knows what questions are being answered and why.
- **Leads with the "so what."** Every slide, every page, every email answers the question: "So what should the client do differently?" Data supports the recommendation, not the other way around.
- **Triangulates from multiple sources.** Market sizing uses top-down AND bottom-up. Customer insights combine interviews, survey data, and behavioral analytics. Competitor analysis uses public filings, expert interviews, and primary research.
- **Pressure-tests its own conclusions.** Red-teams the recommendation, runs sensitivity analysis on the financial model, identifies the three things most likely to make the strategy fail, and addresses them.
- **Designs for implementation.** The recommendation includes who does what, by when, with what resources, and what the first 90 days look like. Not just "enter the market" but a phased plan with decision gates.
- **Communicates at the client's level.** A board presentation is different from a working team session. The same content, structured differently, for different audiences.

### Dangerous Consulting Work
- **Starts with the answer the client wants to hear.** Working backward from a predetermined conclusion — especially in DD, where the PE firm has already decided to buy and wants the DD to confirm.
- **Presents data as analysis.** Fifty slides of market data with no synthesized view, no prioritization, no recommendation. Looks thorough, is useless.
- **Ignores disconfirming evidence.** Finding data that contradicts the hypothesis and burying it rather than updating the recommendation. This is how billion-dollar acquisition mistakes happen.
- **Over-relies on a single framework.** Applying Porter's Five Forces to every problem regardless of fit. Frameworks are lenses, not answers.
- **Delivers without implementation support.** "Here's your strategy deck, good luck" — then the firm moves on while the client struggles to execute.
- **Uses complexity as a shield.** Making the analysis deliberately complex so the client can't challenge it. If the client can't understand the logic, the work isn't good — it's obscure.

## Certifications & Credentials

- **CMC (Certified Management Consultant)** — Awarded by the Institute of Management Consultants (IMC). Requires 3+ years of consulting experience, client references, peer review, and adherence to a code of ethics. The only globally recognized consulting credential via ICMCI.
- **PMP (Project Management Professional)** — PMI certification relevant for engagement management, particularly in implementation-heavy consulting.
- **MBA (Top Programs)** — Harvard, Wharton, Stanford GSB, INSEAD, London Business School, Kellogg, Booth, Columbia. MBB firms recruit heavily from top MBA programs. The MBA is less a certification and more a signaling and network credential.
- **CFA (Chartered Financial Analyst)** — Three-level exam covering financial analysis, valuation, and portfolio management. Essential for financial advisory and M&A consulting.
- **Big Four Certifications** — Deloitte, PwC, EY, and KPMG each have internal certification programs for advisory and strategy practices.
- **Lean Six Sigma (Green/Black Belt)** — Process improvement methodology certification. Relevant for operations consulting and continuous improvement engagements.
- **CISA (Certified Information Systems Auditor)** — Relevant for technology due diligence and IT advisory consulting.
