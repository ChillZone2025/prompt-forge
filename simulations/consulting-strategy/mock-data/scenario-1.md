## PLANTED ISSUES (for evaluation — agent should catch these)

1. **Market sizing uses top-down only with no bottom-up validation.** The TAM of $4.2B is extrapolated from a 2024 Frost & Sullivan report at an assumed 10% growth rate. There is no bottom-up calculation (number of hospitals x average device spend x addressable product lines). No SAM or SOM is calculated — the company cannot realistically address a $4.2B market with $15M investment and no local brand recognition. A credible SOM for Year 1-3 would be 0.5-2% of the relevant sub-segments, not the total market.

2. **Regulatory analysis missing for Vietnam.** The regulatory requirements table lists Vietnam as "Similar to Thailand process" with no registration body, no timeline, and no specifics. In reality, Vietnam requires VIMEC (Vietnam Import-Export of Medical Equipment) registration through the Ministry of Health, and Class C/D devices (which surgical instruments typically are) require 18-24 months for approval. This directly conflicts with the 18-month go-to-market timeline that shows Vietnam revenue starting in Year 2 (month 13-18). The CEO's stated preference for Vietnam as the anchor market makes this gap particularly dangerous — the timeline is physically impossible.

3. **Currency risk unaddressed.** All financial projections are in USD only. Revenue will be collected in Thai Baht (THB), Vietnamese Dong (VND), Indonesian Rupiah (IDR), and Philippine Peso (PHP). No hedging strategy is discussed. The VND has depreciated 15% against USD over the past 5 years, and IDR has shown 8-12% annual volatility. A 10% adverse currency movement across the portfolio would reduce Year 5 projected revenue by $6.9M — wiping out nearly half the projected 18% margin.

4. **Competitor data is 2 years old.** All competitive landscape data cites "Frost & Sullivan 2024 data" and "competitor annual reports, 2024 data." As of 2026, these figures are two years stale. Post-COVID, Southeast Asian medical device markets have seen significant shifts — local manufacturers have gained share, Chinese competitors (Mindray, United Imaging) have entered aggressively, and several multinational competitors have reorganized their APAC operations. Making a $15M investment decision on 2-year-old competitive data is reckless.

5. **Distribution strategy assumes direct-to-hospital model.** The revenue projections footnote states "Assumes direct-to-hospital sales model." Every major successful entrant in SE Asian medical devices uses local distributors — the hospital procurement process in Thailand, Vietnam, and Indonesia is relationship-driven, requires local language capability, and often involves government tender processes that require a locally registered entity. Medtronic, J&J, and B. Braun all entered via distributor partnerships before establishing direct operations. A direct model from Day 1 is a recipe for zero traction.

6. **No exit criteria defined.** The $15M investment plan has no go/no-go decision gates, no milestones with kill criteria, and no definition of what failure looks like. What if Thailand revenue is $500K in Year 1 instead of $2M? What if regulatory approval takes 18 months instead of 12? What if the first 6 months of sales meetings reveal zero hospital interest? There are no defined metrics that would trigger a strategic pivot, a pause, or an exit. The board is being asked to approve $15M with no off-ramp.
