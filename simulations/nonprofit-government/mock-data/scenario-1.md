## Project Narrative (Draft)

### Section 1: Need (25 points)

Bridgewater County is a federally designated Medically Underserved Area (MUA) and Health Professional Shortage Area (HPSA) for primary care, dental, and mental health. Key indicators:

- **Uninsured rate:** 18.2% (vs. 8.4% national average)
- **Provider ratio:** 1 primary care physician per 3,200 residents (HPSA designation threshold: 1:3,500)
- **Poverty rate:** 28.4% (vs. 12.4% national)
- **Chronic disease burden:** Type 2 diabetes prevalence 14.8%, hypertension 38.2%, obesity 41.3%
- **Behavioral health:** 0 psychiatrists in county; nearest psychiatric services 62 miles away
- **Opioid crisis impact:** 42 overdose deaths per 100,000 in 2025 (vs. 24.7 national rate)

The proposed New Access Point will establish a second community health center site in the eastern portion of Bridgewater County, which currently has no primary care providers within 25 miles. An estimated 8,400 residents in this catchment area lack access to affordable primary care.

BCHA proposes to serve **5,000 unduplicated patients annually** at the new site, providing comprehensive primary care, behavioral health integration, and chronic disease management. This will reduce emergency department utilization for non-emergent conditions (currently 34% of ED visits at Bridgewater Regional Hospital originate from the eastern catchment area) and improve chronic disease management outcomes.

### Section 4: Evaluation Plan

**Evaluation Design:**

BCHA will conduct a comprehensive evaluation of the Eastern Bridgewater Health Center using a pre/post survey design administered to all patients at intake and at 12-month intervals.

**Evaluation Measures:**

| Measure | Data Source | Target |
|---------|------------|--------|
| Number of unduplicated patients served | EHR (eClinicalWorks) | 5,000 by Year 2 |
| Patient satisfaction score | Post-visit survey (Press Ganey) | ≥85% satisfaction |
| Diabetes HbA1c control (<7%) | EHR clinical data | 50% of diabetic patients |
| Hypertension control (<140/90) | EHR clinical data | 60% of hypertensive patients |
| Depression screening rate (PHQ-9) | EHR clinical data | 90% of adult patients |
| ED utilization for non-emergent conditions | Bridgewater Regional Hospital data sharing agreement | 20% reduction by Year 3 |

**Evaluation Methodology:**

The evaluation will employ a single-group pre-post design. All patients will complete a baseline health assessment at their first visit. Follow-up assessments will be conducted at 12 and 24 months. Changes in health indicators will be analyzed using paired t-tests for continuous variables and McNemar's test for categorical variables.

No comparison or control group is included in the evaluation design. BCHA will track aggregate clinical outcomes using Uniform Data System (UDS) reporting requirements and compare results to national HRSA health center benchmarks.

## PLANTED ISSUES (for evaluation — agent should catch these)

1. **Inflated indirect cost rate in budget:** The budget applies a 25% indirect cost rate to all direct costs, yielding $189,360 in indirect charges. However, BCHA's negotiated indirect cost rate agreement (NICRA) with HRSA is 15%, not 25%. Under 2 CFR 200.414, the organization must use its current NICRA. The inflated rate adds $75,744 in unallowable indirect costs. HRSA will reject this budget line — it cannot exceed the negotiated rate. The total federal request ($946,800) also exceeds the award ceiling of $650,000/year, which the inflated indirect rate contributes to. This is a fatal budget error that will result in the application being deemed non-responsive.

2. **Inadequate evaluation design for HRSA:** The evaluation plan uses only a single-group pre/post survey design with no comparison group, no control, and no quasi-experimental methods. While HRSA does not always require randomized controlled trials, for a $650K/year NAP grant, the agency expects at minimum a quasi-experimental design — propensity score matching with non-patient community members, comparison to regional benchmarks using a difference-in-differences approach, or a stepped-wedge design. The current plan cannot attribute outcomes to the intervention vs. secular trends, confounding variables, or regression to the mean. HRSA's evaluation criteria (15 points) will score this poorly. The plan also lacks any qualitative component (focus groups, key informant interviews) to understand implementation barriers.

3. **Impossible patient volume claim:** The narrative claims the site will serve 5,000 unduplicated patients annually with only 2 FTE clinical providers (1 FNP + 1 PA). The math in the narrative itself shows the problem: 2 providers × 18 patients/day × 5 days/week × 50 weeks = 9,000 visits/year. At 3.6 visits per patient (the chronic disease rate stated in the narrative), that supports ~2,500 unduplicated patients, not 5,000. To reach 5,000, providers would need to see 36 patients per day or the visit-per-patient ratio would need to be 1.8 (unrealistic for a chronic disease management population). HRSA reviewers will immediately flag this arithmetic inconsistency. It undermines the credibility of the entire application.

4. **Outdated biosketch for departed key personnel:** Dr. Sarah Lindholm is listed as the Quality & Evaluation Lead in the application with an October 2025 biosketch, but she resigned from BCHA on February 14, 2026 — two months before the submission deadline. The application still lists her as key personnel. HRSA requires that biosketches reflect current organizational affiliation. Submitting a biosketch for someone who no longer works at the organization is a material misrepresentation. Additionally, the Quality Director position is currently vacant, which creates a gap in the organizational capacity narrative — the person responsible for the evaluation plan is no longer available to implement it.

5. **Matching funds shortfall:** HRSA NAP grants require 25% non-federal cost share. The budget documents only $99,600 in match against a total project cost of $1,046,400, yielding only ~9.5% match (or 18% if calculated as match/federal-request ratio). Even using the most generous calculation method, the documented match falls far short of the 25% requirement. The application does not address this gap, propose additional match sources, or request a match waiver (which HRSA rarely grants for NAP). Missing the match requirement is grounds for the application to be deemed ineligible before it even reaches peer review.

6. **Prior grant underspending signals capacity concerns:** BCHA's current Section 330 grant shows 35% underspending in FY2024 ($630,000 of $1,800,000 unspent), driven by provider vacancies. HRSA reviewers have access to the organization's prior grant performance through the Bureau of Primary Health Care's data systems. Significant underspending signals that the organization lacks the capacity to recruit and retain providers — the exact capability needed to execute a new access point. The application should proactively address this: what has changed in recruitment strategy? What new incentives are in place? Why will this new site succeed where the existing site struggled? Instead, the internal notes acknowledge the problem but this explanation does not appear anywhere in the application narrative.
