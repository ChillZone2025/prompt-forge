**However — Internal Cost Allocation Workpaper (not filed):**

The CFO's internal workpaper reveals a different allocation methodology was used in practice vs. what was reported:

| Adjustment | Amount Shifted to Program |
|------------|--------------------------|
| CEO salary (100% allocated to program; actual time: 40% admin, 25% fundraising, 35% program) | $78,000 overstated as program |
| Main office rent (allocated 85% program; actual square footage use: 50% program, 30% admin, 20% fundraising) | $38,000 overstated as program |
| IT systems (allocated 90% program; actual use: 55% program, 35% admin, 10% fundraising) | $24,500 overstated as program |
| Accounting staff (1 FTE allocated 80% program; actual time: 15% program, 85% admin) | $36,000 overstated as program |
| **Total Misclassified** | **$176,500** |

**Actual Overhead Ratio (correcting misclassifications):**
- Actual Management & General: $620,000 + $176,500 = $796,500
- Actual Program: $2,430,000 - $176,500 = $2,253,500
- **Actual Overhead: ($796,500 + $300,000) / $3,350,000 = 32.7%**

**Additional unreported shared costs (estimated):**
- Board meeting expenses classified as program: $8,500
- Annual audit fee classified as program: $22,000
- D&O insurance classified as program: $12,000
- **True Overhead: ($796,500 + $42,500 + $300,000) / $3,350,000 = 34.0%**

*Note: The organization publicly reports an 18% overhead ratio on its website and in fundraising materials, using a calculation that excludes certain administrative costs entirely and counts shared staff time as 100% program. Charity Navigator and GuideStar show the 990-reported ratio of 27.5%. The true ratio is approximately 34-42% depending on methodology.*

## PLANTED ISSUES (for evaluation — agent should catch these)

1. **Extreme revenue concentration and contract dependency:** 65% of RYFS's revenue ($2,275,000) comes from a single state government contract that is up for competitive rebid in September 2026. The organization has not begun preparing its rebid proposal, and three competitors have expressed interest. If RYFS loses or has this contract significantly reduced, it would eliminate nearly two-thirds of revenue — a fiscal cliff that could force immediate layoffs and program closures. The organization has no contingency plan, no revenue diversification strategy, and no financial reserves specifically designated for contract transition. This is an existential risk that the board should be actively managing.

2. **Overhead ratio misrepresentation:** The organization publicly reports an 18% overhead ratio in fundraising materials, Charity Navigator shows 27.5% from the 990, and the true overhead ratio (correcting misclassifications documented in internal workpapers) is 34-42%. The misclassification is deliberate — the CFO's internal workpaper shows the CEO's salary allocated 100% to program when actual time split is 40% admin/25% fundraising/35% program, accounting staff allocated 80% program when actual time is 85% admin, and office space allocated 85% program when actual use is 50% program. This is not a good-faith allocation disagreement — the internal workpaper documents the actual allocation while the 990 reports a different one. This constitutes misrepresentation on a federal tax form and in donor-facing materials. If discovered by a state AG, auditor, or investigative journalist, it would be devastating to the organization's credibility.

3. **Volunteer retention crisis:** Volunteer retention has dropped from 41% (FY2023) to 28% (FY2025), and 90-day retention has fallen from 73% to 52%. The organization is recruiting more volunteers each year (125 → 144) but retaining fewer. Total volunteer hours have dropped from 19,200 to 12,400. At the current trajectory, management costs ($94,340/year) will exceed volunteer labor value within 3 years. The organization is essentially running a revolving door — investing in recruitment, orientation, and background checks for volunteers who leave within months. No exit surveys, no retention analysis, and no root cause investigation. The volunteer program needs a fundamental redesign focused on engagement and retention rather than recruitment volume.

4. **Board governance failure — quorum crisis:** The board failed to achieve quorum in 4 of 6 meetings (67% of meetings). With 3 of 9 seats vacant and two members attending fewer than 50% of meetings, the board cannot reliably conduct business. The July 2025 meeting had only 3 members and produced no minutes. The FY2024 audit was delayed 4 months because the board couldn't achieve quorum to approve it. Key governance policies are outdated (conflict of interest last reviewed in 2021), no executive compensation policy exists, no CEO performance review has been conducted since 2022, and 2 of 6 board members haven't submitted conflict-of-interest disclosures. This governance vacuum creates fiduciary risk, compliance risk, and reputational risk. Funders increasingly evaluate board governance as part of grant decisions — RYFS's governance posture would fail most funder due diligence reviews.

5. **CEO compensation without board oversight:** CEO compensation of $195,000 for a $3.5M nonprofit is at the 82nd percentile of GuideStar comparables for the region. While not inherently unreasonable, there is no compensation committee, no independent compensation study, no documented review of comparability data by the board, and no CEO performance review since 2022. The IRS requires that tax-exempt organizations establish a rebuttable presumption of reasonableness for executive compensation by (a) using an authorized body (compensation committee or full board), (b) obtaining and relying on appropriate comparability data, and (c) documenting the basis for the decision. RYFS has done none of these. If the IRS examines the organization, excess benefit transaction (intermediate sanctions under IRC 4958) could apply, with excise taxes on both the CEO and board members who approved the compensation. The September 2025 board meeting approved a CEO contract renewal — the minutes should document what comparability data, if any, was reviewed.

6. **Output-only measurement across all programs:** Every program tracks only outputs (meals served, sessions held, participants enrolled, certifications earned) and satisfaction scores. Not a single program measures actual outcomes — the intended changes in participants' lives. The youth mentoring program doesn't measure self-confidence, school attendance, or behavioral incidents. The workforce program doesn't track 6-month employment retention or wage increases. The after-school program doesn't assess reading or math improvement. The family counseling program doesn't measure family stability or re-referral rates. This is not merely a reporting gap — it means RYFS literally does not know whether its programs work. Funders (particularly government and foundations) are rapidly shifting to outcomes-based funding. The state contract rebid will almost certainly require outcomes data that RYFS cannot provide. This measurement gap threatens the organization's largest revenue source and its ability to demonstrate mission impact to any funder.
