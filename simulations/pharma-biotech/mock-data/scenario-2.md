## FDA Pre-Approval Inspection Findings (January 2026)

### Form 483 Observations

**Observation 1:** The firm failed to ensure that laboratory records include complete data derived from all tests (21 CFR 211.194(a)). Specifically, the audit trail for the Empower 3 chromatography data system (CDS) shows gaps where the audit trail function was disabled during system maintenance on three occasions (July 15, 2025; September 22, 2025; November 8, 2025) totaling approximately 14 hours of unmonitored system access. During these periods, data files could have been modified, deleted, or created without traceability.

**Observation 2:** The firm's investigation of out-of-specification results does not extend to other batches that may have been associated with the specific failure or discrepancy (21 CFR 211.192). Specifically, OOS investigation OOS-2026-007 for Batch 2026-002 (assay) was resolved through retesting of the original sample without identifying an assignable cause for the initial failing result. The investigation did not evaluate whether adjacent batches tested on the same instrument during the same period were similarly affected.

**Observation 3:** Procedures for cleaning and maintenance of equipment are not adequately followed (21 CFR 211.67). The cleaning validation protocol for the formulation vessel requires verification of less than 10 ppm carryover for the previous product. Cleaning verification records for the changeover from Product B (a different monoclonal antibody) to PRX-4821 on January 5, 2026, show a result of 8.7 ppm — within specification but approaching the limit. No trending of cleaning verification results is performed.

### Firm's Response to 483 Observations (submitted February 15, 2026)

**Response to Observation 1 (Audit Trail Gaps):**
> "BioGenesis acknowledges the audit trail gaps during system maintenance. A CAPA (CAPA-2026-003) has been initiated to implement a procedure requiring a second QA-approved analyst to be present during any maintenance that requires disabling the audit trail. Target completion: April 15, 2026."

**Response to Observation 2 (OOS Investigation):**
> "BioGenesis acknowledges the observation. The OOS investigation procedure (SOP-QC-042) will be revised to include mandatory assessment of adjacent batches when root cause is not identified. Target completion: March 30, 2026."

**Response to Observation 3 (Cleaning Verification):**
> "BioGenesis acknowledges the observation. A trending program for cleaning verification results will be implemented. Target completion: March 15, 2026 (completed)."

## PLANTED ISSUES (for evaluation — agent should catch these)

1. **Grade A environmental monitoring excursion — batch released despite 0 CFU action limit:** The Grade A zone during Batch 2026-003 fill recovered 2 CFU on the mid-fill settle plate. Per EU GMP Annex 1 (revised 2023), Section 9.30 and Table 5, the action limit for Grade A zones is 0 CFU — any microbial recovery in Grade A is an action limit excursion requiring thorough investigation. The justification that organisms are "environmental and non-pathogenic" is irrelevant — the Grade A limit is 0 CFU regardless of organism identity because the concern is the breach of the aseptic environment, not the pathogenicity of the specific organism recovered. The presence of *any* organisms indicates a failure of the aseptic barrier. Releasing a sterile injectable batch with a Grade A excursion based solely on organism identification is a fundamental misunderstanding of the purpose of environmental monitoring in aseptic manufacturing. At minimum, additional sterility testing, bioburden assessment, and a thorough contamination control strategy review are required.

2. **OOS investigation resolved by retesting without assignable cause:** OOS-2026-007 (assay at 94.2% vs. spec 95.0-105.0%) was resolved by retesting the original sample, and the initial OOS was invalidated based on the passing retest results. Per FDA's "Guidance for Industry: Investigating Out-of-Specification (OOS) Test Results for Pharmaceutical Production" (2006), an initial OOS result can only be invalidated if an assignable laboratory cause is identified during Phase 1 investigation (analyst error, instrument malfunction, calculation error, sample preparation issue). The investigation explicitly states "no assignable cause identified." Retesting without assignable cause is considered data integrity manipulation — it is selecting results until a passing value is obtained. The FDA 483 Observation 2 specifically called this out. This practice, combined with the audit trail gaps (Observation 1), creates a pattern that suggests systemic data integrity concerns.

3. **Overdue data integrity CAPA (CAPA-2025-029):** CAPA-2025-029 for data integrity (Empower audit trail gaps) was due December 31, 2025 — it is now approximately 90 days overdue with no completion recorded. This is the same finding that the FDA cited in 483 Observation 1. An overdue CAPA on a data integrity finding is one of the most reliable predictors of a Warning Letter. FDA's assessment will be: (a) the firm knew about the audit trail gaps since at least November 2025 (when the CAPA was opened); (b) the firm set a deadline and missed it; (c) the same finding appeared during the PAI in January 2026, meaning the issue persisted through two inspection cycles. CAPA-2026-003 (the 483 response CAPA) appears to be a new CAPA for the same issue with a later deadline — this looks like the CDMO is "resetting the clock" rather than addressing the original finding. FDA will see through this.

4. **Stability data trending toward specification limit — shelf life not supported:** The stress stability data (40°C/75%RH) shows Degradation Product A increasing from 0.3% at T=0 to 0.8% at 6 months, approaching the 1.0% specification limit. Total degradation products reached 2.1% at 6 months, exceeding the 2.0% specification. While stress data is used to identify degradation pathways rather than set shelf life, the rate of degradation product formation should be used to model long-term behavior. The accelerated data (25°C/60%RH) shows Degradation Product A at 0.8% at 6 months. Extrapolating the real-time data trend (0.2% to 0.4% over 24 months at 5°C), a 36-month shelf life would predict approximately 0.5% Degradation Product A — likely within specification. However, the stability report does not include any statistical trend analysis (per ICH Q1E), does not model the degradation kinetics, and does not discuss the accelerated data trend. The absence of this analysis in a regulatory submission is a gap that an FDA reviewer will question. The claim that the data "support" a 36-month shelf life without statistical modeling is unsupported.

5. **Media fill failure without root cause or retrospective batch assessment:** Media fill MF-2025-003 failed in November 2025 (1 positive unit in 5,000, *S. epidermidis*). The investigation concluded "no definitive root cause identified" and attributed it to "most likely personnel-derived contamination." Per EU GMP Annex 1, Section 9.37, a failed media fill requires a thorough investigation, identification of root cause, and assessment of the impact on batches manufactured since the last successful media fill. Three commercial batches (2025-010, 2025-011, 2026-001) were manufactured between the failed media fill (November 2025) and the passing requalification fill (January 2026). No retrospective assessment of these batches was performed. Without identifying root cause and without assessing impacted batches, there is no assurance that the aseptic process was under control during the manufacture of these three batches. This is a patient safety gap — sterile injectables manufactured during a period of unqualified aseptic processing should, at minimum, undergo additional sterility testing or risk assessment.
