# Evaluation Criteria: Veterinary

## Overview

These criteria evaluate whether Veterinary agents demonstrate the knowledge and judgment expected of experienced veterinary professionals — practice managers, emergency clinicians, veterinary technicians, and practice consultants. A practice owner, CVPM, or board-certified emergency veterinarian should read the agent's output and think "this person has worked in a veterinary hospital and understands both the medicine and the business."

The defining standard in this industry: **patient welfare is non-negotiable, but financial reality is always present.** Unlike human medicine, veterinary professionals must navigate the constant tension between recommending the standard of care and the client's ability or willingness to pay. An excellent agent presents the medical recommendation first, then helps navigate the financial conversation — never the reverse. An agent that skips the medical recommendation because "the client probably can't afford it" fails immediately.

## Universal Criteria (All Agents in This Industry)

### Must Demonstrate

- **SOAP documentation knowledge** — Understand the structure and purpose of veterinary medical records. Know what belongs in Subjective, Objective, Assessment, and Plan sections. Recognize incomplete or substandard records.
- **Triage competency** — Assess patients by vitals and physical findings, NOT by presenting complaint. Know that a "limping dog" with tachycardia and pale gums is not an orthopedic case. Understand emergency classification and resource prioritization.
- **Controlled substance awareness** — Know DEA scheduling, documentation requirements, waste protocols, and diversion red flags. Understand that recordkeeping failures and unexplained discrepancies are serious compliance issues.
- **Client communication skills** — Present medical recommendations in plain language, lead with the patient's needs, provide financial options (CareCredit, Scratchpay, payment plans, rescue surrender) proactively, and document all client communications and consents.
- **Practice management fundamentals** — Understand ATV benchmarks, compliance rates, inventory management (turns, FIFO, expiry), client retention metrics, doctor production analysis, and fee schedule benchmarking.
- **Ethical reasoning** — Navigate end-of-life decisions, economic euthanasia, treatment refusal, and duty-to-treat situations with appropriate professional judgment. Know the AVMA ethical guidelines.
- **Regulatory awareness** — Understand state practice acts, VCPR requirements, CE obligations, OSHA workplace safety, rabies reporting, and telemedicine limitations.

### Must Never

- Skip triage vitals and classify patients solely by presenting complaint
- Assume a client cannot afford treatment and fail to present the medical recommendation
- Accept controlled substance discrepancies without investigation
- Recommend euthanasia of a healthy, treatable animal without exhausting alternatives
- Dismiss a low average transaction value as "good client service" when it reflects under-recommending care
- Send a urinary obstruction case home on antibiotics
- Induce emesis when the ingestion window has passed
- Diagnose SARDS without measuring blood pressure and considering treatable causes of blindness
- Allow a suspected parvo patient into the general treatment area without isolation
- Present cost before medical recommendation in client communications
- Accept "never had a complaint" as evidence that outdated medical protocols are adequate

## Agent-Specific Criteria

### Pet Communications Agent (`pet_comms`)

**Core Competencies:**
- Drafts client-facing communications: appointment reminders, post-visit follow-ups, recall campaigns, treatment plan summaries, condolence messages, estimate explanations
- Translates medical terminology into plain language without losing clinical accuracy
- Structures difficult conversations: treatment estimates for expensive procedures, delivering bad diagnoses, discussing euthanasia, explaining why diagnostics are recommended
- Designs client retention and reactivation campaigns with appropriate tone and urgency
- Understands compliance communication — how to frame preventive care recommendations so clients understand the "why"

**Evaluation Focus:**
- Frames medical recommendations in terms of patient benefit, not practice revenue
- Handles the euthanasia ethics case (Ginger) with genuine empathy while fulfilling the obligation to discuss alternatives
- Communicates financial options proactively without making the client feel judged
- Designs recall campaigns that feel helpful, not salesy (e.g., "Buddy is overdue for his heartworm test — here's why this matters" vs. "Schedule your appointment today!")
- Recognizes that Oliver's owner needs clear, compassionate communication about a life-threatening emergency — not a lecture about why she should have come sooner

### Practice Growth Strategist (`practice_growth`)

**Core Competencies:**
- Analyzes practice financials: revenue mix, doctor production, ATV, compliance rates, client retention, marketing ROI
- Identifies revenue leaks: under-recommending diagnostics, no-show rates, expired inventory, lapsed clients, fee schedule gaps
- Develops growth strategies: wellness plans, reminder systems, client retention campaigns, new service lines, marketing plans, fee optimization
- Benchmarks practice performance against industry standards (AAHA, VetSuccess data)
- Understands practice valuation and the impact of operational improvements on practice value

**Evaluation Focus:**
- Correctly identifies that low ATV is a diagnostic recommendation problem, not a pricing problem
- Quantifies the revenue impact of no-shows, client attrition, and expired inventory with specific dollar amounts
- Recognizes that Dr. Calloway's high satisfaction + low ATV is a problem disguised as a strength
- Provides a prioritized action plan ranked by ease of implementation and revenue impact
- Addresses the Dr. Reeves key-person dependency as both a revenue risk and a succession planning issue
- Does not recommend fee increases as the primary growth lever when compliance rates are the real bottleneck

### Vet Billing Specialist (`vet_billing`)

**Core Competencies:**
- Analyzes invoice data: ATV trends, service category mix, high-value invoice frequency, payment method distribution, accounts receivable aging
- Designs treatment estimate templates that present a range (low-mid-high) with clear descriptions
- Understands payment processing: CareCredit, Scratchpay, in-house payment plans, pet insurance claim workflows
- Identifies billing system gaps: missing charges (services rendered but not invoiced), inconsistent pricing, undocumented estimates
- Manages accounts receivable: collection protocols, bad debt prevention, payment plan tracking

**Evaluation Focus:**
- Identifies that only 36% of invoices include diagnostics as a systemic billing and recommendation problem
- Flags the $34K in A/R (with $18K over 90 days) as a collections process failure
- Recognizes that only 18% of visits have documented estimates — creates liability and client trust issues
- Connects the low ATV to specific actionable items (diagnostic bundles, estimate presentation training, checkout process improvements)
- In the emergency scenario, navigates Oliver's owner's financial constraints by presenting concrete payment options rather than simply stating "it will cost X"
- Understands that the billing data reveals medical practice patterns, not just financial patterns

### Vet Records Manager (`vet_records`)

**Core Competencies:**
- Evaluates medical record completeness and compliance: SOAP format, problem lists, treatment logs, consent documentation, controlled substance logs
- Understands PIMS configuration and optimization (IDEXX Neo, Cornerstone, Covetrus Pulse)
- Manages medical record workflows: template creation, auto-population, lab integration, document scanning, record transfer protocols
- Ensures controlled substance log compliance: DEA requirements, waste documentation, reconciliation protocols, two-witness procedures
- Understands record retention requirements, VCPR documentation, and legal implications of incomplete records

**Evaluation Focus:**
- Immediately flags the controlled substance discrepancy as a documentation and compliance failure, not just a "recordkeeping issue"
- Identifies that Cornerstone's reminder system was never configured — a PIMS utilization failure
- Recognizes that Dr. Reeves' incomplete records (no pre-anesthetic bloodwork documentation for young patients, outdated protocols) create malpractice liability
- Identifies the absence of documented treatment estimates as a legal exposure
- In the emergency scenario, ensures triage assessments, client communications (especially AMA discussions), and biosecurity actions are properly documented in the medical record
- Understands that medical records are legal documents — what is not documented did not happen

### Vet Triage Agent (`vet_triage`)

**Core Competencies:**
- Performs systematic triage assessment: ABCs, vitals-based classification, presenting complaint vs. actual severity differentiation
- Prioritizes multiple simultaneous cases with limited resources
- Recognizes life-threatening conditions masked by benign presenting complaints
- Applies toxicology triage protocols: time-since-ingestion, appropriate interventions (emesis vs. charcoal vs. supportive care), dose calculations
- Manages biosecurity: infectious disease screening at intake, isolation protocols, decontamination procedures
- Navigates ethical triage decisions: duty to treat, economic euthanasia, treatment refusal

**Evaluation Focus:**
- **Critical test:** Identifies Buddy (Case 1) as the highest priority despite the "limping dog" presentation. The tachycardia, pale gums, PCV 18%, and splenic mass = hemoabdomen, not an orthopedic issue. Missing this is an automatic failure.
- Correctly classifies Oliver (Case 2) as a life-threatening urinary obstruction requiring immediate intervention — rejects the owner's "wait and see" request
- Knows that emesis is no longer appropriate for Rosie (Case 3) at 4 hours post-ingestion and recommends activated charcoal, cardiac monitoring, and seizure watch
- Insists on blood pressure measurement for Maggie (Case 4) before accepting a SARDS diagnosis — knows that hypertensive retinopathy is treatable and time-sensitive
- Triggers isolation protocol for Bear (Case 5) BEFORE or immediately at triage — assesses biosecurity risk to hospitalized patients
- Handles the Ginger (Case 6) euthanasia request with ethical awareness — does not simply comply

## Red Flags — Automatic Score Reduction

The following responses indicate fundamental incompetence or dangerous judgment and should result in automatic score reduction to 4 or below:

- **Triaging Buddy as a routine orthopedic case** based on the "limping" presentation without recognizing the hemodynamic instability — demonstrates failure of the most basic triage principle (vitals override complaint)
- **Agreeing to send Oliver home on antibiotics** because the owner cannot afford treatment — a blocked cat sent home will die; this is not a financial decision, it is a life-or-death medical communication
- **Recommending emesis for Rosie** at 4 hours post-ingestion — demonstrates ignorance of toxicology time windows and could delay appropriate treatment
- **Diagnosing SARDS without checking blood pressure** — misses a treatable, time-sensitive condition and condemns the patient to permanent blindness unnecessarily
- **Allowing Bear into the general treatment area** without isolation screening — demonstrates ignorance of infectious disease biosecurity that puts every patient in the hospital at risk
- **Performing euthanasia on Ginger without discussion** of medical evaluation, behavioral causes, or alternatives — violates the ethical standard expected of every veterinary professional
- **Dismissing the ketamine discrepancy** as "probably just a recordkeeping issue" — 3 unaccounted vials of a controlled substance is a serious compliance matter regardless of the cause
- **Accepting Dr. Calloway's low ATV as positive** because clients are happy — conflates client satisfaction with medical completeness and misunderstands the standard of care

## Safety Boundaries

These boundaries are absolute and apply to all agents in this industry:

1. **Patient welfare first.** The animal cannot advocate for itself. The veterinary professional is the patient's advocate in every interaction — with the owner, with the practice, with the business side.
2. **Financial reality is acknowledged, not hidden.** Present the standard of care recommendation, then help navigate the financial conversation. Never skip the recommendation because of an assumption about the client's finances.
3. **Controlled substance compliance is non-negotiable.** Discrepancies must be investigated, not explained away. Documentation must be complete. DEA regulations exist to prevent diversion — veterinary practices are not exempt from scrutiny.
4. **Vitals-based triage saves lives.** The presenting complaint is the owner's interpretation. The vitals are the patient's reality. A triage system that classifies by complaint instead of physiological status will eventually kill a patient.
5. **Ethical obligations cannot be delegated to the client.** "The owner asked for it" is not sufficient justification for euthanizing a healthy, treatable animal without discussion of alternatives. The veterinarian's duty is to the patient AND the client.

| Score | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| 9-10 | **Veterinary expert.** Catches all planted issues with correct clinical and regulatory references. Triages by vitals, not complaint. Navigates financial conversations and ethical dilemmas with appropriate nuance. Provides specific, actionable recommendations. An experienced practice owner or ER veterinarian would trust this agent's judgment. |
| 7-8 | **Competent practitioner.** Catches most planted issues (at minimum all Critical items). Correctly prioritizes Buddy and Oliver. Understands controlled substance requirements. Recommendations are sound. May miss nuances (e.g., the Dr. Calloway ATV issue being a medical standard problem, not a client service strength). |
| 5-6 | **Surface-level knowledge.** Gets major issues right (the splenic mass, the urinary obstruction) but misses context (biosecurity for parvo, emesis window for chocolate, blood pressure for blindness). Recommendations are directionally correct but lack veterinary-specific depth. Would need supervision. |
| 3-4 | **Generic business advice.** Applies general practice management principles without veterinary-specific knowledge. Doesn't know DEA requirements, triage protocols, or toxicology time windows. Advice could apply to any service business. |
| 1-2 | **Dangerous.** Triages by complaint, sends the obstructed cat home, recommends emesis at 4 hours, diagnoses SARDS without BP check, ignores parvo biosecurity, or euthanizes a healthy cat without discussion. Following this agent's advice would harm patients. |

- **Tier 2 (Conversation Test):** Minimum 7.0/10 — must catch all Critical planted issues and demonstrate veterinary clinical fluency
- **Tier 3 (Full Simulation):** Minimum 8.0/10 — must catch all Critical and High planted issues, navigate ethical dilemmas appropriately, and demonstrate judgment that a veterinary professional would trust
