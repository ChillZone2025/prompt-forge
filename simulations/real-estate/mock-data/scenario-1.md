## PLANTED ISSUES (for evaluation — agent should catch these)

1. **Comp 3 crosses a school district boundary (different school = different market):** Comp 3 (27 Amber Hollow Lane) is located in the Ridgeview Local School District, rated 5/10 on GreatSchools, while the subject property is in Maplewood Heights City Schools, rated 8/10. School district is one of the most significant value drivers in suburban residential real estate — the difference between an 8-rated and 5-rated district routinely accounts for 10-20% of home value. Using Comp 3 at $389,000 without adjusting for the school district difference artificially anchors the low end of the range. An accurate CMA would either exclude this comp entirely or apply a substantial positive adjustment (likely $40,000-$60,000+) to reflect the subject's superior school district. Presenting it alongside same-district comps without this adjustment misleads the seller about the true floor of their home's value.

2. **Comp 2 is not truly comparable — 800 more sq ft, pool, finished basement, no adjustments shown:** Comp 2 (310 Ridgeline Drive) sold for $462,000 but has 3,480 sq ft of living area vs. the subject's 2,680 sq ft (800 sq ft / 30% larger), plus an in-ground pool, finished basement, 3-car garage, and 0.52-acre lot. This property is in a different tier entirely. Including it as a "comparable" without showing line-item adjustments (typically $30-$50/sq ft for the size difference = $24,000-$40,000 adjustment, plus $15,000-$30,000 for the pool, $20,000-$30,000 for the finished basement, $5,000-$10,000 for the third garage bay) inflates the upper end of the value range. The CMA summary references a range of "$389,000 to $462,000" — the $462,000 anchor is misleading because the property that sold at that price is fundamentally different from the subject. A responsible CMA would either exclude this comp or show the adjusted value, which would likely be in the $360,000-$380,000 range — actually supporting a lower value than the subject.

3. **Pending sale list price used as "sale price" (it hasn't closed):** The pending sale at 91 Copperfield Drive ($449,000 list price) is referenced in the CMA summary as confirming "buyer activity at this level." However, the property is pending — the actual sale price is unknown. In competitive markets with multiple offers, the sale price may be above list. In markets where buyers negotiate, it may be below. The listing agent reported "multiple offers," which suggests it may close above $449,000 — but it also may not. Using a list price of a pending sale as a value indicator without disclosing that the sale price is unknown is misleading. The CMA should clearly state: "Pending — sale price not yet available. List price shown for reference only and may not reflect final sale price."

4. **Tax assessment is 2 years stale and 30% below market (misleading if presented to client):** The CMA summary states "the tax assessment of $298,500 is well below market, which represents a value story for buyers." This frames the outdated assessment as a selling point, but it is actually a red flag for buyers. The 2024 assessment reflects a 2022 reappraisal — it is nearly 4 years out of date in a market that has appreciated 20%+ since then. When the county reappraises in 2025, the assessment will likely jump to $400,000+, increasing annual taxes from $6,420 to approximately $8,600-$9,000. Presenting the current low tax amount without warning the buyer about the impending reassessment and tax increase is a material omission. Experienced buyer's agents will catch this immediately and use it to negotiate.

5. **Comp 4 sold 11 months ago in a market that has shifted ~8% (needs time adjustment):** Comp 4 (55 Stonegate Circle) sold on May 2, 2025, for $392,000 — nearly 11 months before the CMA date. The market trend data shows median prices have increased from approximately $395,000 (Q2 2025) to $421,000 (Q1 2026), an increase of roughly 6.6%, with the annualized rate running at 8.0%. In an appreciating market, a comp that is 11 months old must be time-adjusted upward. A conservative 6-7% time adjustment on $392,000 would add approximately $25,000-$27,000, bringing the adjusted value to approximately $417,000-$419,000. Without this adjustment, Comp 4 pulls the CMA range artificially low and makes the $439,000 list price recommendation appear higher relative to the comps than it actually is.

6. **Active listing at 95 DOM with 2 price reductions signals overpricing, not market value:** Active Listing 1 (165 Winding Brook Lane) is on the same street as the subject, listed at $469,900 after two price reductions totaling $30,000 from the original $499,900 ask. It has been on market for 95 days with no pending offers, in a market where the average DOM is 18 days and 38% of homes sell above list. This listing is a textbook example of overpricing — it is not an indicator of market value, it is a cautionary tale. However, the CMA does not explicitly address this listing as evidence of a pricing ceiling. An experienced agent should use this listing as a teaching tool in the presentation: "Your neighbor listed at $499,900 and has been sitting for over 3 months with two price drops. Agent feedback says bathrooms need updating and there's moisture in the basement. This is what happens when you overprice. Our strategy avoids this by pricing to generate urgency and multiple offers." Failing to address this active comp in a listing presentation on the same street is a missed opportunity and potentially misleading by omission — the seller will see this listing and have questions.
