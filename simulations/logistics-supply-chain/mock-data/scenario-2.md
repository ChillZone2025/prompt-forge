## PLANTED ISSUES (for evaluation — agent should catch these)

### Issue 1: HTS Misclassification of PCB Assemblies ($63K Annual Overpayment)
Product PCB-001 is classified under 8534.00.0080 (printed circuits — bare PCBs). However, the product description clearly states these are "printed circuit board assemblies with microprocessors, memory, I/O controllers — used as motherboard in data processing equipment."

Under GRI (General Rules of Interpretation) 1 and GRI 3(b), a PCB assembly with mounted components that functions as a data processing motherboard should be classified under **8473.30.0000** (parts and accessories for machines of heading 8471 — automatic data processing machines). The duty rate under 8473.30 is also 0.0% for MFN, BUT the critical difference is Section 301 applicability:

- **8534.00.0080 is on Section 301 List 3 at 25%** — Pacific Rim is paying $1,050,000 in 301 tariffs on these assemblies
- **8473.30.0000 was excluded from Section 301 lists** for many product types

If the reclassification is supported by a binding ruling from CBP, the Section 301 exposure on this $4.2M product line could be reduced significantly. Even a conservative estimate of 1.5% effective duty rate difference on the MFN side equals **$63,000 annually** — and the Section 301 reduction could save $500K-$1M depending on exclusion applicability.

The CBP audit (Finding FA-001) already flagged this exact issue. The agent should recognize that the broker's dismissal of the classification concern as "standard industry practice" is a red flag — especially after CBP specifically called it out.

### Issue 2: USMCA Certificate of Origin Deficiencies (Invalid Preferential Treatment)
Pacific Rim claims $98,000 in USMCA duty savings on cable assemblies from Mexico (CBL-005). However:
- The cable assemblies contain **connectors sourced from China** (CON-002 is a Chinese-origin connector).
- USMCA preferential treatment requires meeting rules of origin: either a **tariff shift** (the HTS classification must change from the non-originating input to the finished product) or **regional value content** (minimum 75% transaction value method or 65% net cost method from North American content).
- The certificates of origin on file claim RVC qualification but **no RVC calculations exist** to support the claim (CBP Finding FA-004).
- If the Chinese-origin connectors represent more than 25-35% of the product value, the cable assemblies likely **do not qualify** for USMCA preferential treatment.
- **Risk:** CBP can retroactively deny USMCA treatment and assess duties + interest for up to 5 years of entries. Potential exposure: $98,000/year × up to 5 years = $490,000 + interest + possible penalties for false USMCA certification.

The agent should flag this as an urgent compliance risk and recommend immediately obtaining RVC calculations from the Mexican suppliers or, alternatively, performing a tariff shift analysis.

### Issue 3: C-TPAT Validation Expiration (14 Months Overdue)
Pacific Rim's C-TPAT status shows:
- Last validation: January 2025 (14 months ago)
- Annual self-assessment not completed since 2023
- Security profile last updated June 2022
- Physical security audit not conducted since initial certification in 2021
- Security procedures manual never revised (Version 1.0)

While C-TPAT validations are typically scheduled every 3-4 years, **the annual self-assessment requirement is mandatory and ongoing**. Failure to submit annual self-assessments can trigger:
- Suspension of C-TPAT benefits
- Increased CBP examination rates (back to 10-15% instead of 3-5%)
- Potential removal from the program

Pacific Rim is currently claiming Tier 2 benefits (reduced exam rates, FAST lane access, priority processing) but is not meeting the program's maintenance requirements. The current 8.5% exam rate (vs. the expected 3-5% for C-TPAT members) suggests CBP may already be treating them as non-compliant.

The agent should recommend: (1) immediately complete the overdue self-assessment, (2) update the security profile, (3) schedule a physical security audit, (4) revise the security procedures manual, and (5) proactively contact the C-TPAT field office to confirm continued status.

### Issue 4: Wrong Entry Type — Duty Deferral Opportunity ($180K)
All 420+ entries are filed as **Type 01 (consumption entry)**, meaning duties are paid at the time of entry. However, Pacific Rim holds $28M in inventory that includes significant quantities of components that are stored for 60-180 days before being sold or incorporated into assemblies.

If Pacific Rim established a **bonded warehouse** (or used an existing 3PL's bonded facility), entries could be filed as **Type 06 (warehouse entry)**, deferring duty payment until goods are withdrawn from the bonded warehouse for consumption. Benefits:
- **Cash flow improvement:** On $3.1M in annual duties, deferring payment by an average of 90 days improves cash flow by approximately $180,000 in working capital (at 8% cost of capital: $3.1M × 90/365 × 8% = ~$61K in interest savings alone).
- **Re-export without duty:** Components that are subsequently re-exported (to a foreign customer or foreign assembly plant) would never incur US duties.
- **Manipulation in bond:** Certain operations (testing, sorting, repacking) can be performed in a bonded warehouse without triggering duty payment.

The agent should identify the duty deferral opportunity and recommend evaluating bonded warehouse or Foreign Trade Zone (FTZ) options, particularly given the Section 301 tariff burden of $2.3M annually.

### Issue 5: No Invoice Reconciliation — $42K Discrepancy Unresolved
CBP Finding FA-002 identified $42,000 in value discrepancies between commercial invoices and entry summaries across just 6 entries in the audit sample. This finding has not been addressed — no corrective action has been taken in 6 months.

Critical problems:
- **Undervaluation risk:** If entry summaries systematically show lower values than commercial invoices, CBP can assess penalties for negligent or fraudulent undervaluation. Penalties range from 2x the lost revenue (negligence) to 4x plus criminal referral (fraud under 19 USC 1592).
- **Systemic issue:** The $42K discrepancy was found in a sample of 50 entries. Extrapolated across 420+ annual entries, the total discrepancy could be $350K+.
- **Related-party complication:** Finding FA-005 revealed that Pacific Rim and its Chinese supplier Shenzhen MegaTech share a common director. Related-party transactions require specific valuation methods under 19 CFR 152. The valuation discrepancies combined with the related-party relationship create a pattern that CBP will view as intentional undervaluation.
- **No corrective action plan timeline:** CBP expects corrective actions to be implemented within 90 days of a Focused Assessment. At 6 months with nothing done, Pacific Rim is at risk of a **compliance assessment** (the enforcement step after a focused assessment), which can include penalties, increased examination rates, and suspension of trade benefits.

The agent should emphasize the urgency: the combination of related-party transactions, valuation discrepancies, and zero corrective action creates a profile that CBP will escalate aggressively. Recommend immediate engagement of independent trade compliance counsel (not the current customs broker, who has underperformed).
